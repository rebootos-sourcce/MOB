## TALLY: what it must feel like

It must feel like being received, not processed. The room is already quiet. Nothing asks for anything before it has shown something. The film moves on its own and never talks over a decision. One figure stands still in the middle, because still means unread. When the person writes one true sentence and gets it back with a place attached, the figure takes its first breath. That is the only moment the film may celebrate, and it does so by moving once, not by saying so. Colour keeps one grammar: ink is not yet read, hue is read, a ring means one thing everywhere. The words are short and physical and never reassure, because a mirror that comforts before it reads is lying. Any exit is one tap away, and a person in trouble meets the plainest frame in the product, with everything stopped. The quiet parts are part of the work: the rest on slide three and the settle are where the loud moments land. The person leaves knowing three things without being told: it read me, it can say where, and I can come back.
