# Research, the funnel and the ICPs

Simulated focus group, 18 September. Run against the funnel in `TASKS.md`
section B and the ruling in B0. It answers the four questions B0 leaves open to
the ICPs, plus the delivery format and the funnel shape, and it proposes the
narrative.

**Every quotation in this file is simulated.** Nothing here was said by a real
person. Nothing here is quoted from the codex. The personas are the ones
already in the repo and the numbers attached to them are the ones the engine
already computes.

## 2. What they need to hear to take 63 questions. And the breaking point.

63 is 21 laws asked three times. Once where it costs you something, once where
nobody would know, once on an ordinary day. The spread between the three is
the measurement. `iqScore` will not name a lean when the spread is under 3
because that is inside self report noise.

**This is the argument, and it is not a marketing argument.** Cutting to 21
does not shorten the quiz, it deletes the instrument. One answer per law gives
a mean with no spread, and the engine refuses to read a lean it cannot see. A
21 question version would have to either lie or say nothing.

**Diane. HOLD.** "Tell me fifteen minutes before I start, show me what is left
while I am in it, and let me stop and come back. I will not start a thing whose
end I cannot see. And do not ask me the same question three times without
telling me you are doing it on purpose."

**Derek. BUY.** "63 is nothing. I do intervals longer than that. Length is not
the problem, a pointless rep is the problem. Tell me the three asks are three
different loads and I will do all 63 without complaint."

**Marcus. RESIST.** "Around question 40 I will notice you are cycling the same
twenty one things and I will feel handled. Unless you said so at the top. If
you said so at the top, I will admire it. That is the whole difference and it
costs you one sentence."

**Angela. CONFUSE.** "Why does it matter whether anyone would know. I answer
honestly either way." She is the ICP whose Truth law reads 2.6 against a
baseline of 6.9, so this is the exact question the exercise exists to catch,
and she cannot see why it is being asked. She does not resist the length, she
resists the design, and she does not know that is what she is doing.

**Sofia. HOLD.** "I will take fifteen minutes. I want to know the answers stay
mine. And I want to be able to stop halfway and not lose it, because I get
interrupted."

**James. RESIST.** "Fifteen minutes is a long time to spend proving something
to software. I would give it four. Make the first four minutes worth the next
eleven and I will stay. Front load the finding."

**Ana. HOLD.** "I will answer all of them. Some of them are going to be hard
to answer and I need to know nobody is reading them but me."

**The breaking point, measured against the panel.**

| Length | Would start | Would finish |
|---|---|---|
| 21 questions | high | high, and the reading is unusable |
| 63, no time stated, no progress | moderate | 31 percent |
| 63, fifteen minutes stated, progress shown, resumable | moderate | 60 percent |
| 63, plus the three way design explained in one line | moderate | 68 percent |
| 90 or more | low | not tested, nobody asked for it |

The breaking point is not a question count. It is 63 without a stated
duration and a visible remainder. Under that condition the panel loses half its
finishers. With fifteen minutes stated up front, progress visible and the
design named in one line, 63 reads as rigour to everybody except Angela, and
Angela's confusion is itself the finding.

**Do not shorten it. Frame it.** One line at the top: every law is asked three
ways, because what you do when it costs you and what you do when nobody is
watching are different numbers, and the gap is the reading.

**The supporting arithmetic already exists** in `FEEDBACK-alexander.md` and the
engine computes it. Identification runs 20.8 percent with no laws answered and
48.8 percent with all 21. Skipping costs 28 points of identification and widens
the interval from plus or minus 12.1 to plus or minus 19.3. Put that on the
screen. The number is the argument for finishing, and Alexander asked for the
same thing from the trust side.

## 3. What they expect to get out of it

**Diane. HOLD.** "A number and a cost. If it says I am at 41 I want the next
line to say what the 41 is costing me this quarter. Not what it means about me."

**Derek. BUY.** "A limiter. Name the one thing capping my output and where it
sits. I will train it like anything else."

**Marcus. RESIST.** "I expect to get something I can argue with. If it is
unfalsifiable I have learned nothing. Give me a claim specific enough to be
wrong."

**Angela. BUY.** "I expect it to see me. Something that names the thing I have
been circling for years without a word for it."

**Sofia. HOLD.** "A map I could put in front of a client. And for myself, the
thing I already know said back to me by something with no stake in being kind
to me."

**James. HOLD.** "A ranking against people like me. I will say that plainly. If
there is no comparison the number is decoration."

**Rosa. CONFUSE.** "I do not know what I would want from it. That is probably
the answer."

**Finding, and it splits the panel.** Diane, Derek and Marcus want a cost.
Angela and Sofia want recognition. James wants a rank. These are three
different result pages and averaging them produces a page that serves none of
them. The ruling in `DECISIONS.md` on the result page already sequences the
score, then CQ, DQ and SQ, then how it lands across four registers, then the
claim about healing and performance. That covers Angela and Derek. It does not
carry a cost line and it does not carry a comparison.

**Recommendation.** Add one cost line directly under the score, before the
explanation. Do not add a comparison. A rank against other people turns a
diagnostic into a leaderboard and James is the only one asking for it. He is
100 of 1,000 and he is the panel member most likely to gamify his own answers,
which corrupts his own reading.

## 4. "How is this app going to help me?" In their words.

**Diane. HOLD.** "Does it give me back an hour or does it give me another
practice to fail at. I have a shelf of those. What does it actually do at nine
on a Tuesday."

**Derek. BUY.** "Does it move a number I already track. If my resting rate does
not move and my last kilometre does not move, this is a mood."

**Marcus. RESIST.** "Help is the wrong verb and the fact that you used it tells
me who you think I am. Does it show me the mechanism. I do not need help, I
need the schematic."

**Angela. BUY.** "Will it finally tell me why the same thing keeps happening.
Six modalities and every one of them told me a story about it. None of them
told me where it lives."

**Sofia. HOLD.** "How does it help me when the room is full of other people's
charge and I have nowhere to put mine. Do not tell me to hold boundaries. Tell
me what to run and how long it takes."

**James. RESIST.** "Help implies a deficit. Ask me instead what it improves. If
the answer is that I sleep better, I already sleep fine. People find that cold.

**Ana. BUY.** "Will it tell me this has an end. That is the only thing I am
asking."

**Rosa. CONFUSE.** "Help with what."

**Finding.** Four of eight reject the word help. The product must never use it.
The verb is read, or locate, or clear, or cost. Marcus and James both bristle at
the same syllable and they are 260 of 1,000 between them.

**Finding.** The answer that lands across the widest spread of the panel is
mechanical and short: it names where the charge is held, tells you what holding
it costs, and gives you the sentences that release it, one address at a time.
Diane, Derek, Marcus and Sofia all accept that formulation. Angela accepts it
and wants more warmth than the house voice will give her, which is correct and
should not be fixed.

## 5. "This seems too good to be true." Where disbelief lands.

Five named claims. Each one is the exact sentence that breaks a specific person.

**Claim one. 112 addresses, each seated at a named plexus or nerve.**

Marcus. RESIST. "Anxiety at the adrenal medulla. Self silencing at the vagus
nerve. That is precision as costume. Real precision cites a measurement. If you
tell me a nerve you are telling me you measured a nerve, and you did not, you
asked me 63 questions about honesty. The specificity is doing rhetorical work
and I can see it doing it."

This is the sharpest break in the study and it comes from the ICP whose
Discernment reads 8.8. The anatomy is the most distinctive thing the instrument
has and it is also the least defensible as stated. It must be framed as a map
of seats, the coordinate system the instrument uses, and not as a measured
finding about a person's nerve.

**Claim two. 63 questions locate where charge sits in your body.**

James. REFUSE, step 6. "I answered a questionnaire. Then you showed me a body.
Those are not the same object and you moved between them without telling me.
I make calls for a living. That is a bad one."

The engine agrees with him. With 21 of 21 laws answered and no story told,
`accuracy` returns about 48.8 percent identification at plus or minus 12.1. The
quiz does not read the body. It reads 21 laws, three ways, and places the
result in a body shaped coordinate system. The held addresses that raise
identification come from stories, and at quiz completion there are none.

**This is the trust break of the whole funnel.** Fixing it costs one number.
Put identification and its interval on the result page, next to the score, at
the moment of the reveal. The panel does not punish a stated limit. It punishes
a limit it discovers later. Marcus, James and Diane all move from RESIST to
HOLD when the number is shown.

**Claim three. The tier words. Incoherent, Corrupt, Severe, Collapsed.**

Angela. REFUSE, step 6. "It told me I am incoherent. I closed it. I am not
coming back and I am telling the group chat." CQ 31 to 49 is Incoherent, 21 to
30 is Corrupt, 1 to 20 is Severe. Ana, who is the panel member actually in
crisis and carries Fear, Shame and Sad at 9, would land in the bottom two bands
and be handed the word Corrupt.

This is the most dangerous copy in the product. Not because it is wrong. The
arithmetic is the arithmetic. Because a moral adjective delivered by software
to a person in crisis is a verdict, and a verdict ends the relationship at the
exact moment the product is supposed to begin. The low bands need mechanical
words, not moral ones. High resistance, high load, high drag. Those describe
the same reading and none of them accuse.

**Claim four. The pattern arithmetic, and it is the one nobody has said aloud.**

Derek. RESIST, and he is the one who does the division. "Two thousand patterns
a decade, so at 39 I hold about 7,800. Free tier is 10 a week, 520 a year, and
I accrue 200 a year just by living. Net 320. That is twenty four years. Tier
one is 400 a month, so call it twenty months of paying every month to clear
what I walked in with. I am not saying it is wrong. I am saying you have not
looked at it."

Every number in that paragraph comes from `DECISIONS.md`. The horizon estimate,
age times two hundred. The tier ladder. The two markers at 2,500 and 3,500.
Derek is the endurance athlete and he is the one who will run the arithmetic,
and the arithmetic reads as a subscription with no end.

**This needs the owner's ruling and it is not a copy problem.** Either release
is not uniform and clearing a parent fetter collapses its children, in which
case the product should say so and the ladder should show the collapse, or the
horizon number should not be shown next to the tier rate. Showing both invites
the division, and one of the six ICPs does it unprompted.

**Claim five. We never sell anybody's data, and the email is the only key.**

Sofia. HOLD, hard. "I will not put a client anywhere near this until I know who
can pull it back out. You are telling me you will never sell it. I believe you.
That is not the question. The question is who can ask for it and get it."

As the funnel is written in section B, the email address is the only key, so
anything that knows an address can retrieve that person's somatic and
psychological profile. Section B already names this and calls it not optional.
The panel confirms the priority from the other side: Sofia is the highest value
ICP in the roster because she brings clients, and this is the only thing
standing between her and a recommendation.

**Where disbelief does not land, and this is worth recording.** Nobody
disbelieved the release protocol. Nobody disbelieved that emotion is charge and
charge sits somewhere. Nobody disbelieved the wheel. The mechanism survives
contact. What breaks is the join between the questionnaire and the body, the
moral vocabulary at the low end, and the arithmetic of the ladder. All three
are fixable without touching the engine.

## 6. Delivery format. Video, or raw.

The panel splits three ways.

| Position | Panel share | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Wants the frame set before the result | 38 percent | Angela, Sofia, Ana |
| Wants the raw result and will work it out | 34 percent | Marcus, Derek, James |
| Takes either, as long as the numbers sit underneath | 28 percent | Diane, and the undecided remainder |

**Marcus. REFUSE, and it is a format refusal.** "If a video plays at me before
I see my own result, I have been sold to. I asked a question and you gave me a
commercial. Show me the number."

**Angela. CONFUSE, without the frame.** "I got a wheel and some words I did not
know and a score of 44 and I did not know if 44 was bad. I needed somebody to
tell me what I was looking at."

**Derek. RESIST.** "I will not watch a video. I have never watched a video in
an app. Put it in text and I will read it in nine seconds."

**Diane. HOLD.** "I will watch ninety seconds if it is about my result. I will
not watch three minutes about your philosophy."

**Recommendation. Ship raw. Add one screen, not a film.**

Three arguments, in order of weight.

**One, the split does not favour video.** The 34 percent who want it raw
include the two ICPs most likely to walk on contact with a video, and one of
them walks permanently. The 38 percent who want the frame do not need a film to
get it, they need to know whether 44 is bad. That is one screen of text.

**Two, a hosted video is an outbound request.** `CLAUDE.md` already records
that `fonts.googleapis.com` contradicts the privacy posture because it sends a
person's IP to a third party before they have typed anything. A video player
does the same thing, on a page that is about to display somatic and
psychological self report, and it does it with a heavier fingerprint than a
font. Choosing video means arguing against a position already taken in the
repo.

**Three, raw ships now and video does not.** Production, hosting, a script, a
voice and a rerecord every time the tier names change. B0 already names the
cost. The panel does not pay for that cost in comprehension, it pays for it in
one screen.

**What the one screen has to carry.** Four lines, before the wheel, on the
result page. What the score is. Whether this score is high or low and against
what. What the instrument knows and does not, as the identification number and
its interval. What the next single action is. Skippable, and never in front of
the number.

**Where video does belong.** Cold traffic, above the funnel, as the ad. Not on
the result page. A person who has answered 63 questions has earned the number,
not a preamble.

## 7. What kind of funnel they think it is, and what would get them in

**What they think it is, unprompted.**

Diane: "A lead magnet for a coaching programme. The score is the hook and the
call is the product." HOLD.

Marcus: "A personality quiz with better art direction. I have seen the
sixteen letter one, I have seen the nine number one, and this one has a body
diagram." RESIST.

Angela: "A spiritual assessment." BUY, and she is the only one who reads it as
what it is describing itself as.

James: "A funnel. Obviously a funnel. I have built them. The question is what
is at the bottom and whether the price is on the page." HOLD.

Derek: "A supplement ad structure. Diagnose, then sell the fix." RESIST.

Sofia: "A practitioner tool being sold to consumers, which usually means the
practitioner version is the real product." HOLD.

**Four of six read it as a lead magnet before it has said anything.** That is
the default read of any quiz on the internet in 2026 and the product does not
get to opt out of it. It has to break the pattern explicitly in the first
screen or it inherits every assumption.

**What kind would actually get them in.**

Not an insight funnel. A cost funnel.

The insight funnel says: find out who you are. That is the sixteen letter
product and the panel has taken it and forgotten it. Marcus, Diane, Derek and
James all reject it in four different sentences.

The cost funnel says: find out what you are paying. It is the same 63
questions and the same result page. What changes is what the ad promises and
what the first line under the score says. It carries the stakes B0 asks for
because a cost is a stake, and it talks to the pain without naming the pain,
which is what gets past Gordon's whole category of objection even though
nothing gets past Gordon.

**The step that loses the most people, and it is not the quiz.**

Simulated against the panel, using the funnel as written in section B.

| Step | As written in section B | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Sees the ad | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Starts the quiz | 310 | 310 |
| Finishes all 63, with duration stated and progress shown | 186 | 186 |
| Enters an email | 121 | 121 |
| Installs the app to see the score | 52 | score shown on the web, 121 |
| Signs up, record comes down | 47 | installs for the protocol, 63 |
| Sees a score | 47 | 121 |

Step 4 of the funnel as written is: to see the score they download at least the
free app. That step costs the panel 69 of 121 people, and they are the people
who already answered 63 questions and handed over an address. They did the
work. Withholding the number they were promised is the single largest loss in
the funnel and it is self inflicted.

**Recommendation. The score is the promise. Pay it on the web.** Show the
score, the band, the cost line, and the identification interval on the result
page, in the browser, immediately. Gate the release protocol, the wheel, the
112 addresses and the meter behind the install. The gift of 100 patterns is
already the reason to install and it is a better reason than withholding a
number. A person who has seen their own score and been told what it costs has a
reason to install. A person who has been told to install to find out has a
reason to close the tab, and 69 of them do.

## The narrative

From the mechanics the engine already runs. 112 addresses, each one a seat.
Nine charges. 21 laws asked three ways. CQ as intention times integrity over
resistance. A release that is one sentence over one address through one
channel.

**For the ad and the landing page.**

    Emotion is charge. Charge that does not leave has to be held.
    There are 112 places in you where it sits, and holding it costs.
    63 questions read which seats are loaded and what the load is taking.
    Then you get the sentences that let it go, one seat at a time.
    Nothing to believe. The body already kept the ledger.

**The strongest single sentence.**

    You are still paying for every charge you never released.

Second position, if the ad needs a question instead of a statement:

    Find out what you are paying to hold it.

Third, for the result page, directly under the score:

    This is what the holding costs.

Why the first one wins. It is second person and present tense, so the stakes
are current and not historical. Paying is the cost the panel asked for in
question 3 and it is mechanical, not moral. Released names the mechanism the
product actually runs. It contains no wellness word, no promise, no adjective
about the person, and nothing the engine cannot defend.
