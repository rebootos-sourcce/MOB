# The aesthetic

Ruled in `TASKS.md` FN4: the funnel wears the app's aesthetic. Fonts, colours,
the way Atüned is written, and powered by Source OS. He has not chosen which
page it echoes, so what is shared is the design language and not a copy of a
screen.

**No hex value appears in this document.** The palette is in
`funnel/tokens.css`, which is generated by `tools/tokens.py` from the app's own
stylesheet and carries 44 tokens. A palette restated by hand is correct on the
day it is copied and wrong afterwards. Assertion 16 in `marketing/tests.js`
fails on any literal colour anywhere in an art direction field, and assertion
16 also fails on any ink that is not a token the generated file defines.

---

## 1. The colour is decided by the address, not by the designer

This is the rule that makes art and copy one seat rather than two.

Every hook is keyed to a charge and a seat. The seat has a token. So the hook's
ink is a lookup, exactly as the hook's words are:

    Root      --root
    Sacral    --sacral
    Solar     --solar
    Heart     --heart
    Throat    --throat
    3rd Eye   --eye
    Crown     --crown

One rename, `3rd Eye` to `--eye`, and it is named in `tests.js` rather than
inferred. Assertion 17 fails if any hook's ink is not the token its seat says,
so a heart hook cannot drift into a root colour because somebody preferred it.

**Muted is a saturation ruling and not a contrast one.** The generated file says
so at the surface ramp block, and it changes no hue. So a marketing surface may
not reach for a brighter version of a seat colour to make a page louder. The
seat colours are argued from autonomic response and a saturated version of one
is a different argument.

**One dark ink sits on every seat colour.** `--on-flat` exists because all seven
seats are light enough to carry the same dark ink in every lighting. So a filled
seat panel takes `--on-flat` and nothing else, and there is no per seat ink
decision to make.

---

## 2. Two tokens carry a trap, and both traps are written into the generated file

**`--gold` is an alias for `--accent`, and the accent is blue.** Ruled. So
everything reaching for the word gold gets blue, which is correct for the
eyebrows and the rules that were gold before the ruling, and wrong for the one
thing asked for in actual gold. `--au` is the real gold, muted and warm, and
the generated file's own comment records that Source OS under the wordmark
rendered blue twice **because the token lied about its own name**. Anything in
marketing that wants gold asks for `--au` by name.

**`--num` is not a second family.** Its comment records the defect: the token
was documented as carrying a face that was never in the build, so every number
in the product fell through to `system-ui`, and which face that is depends on
the machine. `document.fonts.check` returned true and hid it, because check
answers whether text can be rendered at all and a fallback counts. Inter carries
the digits now. **Any figure in a marketing surface asks for tabular figures
through `font-variant-numeric`**, which Inter supports, because a column of
readings that does not line up is the one thing that makes an instrument look
like a toy.

---

## 3. The type

One family. `--sans` is Inter, with Inter Tight and then the system stack
behind it. There is no second face and no per person override, because the font
tuner is gone on the owner's ruling.

The reason Inter is the face is measured and worth carrying into marketing:
**Lexend clipped the rail. Witness rendered as Wit.** Inter is narrower at the
same size and carries more characters per line without dropping the point size.
So a marketing surface that needs another word on a line gets it from the face
rather than from shrinking the type.

**Google Fonts is gone and the product makes no outbound request at all.**
Inter is embedded as a variable font, latin subset, 300 to 700 in one file, at
about 48 kilobytes raw and 64 as base64. `tests/design.js` gate 7 watches the
network and fails on any request that is not one of the two local rasters. A
marketing page that pulls a webfont, a tracking pixel or an embedded video
breaks a gate that is already green, so any such asset is self hosted or it does
not ship.

---

## 4. The contrast floor, and why it is a reach argument rather than a taste one

`--dim` was below AA everywhere it appeared and carried **104 of the 105
contrast failures** on one surface. One value, 104 defects. It is the only token
marking the second tier of the information architecture: every eyebrow, every
key label, every explanatory subline.

The part that matters for marketing is the reason the generated file gives for
moving it: **six of the thirteen people in the reference sample are 57 or
older, and contrast sensitivity falls materially past 55.** At the old value
half the sample lost the whole layer that tells them what they are looking at.

The reference ICP roster in `engine/data/people.js` runs from 35 to 66. James is
57, Rosa 61, Wren 66. So a marketing surface that sets its subheads and its
eyebrows in `--dim` at small sizes is not making a stylistic choice, it is
deciding which half of the panel can read the second tier.

## 5. Motion

Three curves and four durations, named for the job so a hover and a tab cannot
drift to the same speed. The generated file records what forced this: **381 of
384 live animated elements ran on the browser default ease**, a symmetric curve
that reads as mechanical because it decelerates as slowly as it accelerates.

    --ease-out    a thing arriving. fast off the mark, long settle
    --ease-in     a thing leaving. it does not need watching out
    --ease-land   a thing that lands. one overshoot, no ring

    --t-micro     120ms   hover, press, focus
    --t-element   220ms   one thing entering or leaving
    --t-surface   320ms   a panel or a rail
    --t-context   420ms   a whole tab, and the longest anything may take

**The floor and the ceiling are both stated in the file and both matter here.**
Below about 100ms a change reads as a jump with no cause. Above about 150 the
pointer has already left. So a marketing hover is 120ms and not 300.

**And a rule this system adds, from the hook table rather than from the
tokens.** Six of the seventeen hooks specify no motion at all: H02, H10, H11,
H13, H14 and H17. There is no pattern by seat and the first version of this
paragraph asserted one, which was checked and was false. The reasons are per
hook and each is written in its own art field. Two are worth repeating because
they generalise: motion on a line served to somebody carrying anger reads as
prodding, and motion on a line served to somebody who feels watched reads as
being watched. The decision and the reason sit on the same row as the words,
which is the point of art and copy being one seat.

## 6. Spacing, radius, and the touch floor

Three spacing steps and nothing between them. `--g1` 6px inside one control,
`--g2` 10px between controls, `--g3` 16px between blocks. Gaps of 5, 7, 8, 9
and 11 were all doing one of those three jobs with no reason stated, so they
read as drift rather than as rhythm.

Three radii, `--r` 16px, `--r-s` 11px, `--r-xs` 8px.

`--tap` is 44px and it is a floor, not a target. **69 interactive elements
measured under it**, which is why the number lives in one token instead of
forty padding declarations.

`--shadow` is one value. There is not a second elevation.

---

## 7. Icons are ring, not fill

Standing ruling. The seat glyphs in `canon.js` `SEATGLYPH` are stroked paths in
a 24 unit box and the six gate glyphs in `GATEGLYPH` are the same. So an
illustration for a hook is drawn in the same idiom: outline, 24 unit box,
stroke weight carrying the meaning.

The lesson underneath it is in `nodes.js`: **thirty nine saboteurs shared one
glyph** and a person opening the deck saw the same mark thirty nine times. The
fix was not thirty nine new marks, it was six, each drawn as what a structure
does to a shape under load. That is the model for hook illustration and it is
why every art field in `hooks.js` describes a structural operation on a shape
rather than a picture of a feeling.

**And one prohibition worth writing out.** H02 sits at the Heart and its art
rule says never draw a heart. The seat is called Heart and the organ is not the
subject. A drawn heart moves the page from instrument to greetings card in one
mark, and it is the single easiest way to lose the position.

## 8. How the name is written

The app writes it `At<span class="um">u</span>ned` with a dot on the bar rather
than on the letter, answering to the background through `--um`, which is white
on dark as ruled. **Source OS** sits under the wordmark, and the boot screen
reads **Powered by Source OS**.

It is written **Source OS** and not SOURCE OS. The brief says the second and the
standing voice ruling is sentence case with no all caps copy, so the app's
casing is the correct one and the brief's is the informal one.

**Two findings in the shipped funnel, reported and not fixed, because
`funnel/` is another seat's.**

1. **`funnel/index.html` has the umlaut mechanism and not the wordmark.** The
   dot rule is there at line 38, using `--um`. The `<title>` is the plain
   spelling. So the page carries the machinery for the mark and does not set it.
2. **Source OS appears nowhere in `funnel/index.html` or `funnel/quiz.html`.**
   Searched both for all three casings. FN4 asks for it by name and it is not
   on the funnel yet.

**Two things were checked and are not defects,** recorded because both looked
like one and this repository has been bitten three times by a probe reporting
its own bug.

1. `--measure` and `--gut` are used in `index.html` and are not in
   `tokens.css`. Both are defined locally at line 23. `--gut` is 16px, which is
   `--g3`'s value under a second name, and that is token drift rather than a
   missing definition.
2. The generated file's header says 44 tokens and a first count returned 45. The
   count was wrong: it caught `--law-accent`, which appears only inside a
   comment explaining where the accent came from. Stripped of comments the file
   declares exactly 44 with no duplicates, and the header is correct.

## 9. The one aesthetic rule that comes from the position rather than the tokens

The position is instruments, not wellness. Every art field in `hooks.js` is
written against that and several of them refuse a specific image:

- no wound, no crack, no shattered glass. Damage imagery makes a reading a
  verdict
- no comparison drawn as a bar or a ladder. A bar chart of two people is the
  picture of the problem, rendered approvingly
- no light, no halo, no mandala, and the reason is addressed: H16 is served to
  people who have already bought the mandala, so the coordinate grid is the
  exotic object on that key and the halo is the familiar one
- no question mark standing in for anxiety. H09's four paths end in nothing,
  because a question mark is a joke about the thing the line is naming
- the more an anatomical figure looks like a plate out of a reference text, the
  harder it works

**And nothing resolves on screen.** Several art fields say it separately and it
is one rule: a figure that heals, a gap that closes, an offset that comes into
register, is a promise the product has not made. The reading names a load. What
moves is the person's decision and an animation may not make it for them.
