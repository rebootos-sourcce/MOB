## Behavior profile

Avarice / Prodigality — limiting-belief expression
Behavior: Hoarding beyond needs or spending to secure approval; leverages possession for control; sacrifices trust to acquisition.
Movement: ATTACH to resource → HOLD or DISRUPT its flow → CONTRACT safety to possession → REPEL reciprocity.
Belief/reframe: I am safe and valuable only if I keep more than enough—or spend to prove that I matter.
Trigger: Fear of loss, comparison, insecurity, sudden money, status pressure, or opportunity to control through resources.
Real-world example: A person who could afford a fair share stops withholding it to gain control, pays transparently, and keeps a sustainable reserve.
Practice: Identify one resource choice: fulfill an obligation, release one unnecessary grasp, or practice bounded generosity without debt or resentment.
Differential: Poverty, prudent saving, disability-related needs, ambition, and financial planning are not greed; impulsive overspending is not generosity.
Success: Makes sustainable resource decisions under scarcity cues, honors obligations, and gives freely only within real capacity.

## Four depth passes

### Pass 1: literary and source image

Dante places avarice AND wasteful prodigality on the same terrace; the penitents lie face down, and examples praise generosity and honorable use of goods. The vice/virtue names form a source frame, not a psychological diagnosis.

Sources: https://danteworlds.laits.utexas.edu/purgatory/07avarice.html, https://www.ismreview.yale.edu/climbing-the-mountain-of-the-beatitudes

### Pass 3: limiting belief and real-world consequence

Belief: I am safe and valuable only if I keep more than enough—or spend to prove that I matter. Trigger: Fear of loss, comparison, insecurity, sudden money, status pressure, or opportunity to control through resources. Cost: Obligations, fair exchange, and care may yield to possession; depletion or rigidity destabilizes relationships and future security.

Sources: https://www.abct.org/get-help/what-is-cognitive-behavioral-therapy/, https://www.abct.org/get-help/what-to-expect-from-cognitive-behavioral-therapies/

### Pass 4: polar discriminants and journey integration

Vice/virtue discriminant: Poverty, prudent saving, disability-related needs, ambition, and financial planning are not greed; impulsive overspending is not generosity. Cycle: Loss cue → possession-as-safety belief → hoard or prove worth through spending → short relief → deeper insecurity.
