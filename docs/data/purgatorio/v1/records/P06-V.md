# purgatorio:P06-V — Gluttony

## Behavior profile

Gluttony — limiting-belief expression
Behavior: Repeatedly consumes or seeks stimulation past a chosen useful limit while the underlying need remains unaddressed.
Movement: SEEK relief → APPROACH substitute → HOLD repetition → AVOID underlying feeling.
Belief/reframe: More consumption will finally fill the discomfort or emptiness I feel.
Trigger: Stress, emptiness, boredom, reward cues, loneliness, uncertainty, or fatigue.
Real-world example: After a stressful meeting a person notices an urge to scroll for hours, contacts a friend, then chooses a bounded period of entertainment.
Practice: Before a habitual reach, ask “What do I actually need?”; choose nourishment, rest, connection, pleasure, or a pause deliberately.
Differential: Hunger, enjoyment, celebration, medical appetite changes, food insecurity, or healthy self-soothing are not automatically gluttony.
Success: Experiences discomfort or appetite, accurately distinguishes needs, and chooses sufficient behavior without harsh deprivation.

### Pass 1: literary and source image

The sixth terrace places desired fruit and water out of reach; exemplary stories emphasize temperance and right relation to appetite. The vice/virtue names form a source frame, not a psychological diagnosis.

### Pass 3: limiting belief and real-world consequence

Belief: More consumption will finally fill the discomfort or emptiness I feel. Trigger: Stress, emptiness, boredom, reward cues, loneliness, uncertainty, or fatigue. Cost: Attention and choice narrow; consumption competes with relationships, rest, finances, and intended work.

Sources: https://www.abct.org/get-help/what-is-cognitive-behavioral-therapy/, https://www.abct.org/get-help/what-to-expect-from-cognitive-behavioral-therapies/

### Pass 4: polar discriminants and journey integration

Vice/virtue discriminant: Hunger, enjoyment, celebration, medical appetite changes, food insecurity, or healthy self-soothing are not automatically gluttony. Cycle: Discomfort → substitute intake → short relief → unmet need persists → repeat.
