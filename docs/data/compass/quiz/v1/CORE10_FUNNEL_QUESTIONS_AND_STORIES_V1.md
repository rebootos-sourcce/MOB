# ATUNED — Core 10, Three-Register Funnel Quiz
**Status:** Question and story content package; NOT wired into the live funnel or CQ.  
**Canonical source:** `atuned_src/engine/data/canon.js#SI`. Editorial selection is exactly 10 of 21 laws across **all 7 bands**.

## How it works

- **Behavior (0–10)**: how often did the person act in alignment when they had a fair opportunity?
- **Pressure (0–10)**: how often did they act in alignment when it became uncomfortable, risky or costly?
- **Mechanism (unscored)**: what loss or consequence did they anticipate, if any? The user chooses or supplies their own words, and can reject all guesses.

**0** = never; **5** = about half the eligible times; **10** = always. Missing or inapplicable = null. This is **20 scored prompts and 10 unscored contextual prompts**, not 30 numeric measures. The 4–6 display range is the owner's conceptual oscillation band, not a validated average.

## Full question list and story payloads

### 01. Truth · Throat

**Behavior — 0–10:** When someone needed an honest answer, how often did you give it?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When honesty could upset someone, how often did you still tell the truth when safe?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you held back, what were you afraid might happen?

Options: `Rejection` · `Conflict` · `Punishment` · `Loss of privacy` · `It was unsafe` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The promise you didn't mean:** A friend asks you to help this weekend. You already need rest, but you say yes to avoid disappointing them. By Saturday, you feel trapped and resentful.

**When aligned:** You give an honest answer, even when it is uncomfortable. Both people can make a real choice.

**When reversed:** You hide your limits to keep approval. The promise and your real intention drift apart.

**Potential release:** Notice the belief that an honest no will cost connection.

**Become:** Give one respectful, truthful answer about what you can actually do.

**Context boundary:** Privacy, danger and coercion are not failures to practice truth.

### 02. Transparency · Throat

**Behavior — 0–10:** When your choices affected someone, how often did you share what they needed to know?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When admitting a mistake felt embarrassing, how often did you tell them?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you hid something important, what were you trying to protect?

Options: `Reputation` · `Trust` · `Privacy` · `Safety` · `Avoiding blame` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The mistake no one saw:** You spot an error in a shared project. Nobody else has noticed. If you say nothing, the team will make a decision using the wrong numbers.

**When aligned:** You name the error and what it affects. Others can make informed choices and correct course.

**When reversed:** You protect your image by hiding something relevant. People act on incomplete information.

**Potential release:** Notice the worry that admitting a mistake will make you less valuable.

**Become:** Share one necessary fact clearly with the person affected.

**Context boundary:** Transparency is not an obligation to share private or unsafe information.

### 03. Unity · Crown

**Behavior — 0–10:** When someone disagreed with you, how often did you treat them with respect?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When your group judged someone, how often did you decide for yourself how to treat them?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When respect was difficult, what felt threatened?

Options: `Belonging` · `Values` · `Safety` · `Pride` · `Old hurt` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The person everyone disagrees with:** A group conversation turns against someone who sees things differently. You don't agree with them, but you notice people are talking as if they matter less.

**When aligned:** You keep your judgment of an idea separate from your respect for a person.

**When reversed:** You join in treating a person as less worthy because they differ from your group.

**Potential release:** Notice the need to belong that can overpower your own values.

**Become:** Disagree with one idea without diminishing the person.

**Context boundary:** Equal dignity doesn't require agreement or staying near someone unsafe.

### 04. Presence · 3rd Eye

**Behavior — 0–10:** When someone was speaking, how often did you give them your attention?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When worry or distraction interrupted you, how often did you return to the moment?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you drifted away, what drew your attention?

Options: `Future worries` · `Past events` · `Overload` · `Restlessness` · `Wanting escape` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The conversation you missed:** Someone close to you is telling you about a hard day. Your phone lights up. You keep checking it and miss what they actually needed.

**When aligned:** You return your attention and understand more accurately what is being shared.

**When reversed:** You leave the moment without noticing. Assumptions replace what was actually said.

**Potential release:** Notice what pulls your attention away, without judging the distraction itself.

**Become:** Put one distraction aside and listen long enough to reflect back what you heard.

**Context boundary:** Pain, disability, nervous-system load and divided demands can limit attention; adjust expectations fairly.

### 05. Compassion · Heart

**Behavior — 0–10:** When someone struggled, how often did you take their feelings seriously?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When tired or annoyed, how often did you stay kind without ignoring your limits?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you pulled away from someone's pain, what made it hard?

Options: `Being drained` · `Not knowing how to help` · `Fear of getting pulled in` · `Discomfort` · `My own hurt` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — Fixing when they needed listening:** A colleague tells you they're overwhelmed. You immediately list solutions. Later you realize they needed to be heard before they could think about next steps.

**When aligned:** You make space for the person's actual needs while keeping your own limits.

**When reversed:** You try to stop your discomfort by fixing or dismissing the other person.

**Potential release:** Notice the urge to end discomfort quickly.

**Become:** Ask once: 'Would listening or problem-solving help more?'

**Context boundary:** Compassion does not require self-sacrifice, endless availability or ignoring boundaries.

### 06. Courage · Solar

**Behavior — 0–10:** When something mattered but felt scary, how often did you take a reasonably safe step?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When speaking up might bring disapproval, how often did you speak when safe?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you held back, what did you expect could happen?

Options: `Rejection` · `Loss` · `Conflict` · `Uncertainty` · `Real danger` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The conversation you keep delaying:** A workplace issue keeps coming back. You know a conversation could help, but you rehearse it for days because you're worried how it will land.

**When aligned:** You take a proportionate, reasonably safe step aligned with what matters.

**When reversed:** You avoid every possible step and let an important issue continue without review.

**Potential release:** Notice whether rejection, conflict or uncertainty is driving the delay.

**Become:** Choose one small, safe action toward the conversation.

**Context boundary:** Declining to enter danger is sound judgment, not lack of courage.

### 07. Duty · Solar

**Behavior — 0–10:** When you made a promise you could reasonably keep, how often did you follow through?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When you couldn't meet a commitment, how often did you tell the other person in time?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When a commitment slipped, what got in the way?

Options: `Overload` · `Difficulty saying no` · `Fear of disappointing someone` · `Avoidance` · `Changed circumstances` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The yes that became too much:** You agree to finish a task by Friday. New demands pile up, and by Wednesday you know you won't finish. You consider staying silent.

**When aligned:** You keep workable promises, or speak up early so the other person can adapt.

**When reversed:** You hide an impossible commitment and let others discover the cost too late.

**Potential release:** Notice the belief that disappointing someone makes you unworthy.

**Become:** Renegotiate one commitment while there is still time.

**Context boundary:** Forced obligations and unreasonable demands are not freely chosen duties.

### 08. Accountability · Solar

**Behavior — 0–10:** When a mistake affected someone, how often did you try to repair it?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When apologizing was uncomfortable, how often did you apologize directly when appropriate?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you delayed repair, what stopped you?

Options: `Shame` · `Rejection` · `Punishment` · `Not knowing what to do` · `Feeling it was too late` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The explanation before the apology:** You forget something that mattered to a friend. When they mention it, you immediately describe how busy you've been. They still haven't heard you own the impact.

**When aligned:** You name what you did, acknowledge the effect, and offer a proportionate repair.

**When reversed:** You use a true explanation to avoid taking your share of responsibility.

**Potential release:** Notice the fear that admitting a mistake makes you fundamentally bad.

**Become:** Say what happened and ask what repair would be useful, when appropriate.

**Context boundary:** Taking your share does not mean accepting blame for someone else's actions.

### 09. Temperance · Sacral

**Behavior — 0–10:** When you'd had enough of something you wanted, how often did you stop?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When stressed, how often did you choose what you needed over an escape that left you worse off?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you kept going past enough, what were you looking for?

Options: `Comfort` · `Stress relief` · `Control` · `Filling emptiness` · `Automatic habit` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — One more hour:** You keep scrolling late at night, looking for a little relief after a hard day. An hour later, you're more tired and the relief hasn't lasted.

**When aligned:** You recognize what you actually need and choose an action that supports it.

**When reversed:** You keep reaching for more even when it is no longer helping.

**Potential release:** Notice whether comfort, avoidance or habit is behind the next reach.

**Become:** Pause at a familiar moment and choose rest, connection or another real need.

**Context boundary:** Do not label health conditions or basic needs as lack of temperance.

### 10. Non-Harm · Root

**Behavior — 0–10:** When upset, how often did you avoid unnecessary harm in what you said or did?

**Pressure — 0–10:** When setting a boundary, how often did you do it without trying to punish the other person?

**Mechanism — choose what fits or write your own:** When you wanted to hurt back, what felt threatened?

Options: `Being heard` · `Respect` · `Control` · `Safety` · `Being understood` · `Something else, in my own words` · `None of these` · `I'm not sure` · `Prefer not to answer`.

**Story — The true but cutting reply:** Someone upsets you. You have an accurate criticism ready, but you also know the way you could say it would be needlessly cruel.

**When aligned:** You state what is true and set a firm limit without adding unnecessary injury.

**When reversed:** You use truth or silence as punishment and increase the damage.

**Potential release:** Notice what part of you wants the other person to feel the pain you felt.

**Become:** Express one boundary without humiliation or retaliation.

**Context boundary:** Protecting yourself or others from immediate harm is not the same as unnecessary aggression.
