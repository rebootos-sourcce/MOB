# Marma validation, against the pasted "112 node energetic architecture"

Research pass run 25 September, checking a document pasted into the session
against real sources on marma, the system of vital points first written down
in the Sushruta Samhita, a Sanskrit surgical text. Logged from `TASKS.md`
block `BI`, which stays the short version; this file is the full evidence,
confidence tagged and sourced.

One limit applies to every tag below. This session's network refused every
attempt to open a page in full: Wikipedia, PubMed Central, PubMed,
archive.org, wisdomlib, chestofbooks and the journal sites. So every check
rests on search results from named sources, not on full texts read start to
finish. Tags used throughout:

- **Verified**: a named source states it, and the passage came back in a
  search result.
- **Unconfirmed**: plausible, but no source found states it.
- **Unsupported**: no source found, or the sources contradict it.
- **My reading**: an inference, marked as one, not a sourced fact.

Firmer evidence, if wanted: someone on an unblocked network reading the
marma chapter of the 1911 Bhishagratna translation in full lifts the count
and names from "search result" to "read in full." Estimated about an hour.

## 1. The real count and breakdown, against the document's

The classical system:

- 107 marmas, set out in the Sushruta Samhita, Sharira Sthana (its anatomy
  book), chapter 6. Verified (1911 Bhishagratna translation; two PubMed
  Central articles; IJSHR 2019 review; Charak Samhita Online).
- By region, 107 in all. Verified (IJAR conceptual review; The Ayurveda
  Experience; Healthline).
  - 11 in each limb, so 44.
  - 12 in the chest and abdomen: 9 chest and 3 abdomen.
  - 14 in the back.
  - 37 above the collarbones.
- By tissue: muscle 11, blood vessel 41, ligament and tendon 27, bone 8,
  joint 20. Verified (Charak Samhita Online; IJSHR 2019).
- By outcome of injury: 19 fatal within a day, 33 fatal within weeks, 3
  fatal when an embedded object is pulled out, 44 maiming, 8 painful.
  Verified, in the 1911 translation's own words.
- The text's frame is surgical: where each point is, what it is made of,
  what a wound there does. The document calls it "classical neuro-energetic
  physiology," which is the document's frame, not the text's. Verified
  (1911 translation; IJSHR 2019).
- The document glosses *sira* as "nerve plexuses." Verified (PubMed Central
  articles; Charak Samhita Online). The sources give *sira* as blood
  vessels, and *snayu* as ligaments and tendons, which some readings extend
  to nerves. 41 of the 107 are blood vessel points. The gloss pulls the
  system toward a nervous system reading the text does not make.

The document against it:

- It claims 107 points plus the mind (Manas) make 108, and 4 outside points
  make 112.
- Its own rows, recounted by script. Verified (recount of the document).
  - Head and neck 40 (it says 38).
  - Chest 9. Abdomen 5 (classical 3). Back 14. Arms 22. Legs 22.
  - Row total 112 before the 4 outside points are added, 116 with them.
- **Correction to the finding first logged at `TASKS.md` BI1.** The rows do
  sum to 112. But one of the 112 is Manas, which the document itself calls
  the mind, not a physical point. The physical figure is 111, not 112.
- Exactly where 111 comes from. Verified (arithmetic on the document
  against the classical list): 107 classical, minus 2 (the arm pair of
  Lohitaksha is dropped), plus Hanu 2, Agrahastha 2 and Agrapada 2. The
  limbs come to 22 each only because an invented fingertip or toe tip point
  replaced the Lohitaksha that was moved out.
- "107 plus the mind is 108" appears on modern charts and blogs, not in the
  classical text. Unsupported as classical. One search result attributes to
  Frawley, Ranade and Lele's 2003 book a remark that the skin could be
  called the 108th; the book could not be opened to confirm. Unconfirmed.
- A real 108 point body system does exist, and it is a different one: Tamil
  Siddha *varmam* counts 12 lethal points and 96 touch points, with Tamil
  names. None of its names are in the document. Verified (National Health
  Portal of India; Varmam Academy; Wikipedia, Varma kalai).
- No system claiming 112 marmas was found.
- A measurement error: the document places Ani "3 inches" above the elbow
  and the knee. The text says 3 *angula*, three of the person's own finger
  breadths. Verified (Easy Ayurveda, Aani marma; JAIMS review of Ani). One
  dissection study uses roughly 2 cm per angula, about 6 cm, a little over
  2 inches (approximation).

## 2. The names

All 43 classical names are present. Verified by name (1911 translation;
Easy Ayurveda pages for the chest, the back and Simanta; JAIMS review of
the back points; the per point papers in section 3).

- Above the collarbones, 14 names: Nila (the document spells it Neela),
  Manya, Matrika, Krikatika (Krukatika), Vidhura (Vidhuram), Phana, Apanga,
  Avarta, Shankha, Utkshepa, Sthapani, Simanta, Shringataka, Adhipati. The
  document's counts for these 14 sum to 37, the classical total. The ones
  checked one by one match: Matrika 8, Shringataka 4, Simanta 5, Sthapani 1,
  Utkshepa 2.
- Chest, 5: Hridaya, Stanamula, Stanarohita, Apalapa, Apastambha.
- Abdomen, 3: Guda, Basti, Nabhi.
- Back, 7: Katikataruna, Kukundara, Nitamba, Parshvasandhi, Brihati,
  Amsaphalaka, Amsa.
- Limbs, 14. Verified (1911 translation; Easy Ayurveda, brief description
  of 107 marmas). In the leg: Kshipra, Talahridaya, Kurcha, Kurchashira,
  Gulpha, Indrabasti, Janu, Ani, Urvi, Lohitaksha and Vitapa. In the arm,
  Manibandha, Kurpara and Kakshadhara stand in place of Gulpha, Janu and
  Vitapa. The 1911 translation: "The Marmas in the hands are almost
  identical with those of the legs, with the exception that Manivandha,
  Kurpara and Kakshadhara Marmas occur in the place of the Gulpha, Janu and
  Vitapa Marmas respectively." Bahvi is the arm's name for Urvi.

Not classical:

- **Manas.** The mind, added to reach 108. Unsupported as a marma.
- **Hanu**, the jaw. Real, but modern: listed in Lad and Durve's 2008 book
  *Marma Points of Ayurveda*, which presents 117 points, not Sushruta's
  107. Verified as modern (the book's publisher page and reading sample).
- **Agrahastha and Agrapada.** Unsupported (wisdomlib dictionary entries).
  Ordinary Sanskrit words for the tip of the hand and forepart of the foot;
  no source uses either as a marma. The document also counts "tips of all
  10 fingers combined" as 2.
- **Kantha**, the throat, appears only in the document's drawing, not the
  107. The word appears in other lists of vital places, but which list the
  drawing drew on is unclear. Unconfirmed (Dharmawiki, Kantha).

Misplaced:

- **Lohitaksha.** The text has four, one in each limb, at the root of the
  arm and the root of the thigh. The document keeps two, files them under
  the abdomen, and drops the arm pair. Verified (IJAAR Lohitaksha study;
  lower limb Lohitaksha study; JAIMS upper limb dissection study).

## 3. Twenty nerve and organ claims, spot checked

Tallies: nerve and blood vessel claims, 12 check out, 3 close with a wrong
detail, 2 plausible but unconfirmed, 3 wrong. Organ claims, 5 match the
classical text (the organ it names, or what it says an injury does), 1 is a
modern massage book claim, 3 put the organ in the wrong place, the rest are
either unsourced neighbours or have no source at all.

**Head and neck**

1. **Shankha**, the temple. Document: pterion, superficial temporal artery,
   auriculotemporal nerve. Organ: inner ear, temporal lobe. Close, key
   vessel missing: the pterion location is right, but journal papers name
   the middle meningeal artery beneath it (why the text rates this fatal
   within a day), which the document omits. Verified location, incomplete
   list (IJNRD 2024; JAHM review). Organ: unconfirmed.
2. **Utkshepa**, above the ear. Document: temporalis muscle, "motor
   branches of CN VII" (facial nerve). Organ: smell cortex. Wrong: the
   temporalis is driven by the deep temporal nerves from the trigeminal
   nerve's jaw branch, not the facial nerve. Unsupported (Kenhub; IMAIOS
   citing StatPearls; Utkshepa dissection report). Organ: unsupported.
3. **Sthapani**, between the brows. Document: supraorbital nerve,
   frontalis. Organ: pituitary, frontal lobes. Close, wrong branch:
   published anatomy puts the supratrochlear nerve and vessels and the
   procerus muscle here; the supraorbital nerve is a neighbour further out.
   Verified location, wrong nerve (IJAPR Sthapani study; StatPearls,
   supratrochlear). Organ: unsupported.
4. **Vidhura**, behind the ear. Document: facial nerve exit, greater
   auricular nerve. Organ: ears, balance. Checks out: studies place the
   facial nerve and its artery at their exit hole here. Verified (Vidhura
   and deafness study; JAIMS Vidhura study). Organ: ears match the text
   (injury causes deafness), verified; balance is an extension, unconfirmed.
5. **Krikatika**, skull meets spine. Document: occiput to C1, vertebral
   arteries. Organ: brainstem. Checks out (Easy Ayurveda, Krikatika; IJARESM
   review). Organ: neighbour, unconfirmed; the text's own effect is an
   unsteady, shaking head.
6. **Manya and Nila**, front and side of neck. Document: Manya with carotid
   and vagus nerve, organ vocal cords; Nila with internal jugular vein,
   organ thyroid. Checks out (Neela and Manya dissection study; JAIMS
   review). Organ: vocal cords match the text (injury brings loss or change
   of voice and loss of taste), verified; thyroid unsupported.
7. **Matrika**, 8 points on the neck. Document: sternocleidomastoid
   branches, ansa cervicalis. Organ: thymus, pharynx, lymph. Wrong: studies
   read Matrika as the great vessels of the neck, carotid and jugular (why
   the text rates them fatal within a day). Unsupported (IJAM Matrika
   study; Easy Ayurveda, Matrika). Organ: wrong place, the thymus sits in
   the chest behind the top of the breastbone. Unsupported (StatPearls,
   thymus).

**Chest**

8. **Hridaya**, centre of the chest. Document: cardiac nerve plexus. Organ:
   heart, thymus. Checks out (JAIMS Hridaya review). Organ: heart matches
   the text exactly (between the breasts, seat of the three qualities of
   mind, fatal within a day), verified; thymus is a neighbour, unconfirmed.
9. **Apastambha**, upper chest. Document: second rib cartilage, lung nerve
   plexus. Organ: bronchi, trachea. Checks out in substance, though reviews
   read it one level lower, at the third rib cartilage (IJAPR and IJRAP
   reviews). Organ: bronchi match the text's "air carrying channels"
   (injury brings cough, breathlessness, death), verified; one review
   excludes the trachea since there is one trachea and two points.
10. **Apalapa**, below the armpit. Document: lateral chest, long thoracic
    nerve. Organ: spleen (left), liver (right). Checks out for location
    (lateral thoracic and axillary arteries, one dissection notes the long
    thoracic nerve). Verified (lateral thoracic artery review; JAIMS
    Apalapa review). Organ: wrong place, the spleen sits upper left
    abdomen, the liver upper right. Unsupported (TeachMeAnatomy; Kenhub).

**Abdomen and pelvis**

11. **Nabhi**, the navel. Document: "celiac and superior mesenteric
    convergence." Organ: small intestine, stomach, pancreas. Wrong level:
    the celiac plexus sits at T12 to L1, above the navel, which sits three
    to four vertebrae lower near L3 to L4. Unsupported (Kenhub, celiac
    plexus; AJR cadaver study; Kenhub, umbilicus; International Journal of
    Spine Surgery CT study). Organ: neighbours, unconfirmed; the text calls
    the navel the origin of the vessels.
12. **Lohitaksha**, the groin. Document: femoral triangle, femoral nerve
    and artery. Organ: reproductive organs. Checks out for the leg point
    (femoral triangle, where the great saphenous vein joins the femoral
    vein; injury brings blood loss and paralysis). Verified (IJAAR; lower
    limb Lohitaksha study). Count and region still wrong, per section 2.
    Organ: unsupported.

**Back**

13. **Kukundara**, lower back dimples. Document: sacral dimples, sacral
    nerve branches. Organ: kidneys, adrenals. Plausible, unconfirmed:
    studies tie it to the sacroiliac joint and the nerve trunk feeding the
    sciatic nerve (injury brings loss of feeling and movement below); the
    document names nearby but different nerves (International Journal of
    AYUSH; TIJER dissection study). Organ: wrong place, kidneys lie between
    T12 and L3, well above the sacrum. Unsupported (TeachMeAnatomy; Kenhub).
14. **Parshvasandhi**, the flank. Document: lower rib cage. Organ: kidneys,
    adrenals. Plausible, unconfirmed (Easy Ayurveda, back points); no paper
    found names the document's nerves. Organ: kidneys do sit in the flank,
    but no marma paper makes the link, unconfirmed. Note the document gives
    kidneys and adrenals to two different points.
15. **Amsaphalaka**, the shoulder blade. Document: suprascapular nerve.
    Checks out (two JAIMS dissection studies): near the inner border of the
    shoulder blade, injury brings numbness and wasting of the arm.

**Arms**

16. **Kakshadhara**, front of the armpit. Document: brachial plexus
    "trunks," axillary artery. Organ: lungs, heart. Close, wrong division:
    the nerve network here is at the cords stage, wrapped around the
    axillary artery; the trunks sit higher, in the neck. Verified artery,
    wrong division named (JAIMS Kakshadhara study; PubMed Central, brachial
    plexus cords). Organ: unsupported.
17. **Kurpara**, the elbow. Document: ulnar nerve, inner elbow. Organ:
    liver (right), spleen (left). Checks out: the ulnar nerve runs behind
    the inner knob of the elbow, a maiming point in the text. Verified (AYU
    journal Kurpara study; Kurpara anatomical reviews). Organ: a modern
    massage book claim, verified as modern, unsupported as anatomy.
18. **Kshipra**, thumb web. Document: first dorsal interosseous muscle,
    deep radial artery. Organ: heart, lungs. Checks out: the radial artery
    passes between the two heads of that muscle; the text says death
    follows a delay from convulsions, read by modern authors as tetanus.
    Verified (JAIMS Kshipra study; wisdomlib essay). Organ: unsupported.

**Legs**

19. **Indrabasti**, the calf. Document: tibial nerve, posterior tibial
    vessels. Organ: bladder, adrenals. Checks out: posterior tibial and
    peroneal arteries, tibial nerve, soleus muscle; the text says death
    from blood loss. Verified (JAIMS lower limb dissection study;
    Indrabasti dissection study). Organ: unsupported.
20. **Vitapa**, between groin and scrotum. Document: ilioinguinal nerve,
    genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve. Organ: testes, ovaries.
    Checks out: spermatic cord, ilioinguinal nerve, inguinal canal.
    Verified (IJHSR Vitapa study; Vitapa dissection study). Organ: matches
    the text (injury brings impotence or low sperm count), verified.

**Organ claims traced to other systems, outside the twenty:**

- The liver at the right elbow and knee, spleen at the left (Kurpara,
  Janu): modern marma therapy writing, not the classical text, no
  anatomical path given. Verified as modern therapy claim, unsupported as
  anatomy (The Ayurveda Experience; Planet Ayurveda).
- Vision at Kurcha, small intestine at the arm's Indrabasti: same family of
  modern therapy writing. Verified as modern, unsupported as anatomy.
- "Kidney 1 (Wellspring)" at the foot's Talahridaya: verified as an
  overlap, unsupported as an organ (Journal of Acute Disease, 2024). That
  is the Chinese acupuncture point Yongquan, at the same spot. "Kidney"
  there names an acupuncture channel, not the organ. The hand version's
  "Heart-Pericardium" matches acupuncture point Pericardium 8, also at the
  centre of the palm (reading, not verified).

**The emotional and physiological claims:**

- The text touches the mind in only a few places, as outcomes of wounds,
  not stored feelings. Verified (1911 translation; Easy Ayurveda and
  IJARESM on Simanta): Hridaya is the seat of the three qualities of mind
  (sattva, rajas, tamas); injury to Simanta brings madness, fear, loss of
  mind; the fatal-within-a-day group brings bewilderment of mind and
  intellect.
- None of the document's per point emotions, such as "nihilism," "poverty
  consciousness" or "backstab trauma," appears in any source found.
  Unsupported.
- The document's claim that the points are "the exact physical and
  electromagnetic crossroads where chronic inflammatory density gathers"
  has no source measuring anything like it. Unsupported.
- Clinical evidence is thin. Verified (PubMed Central case report; JMIR
  Research Protocols): a single patient case report, and a randomised trial
  protocol published in 2026 for disc related leg nerve pain, which states
  no earlier randomised trial had tested marma therapy for that condition.

## 4. The four points outside the body

- The names Patala-Bindu, Pingala-Mandala, Ida-Mandala and
  Paramashiva-Dvaram, and the alternates Tiryak-Vigraha and Tiryak-Sthira,
  appear nowhere found as points. Unsupported. Not even stable inside the
  document itself: its drawing uses only the Tiryak names, its text gives
  both.
- The document's own brackets say what two of them are: "(Earth Star)" and
  "(Stellar Gateway)." These come from modern New Age lists of 12 or 13
  chakras, and even the sites that teach them say they are not from the
  Vedas. Verified (allcrystal.com; College of Psychic Studies).
- "The aura/Merkaba": the Merkaba teaching comes from Drunvalo Melchizedek's
  Flower of Life workshops, 1985 to 1994, published as a book in 1998.
  Verified (the book's archive record; drunvalo.org).

Real parts, used out of place:

- **Ida and Pingala** are real channels in hatha yoga, verified for the
  channels, unsupported for the placement (Wikipedia, Nadi (yoga)): the
  left (lunar) and right (solar) channels run inside the body from the base
  of the spine, linked to the left and right nostrils. The left/right
  polarity is traditional; placing them 6 to 12 inches outside the body is
  not. A 1994 study of 48 men (Telles, Nagarathna and Nagendra, Indian
  Journal of Physiology and Pharmacology) found a 37 percent rise in a
  metabolic measure with right nostril breathing and higher skin resistance
  with left nostril breathing, read by the authors as lower sympathetic
  arousal, about breathing through one nostril, not points outside the
  body.
- **Patala** is the lowest of seven underworlds in Hindu cosmology.
  Verified (Wikipedia, Patala). A modern teaching places seven lower
  chakras down the legs, with a Patala chakra at the soles as the seat of
  the darkest states, such as hatred and destructiveness (appears on
  several pages including a 1994 Hinduism Today article; exact wording not
  pinned to one source). The document puts Patala below the feet as a
  grounding anchor, reversing the one teaching that names it.
- **Paramashiva** is real: the highest aspect of Shiva in Kashmir Shaivism
  and Shaiva Siddhanta. Verified (Wikipedia, Parashiva). The nearest real
  point above the head in that tradition is the *dvadashanta*, "the end of
  twelve," twelve finger breadths above the crown, from the Vijnana
  Bhairava Tantra (Christopher Wallis at hareesh.org; nathas.org
  dictionary; wisdomlib). Twelve finger breadths is roughly 24 cm, about 9
  inches, not the document's 12 to 18 (approximation).
- **Kaula** is a real tantric tradition. Its best known 112 is the Vijnana
  Bhairava Tantra's 112 meditation techniques, dated seventh to ninth
  century. They are methods, not places in the body. Verified (Wikipedia;
  hareesh.org).
- **Siddha**: the Tamil Siddha system has 108 points, all in the body. No
  Siddha source placing points outside the body was found.
- The closest match to the document's shape is a modern teaching by
  Sadhguru of the Isha Foundation. Verified (isha.sadhguru.org; Sadhguru on
  X): 114 chakras, 2 outside the body and 112 within, of which 108 "can
  actually be worked upon" and 4 "just flower as a consequence." Not the
  same structure (his 4 are inside the body, his outside points are 2), and
  it is a living teacher's teaching, not a classical text.

**Verdict.** The four were very likely invented to reach 112 (reading, not
a sourced fact), resting on: no attestation for any of the names, parts
borrowed from modern sources, names unstable inside the document itself,
and the fit to this product's own 108 plus 4.

**This product's own four field anchors** (Sol Star, Stellar Gateway, Earth
Star, Gaia Gateway) come from the same modern New Age lists as the
document's borrowed parts. The crystal and energy healing sites describing
Gaia Gateway place it 18 to 24 inches below the feet. The Chakra glossary
entry's own standard, "held because it is the vocabulary the work is done
in, not because it has been measured," would apply to these four as well.
Verified (allcrystal.com; College of Psychic Studies; healing-sounds.com).
