# Harmonic research: can the nervous system be measured in hertz

Eero Vatnajokull, sound. 25 September 2026.

**Method.** Web search only. WebFetch is blocked here, so every external claim rests on search result snippets, not on papers I read. "Not found" means the search returned nothing usable.

**Tags.** [measured]: an instrument on a body or object. [traditional]: asserted by a person or lineage. [proposed]: my recommendation. Two more: [arithmetic] is my own sum, checkable, and [modelled] is a simulation, not a body.

## The short answer

- No measured relationship between nerve path length and a pitch. Not found.
- The body's measured resonances sit at 4 to 30 Hz, and near 1 kHz for the skull. None matches any chakra set.
- Every chakra set is a modern assertion, and they contradict each other.
- Sound measurably moves breathing and heart rate variability, and the evidence says tempo does it, not pitch.
- The build already prints the solfeggio numbers beside nerve plexus names, unlabelled. That breaks the product's own rule today.

## 1. What the traditions claim

**Solfeggio, 396 to 963 Hz.** [traditional] Joseph Puleo, a naturopath, said in the 1970s he found six numbers by reducing verse numbers in Numbers 7 to single digits. Published with Leonard Horowitz in *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse*, 1999. 963 was added later. Musicologists find no link to medieval solfège. It was not a chakra table when published. This is the set in `FLOWSEAT`.

**C to B, 256 to 480 Hz.** [traditional] Root 256, sacral 288, solar 320, heart 341, throat 384, brow 448, crown 480. The C is "scientific pitch", C4 at 256, proposed by Joseph Sauveur in 1713 so every C is a power of two. Jonathan Goldman's vowel work gives crown as B at 480. [arithmetic] It is not one scale: the ratios to 256 are just intonation (9/8, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 15/8) except 448, which is 7/4. A just A is 426.7. Who first attached these to the seats: not found.

**Cosmic octave.** [traditional] Hans Cousto, 1978. [arithmetic, correct as arithmetic] Double an orbital period's frequency until it is audible: one day, doubled 24 times, is 194.18 Hz. The year, doubled 32 times, is 136.10, sold as "om". The chakra assignment in circulation is root 194.18 (day), sacral 210.42 (moon), solar 126.22 (sun), heart 136.10 (year), throat 141.27 (Mercury), brow 221.23 (Venus), crown 172.06 (Platonic year). The sums are real. Planet to seat is assertion.

**Bowls.** [traditional] Crystal chakra sets are sold as C through B, root to crown, at A = 440 or 432. At 440, root C is 261.6, not 256. [measured] Metal bowls are reported at about 110 to 660 Hz with inharmonic overtones near 2.76 and 5.0 times the fundamental, so a single Hz label names only the lowest partial.

**The contradictions.** [arithmetic]

| Seat | Solfeggio | C to B | Cousto |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | 396 | 256 | 194.18 |
| Heart | 639 | 341 | 136.10 |
| Crown | 963 | 480 | 172.06 |

- Root has three answers spread over more than an octave.
- Cousto does not ascend: solar sits nearly nine semitones below sacral, crown below brow.
- Solfeggio root and sacral are 0.89 of a semitone apart. In sequence they sound like one note, slightly out of tune.

## 2. What is measurable

**Conduction.** [measured] A alpha fibres 80 to 120 m/s, C fibres 0.5 to 2 m/s. A speed, not a pitch.

**Resonance,** meaning how much the body amplifies vibration applied to it. [measured]
- Whole body, vertical: about 5 Hz seated, strongest across 4 to 8 Hz; standing 7.5 to 12. ISO 2631 sets its tightest limits there.
- Chest and abdomen 4 to 10 Hz. Spine 10 to 12. Head and neck 16 to 30.
- Eye: about 19 Hz in US Air Force work, 58 to 72 Hz in a later study, varying with eye pressure.
- Skull: first resonance about 1 kHz. Stenfelt measured 1.2 kHz on a dry skull.
- Mouth to chest wall sound transmission peaks near 130 to 143 Hz in one study. [modelled] A thorax simulation gives 28 and 41 Hz.
- A resonance of the sternum, heart or any plexus near a chakra value: not found.

**Hearing and touch.** [measured] Hearing spans about 20 Hz to 20 kHz, most sensitive at 2 to 5 kHz. Skin feels roughly 10 to 1,000 Hz, with Pacinian sensitivity peaking near 250 Hz, where 10 nanometres of movement can be felt. A bowl is felt in the skin it touches, not at a seat.

**The two rhythms sound moves.** [measured]
- Breathing near 6 a minute (0.1 Hz) produces the largest heart rate oscillations, through the baroreflex. The point is lower in taller people. That is the only measured link between body size and a frequency I found, and it is at 0.1 Hz.
- Bernardi, *Heart*, 2006: faster tempo raised breathing, blood pressure and heart rate regardless of style. A pause dropped all three below baseline.
- Bowls: Goldsby 2017 (62 people, observational) found less tension, anger and fatigue. A 2025 review of 14 studies reports higher heart rate variability and lower heart rate.
- A study testing one bowl frequency against another for a seat specific effect: not found.

## 3. The length claim, worked

A standing wave needs a path, a speed and reflecting ends. With both ends alike, f = v / 2L. With one end open, f = v / 4L.

[arithmetic] Soft tissue at 1,540 m/s:

| Path | v / 2L | v / 4L |
|---|---|---|
| 2.00 m | 385 Hz | 192.5 Hz |
| 1.75 m | 440 Hz | 220 Hz |

In bone, at 3,300 to 4,080 m/s, 2 m gives 825 to 1,020 Hz.

- **The inputs choose the answer.** Height, tissue and boundary move it from 192 to 1,166 Hz, two and a half octaves. Every set fits. A 1.75 m person "gives" concert A. A quarter wave on 2 m lands 1.7 Hz from Cousto's earth day. An argument that confirms every set confirms none.
- **The body is not a tube.** It is bone, fat, fluid and air, heavily damped. Its measured resonances are 4 to 12 Hz because they are the skeleton moving on soft tissue, not sound reflecting along its length. At 385 Hz the wavelength in tissue is 4 m, twice the body.
- **At nerve speed nothing resonates.** [arithmetic] A signal crosses 2 m in 17 to 25 ms on A alpha fibres. An action potential regenerates as it travels and does not reflect off the far end. The inverse of a transit time is a number in hertz, not a frequency the nerve has.

What it licenses: a longer vibrating path sounds lower. So root lowest and crown highest can be a rule of the sound language. That is ordering, not measurement. Solfeggio and C to B follow it. Cousto does not.

## 4. Using frequency without lying

**Live now, failing the rule.** `atuned_src/engine/data/practice.js:114-120` holds solfeggio. `ui/mapshelf.js:138` prints "Muladhara · 396 Hz · source 502" and `ui/analytics.js:302` prints "Cranial plexus · 963 Hz". The adjacency to anatomy makes the claim. Raised in `reviews/sound.md`, still open.

**(a) Design vocabulary.** [proposed] One root frequency per seat for earcons and practice tones. Labelled wherever named ("tuning: solfeggio, a modern numerological set"). Never beside a reading. TASKS CF3, a pitch for every address by position, belongs here. Between solfeggio root and sacral those steps are too small to hear.
**Grade: passes, if labelled.** Nothing is measured and nothing claims to be.

**(b) Practice aid.** [proposed] A tone plays and the person reports where they felt it and how strongly, on existing scales. The printed number is their report. The best evidenced form is a breath pacer: a tone that swells and falls near 6 a minute. Tapping each inhale gives a real tapped rate, timed on the device. Rhythm, not pitch, is what the literature supports.
**Grade: passes.** The number is something the person did.

**(c) Measurement.** [proposed] Needs sensors the product lacks: a strap or camera pulse for heart rate variability, a microphone for breath or for the pitch of the person's own hum (a real bodily frequency, measurable locally with Web Audio), a contact accelerometer on the sternum. Each is a new permission, and some a new device. The microphone is sensitive here given the open speech recognition defect in `reviews/sound.md`. Browser support: not searched.
**Grade: fails today.** With sensors it could pass for heart rate variability, breath and voice pitch. None of those is a seat frequency.

## 5. Questions for you

1. **Is the harmonic layer a reading or a language?** A reading claims the body has a frequency at a seat, and nothing backs that. A language gives each seat a tone the way it has a colour, stated as a system. I recommend language.
2. **Which set do you hold, if any?** Solfeggio (Puleo, 1999), C to B (Sauveur's pitch, used by Goldman) or Cousto (1978). They disagree at root by over an octave, and the palette is already derived from solfeggio.
3. **Is "the length of the nervous system" a metaphor for the ascending seats, or a claim about physical length?** As metaphor it holds and gives the sound its one rule. As physics, the arithmetic can produce any number, so it supports none.
4. **Should a seat's tone be heard anywhere, or only in the release?** My default: only in the release, off by default, remembered once on.
5. **Do the four outside addresses have a frequency at all?** No tissue, so no measurement even in principle. A tone outside the seven, like the colour question in CB10, or silence.
6. **Are the bowls you mean ones you own?** A phone recording of each gives their real fundamentals and overtones: the first measured number in this layer, about the bowls rather than you.
7. **May the "396 Hz" lines gain a label or leave the anatomy now,** before any sound is built?
8. **Is a breath pacer in the release the sound you want?** It is the one use the research supports.
