## His three questions, answered for what this round touched

The owner's words, repeated on 24 October: "does everything have a record, is everything in a templated format, is the description from parent to children clear".

- **Does everything have a record?** Every top level part of canon.json now has a row in `REG_MAP` (`atuned_src/engine/registers.js`) saying where it lives in this repository: ported here, held here in this repository's own version, or held out of the product and why. On 24 October that is 18 registers and 7 other sections. The gate fails if the vendored file has a part the map does not place, so a register reboot-os adds later is a named failure on the next merge and never a silent gap.
- **Is everything in a templated format?** The seven ported registers have one template each, and `codesCheck()` holds every row to it: the fields it must carry, of the right kind, with codes that run without a gap. Two places where canon.json breaks its own template are kept as written and held by name, so neither is hidden and neither is rewritten without him: the Dermis row of the organs, whose fourth field is a note where every other organ has a sentence the pattern says in the first person, and four words canon.json puts on two ladders each.
- **Is the description from parent to children clear?** Every ported row names its parent, and the gate holds each link to something this repository holds. A ladder's charge reads as a child emotion, or is refused by a row that says why. A word on a ladder is its ladder and its step. An amplifier stands on a body address. A route runs from a ladder's charge to a body quadrant. A derived measure names its charge and the law it is. The astrology overlay keys only signs, animals and archetypes that have codes, or that are refused by a row that says why.

For the registers this repository already had its own version of, the answer is no, and the decisions at the end are the reason. The registry he asked about on 24 October has a section of its own below: its format has parents and children, and most of its content does not yet.

## The locks, named against this repository

`engine/data/registers.js`'s own header promises that this file names each disagreement between canon.json and this repository. `REG_MAP`'s own row for canon.json's locks (`engine/registers.js:53`) says why that promise was not kept in full for the locks: "some are contradicted here by the arithmetic or the labels, and the plan lists which." Until now the tactics table above only pointed at D3 and D4 for that. This section is the list, canon.json's ten locks (`vendor/reboot-os/canon.json:24` to `:49`), checked one by one.

No earlier write up of canon.json's locks, checked this way, turned up anywhere in this repository. The 23 October records audit (`CANON-RECORD-AUDIT.md`) checked something else, whether a record has one template and a clear parent to child line, and does not name these locks. One of the ten, `divine_term`, already has its answer recorded in code rather than in this file, and is quoted below rather than reasoned out fresh. The rest is a direct check against the files named, run now rather than recovered.

**`chain`.** canon.json's chain runs fetter, then 2+ nodes compound into a saboteur, 2+ saboteurs into a complex, 2+ complexes into a hyper-complex, 2+ hyper-complexes into a super-complex or character, then mask as the seam, then archetype, then the blueprint or domain it lands in, with `chain_operator` holding "two or more at every rung" throughout. This repository's live chain (`TRACE_CHAIN`, `engine/trace.js:111`) carries four of those names: saboteur, complex, hyper_complex, super_complex. The two or more rule holds everywhere that chain runs: a complex compounds two saboteurs of one family and a hyper-complex compounds a family's complexes, by the same file's own words (`engine/trace.js:202` to `:203`). Fetter and character are left out on purpose, by that file's own comment: "the address and the fetter are not here and never will be: both are a pattern", and "Character is no type at all... the whole reading and not one node in it" (`engine/trace.js:104` to `:110`). Mask, archetype and domain are not trace node types at all (`TRACE_NODE_TYPES`, `engine/trace.js:114` to `:116`). So the chain canon.json locks runs eight rungs and this repository's own graph runs four, and the top four, mask, archetype and the domain it lands in, are not wired to the saboteur chain yet. Status: open. Not covered by D3, D4 or any dated ruling found.

**`saboteur_addresses`.** canon.json's lock: "GENERATIVE, never assigned. Bottom-up from fetter scores. ## Strategy

- **One source of truth, and canon.json is an input to it.** Nothing here reads reboot-os at run time. The copy merged against is vendored and pinned by md5, and CODE_SOURCES (the table of where every label comes from) cites that md5. A newer canon.json is merged by running the port tool against it, reading the differences it names, and committing the new copy, its md5 and the changed rows together.
- **His book is the newest statement of his content.** canon.json (8 September, built from v184), the reboot-os app's tables and the registry are copies of his content made at earlier dates. Where they disagree, the newest dated source wins unless he has ruled otherwise, and each case is named here. Wherever it was checked this round, this repository follows the book, except for the four domain names in D8 and the Everyman, which is D6.
- **The registry is kept beside the engine, never inside it.** It is research, not product, and it does not ship. When one of its records is worth a row here, the row is ported by its code, the way the seven registers were, with the registry cited as its source. Nothing reads the registry at run time, and the version brought here is the newest, the 147 record file, not `main`'s 125.
- **Identity first, then data, then content, then interface.** Codes come before tables, tables before what a reading prints, and what a reading prints before the screens that draw it. A screen built on a table whose content is still in dispute would have to be rebuilt after the ruling.
- **Nothing ported moves a number until he rules.** Every ported table is inert, and the gate proves it on every run by scrambling them all and finding `compute()` unchanged.
- **His words are carried as written.** Where canon.json breaks its own template, or breaks a house rule, the row is kept or withheld whole, held by name, and put to him. It is never quietly reworded.
- **Identity never moves.** No stored number or name is renumbered by a merge. Where canon.json numbers or names a thing otherwise, the other number or name becomes a row in the codes' alias table, and every record still resolves.
