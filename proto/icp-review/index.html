The shelf and the Dial, in front of the six

He asked: "simulate this interface with the ICPs and the focus group, see what they say about the shelf and the circular design." Each of the six ICPs on file was loaded with their own stories and shown their own reading, on the shelf prototype and on the Field's Wheel and Dial, at a desk and on a phone. What follows is what each would say, the measurements it rests on, and the one finding that runs through all six.

Read this first. What a simulated panel is, and is not.

It can
  • Hold the design against six different sets of stated needs at once, which a single reviewer cannot. Several findings below came from asking what a given person would see, and were then measured.
  • Name who in the audience a defect costs, so a fix has a reason attached.
  • Point measurement at the right place. Every number on this page was taken off the running prototype by shoot.js and reach.js. Those numbers are evidence. The words around them are not.
It cannot
  • Tell you what a real person feels. Every quotation here is written by one model reading six persona records. Nobody said any of it. Six voices, one author, one set of blind spots.
  • Tell you whether Angela would feel seen, whether anyone comes back tomorrow, or whether the circle feels like a loop. The project's own rule, CL22: "a simulated panel cannot answer whether something feels inspired."
  • Stand in for a sample. These are six constructed people with fixed stories. No percentages of a thousand person panel are given here on purpose, because they would be invented.
  • See motion or hover. The panel saw still captures of real presses.

The tags (BUY, HOLD, CONFUSE, RESIST, REFUSE) are the house set from RESEARCH-icp.md. They are judgement, not a vote. The next real step is five people, twenty minutes each, on their own phone.

The finding across all six

On a phone, the shelf opens on whatever the finger lands on, and the heavier a person's charge, the less of it the finger can land on.

Cause. Not the shelf. On the Wheel, a person's patterns are drawn as beads that sit where their charge is heaviest, and a bead answers a press before the address under it does. At 1,600 wide there is room around them. At 390 each address is about 20 pixels deep and the beads cover it. So the thing a person most needs to press is the thing most covered.

Measured by reach.js, the product's own hit test over each ICP's heaviest address. A clear address reads 71%, not 100, because every segment shares its inner edge with the seat band.

The Dial is worse on the same axis. Pressing each ICP's heaviest mark on the Dial opened the shelf every time, 12 of 12, but the ring that marks the pressed thing came on 0 times of 12. The shelf only knows how to ring a mark on the Wheel's canvas and on the Body figure. On a desk the Dial also fills the stage, so the open shelf covers its right third and whatever was pressed there (Diane, below). On a phone one of six Dial presses, aimed at the centre of James's Escapism bar, opened Addiction.

The move. On a phone, a tap opens the shelf on a short list of everything under the finger, heaviest first, one row each. One extra tap, and it is never wrong. This is also the answer to CZ Q5, still open: "should a tap open the shelf instead of, or as well as, humming, given a finger cannot reliably pick one line there?" Then teach the shelf's ring to read the Dial's own hit list, and pan the Dial clear the way it already pans the Wheel. Cost. One new shelf state, and a second ring source. Grade delta. Heaviest address reachable on a phone: from a median of 8% of its segment to one tap for all six.

What I need from him

1. On a phone, what does a tap on a crowded spot do? Today it opens whatever is drawn on top, usually a pattern, and Derek's heaviest address cannot be reached at all.

Either way. A tap opens the shelf on a short list of everything under the finger, heaviest first: always right, one extra tap. Or the address always wins over the pattern on a phone: one tap, but patterns then have to be reached from the list in the shelf.

2. Should the Dial print the heaviest addresses' names on the picture? James: "I will not open that on a plane."

Either way. Keep the names: the Dial stays the view that names things, and the most private words sit in the largest type. Or show the seat and the weight on the picture, and the name only in the shelf when pressed: private at a glance, one press to read.

3. Should a sex specific address follow the person's sex? The profile records sex at birth. Nothing uses it. Derek is shown a uterovaginal plexus.

Either way. The card reads the profile and names the matching structure where one exists: correct for the person, and the codex gets a second seat for some addresses (BOOK-ERRATA.md territory). Or the seat is renamed to a structure everybody has: one map for everybody, and the codex moves.

4. The Dial's callout for a law prints "Humility, most shut law, 2.1 of 10." Angela walked once already at a moral word with a score. Name the law and drop the score, or keep the score and drop the words "most shut"?

How this was made

shoot.js opens proto/shelf/shelf.html (read only) with no persona, loads each ICP from atuned_src/engine/data/people.js, commits their own lines from sim/stories.js the same way the shelf's own build commits James's, and presses their heaviest address with a real click or tap: on the Wheel, then on the Dial. It writes every capture and shots/facts.json, which carries each card's text, its word and number count, and whether the ring came on. reach.js samples a 17 by 17 grid over each ICP's four heaviest addresses and asks the product's own hit test what answers; it was checked first on an address with nothing over it, which reads 71%, the ceiling. The ages and roles are the ones on file in people.js. The needs quoted are from RESEARCH-icp.md. The reactions are simulated.
