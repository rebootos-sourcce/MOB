The Chakra Band

Every address on screen today wears its seat's colour, so two addresses at opposite ends of the same seat are the same colour. This asks whether a seat can be a band rather than a point, so an address carries its own hex derived from where it sits. The derivation is built and measured here. It works, it costs more than it returns in its current form, and there is a version of it that returns everything asked for. All three are below with their numbers.

What Is Being Asked, And What Came Back

Seat to frequency, frequency to wavelength, wavelength to a colour a screen can show, and then position inside the band. Each step is below with its arithmetic. Three findings first, because they decide what to do with it.

The red end of the spectrum has no colour resolution in sRGB. Everything from nanometres upward converts to exactly #FF0000, which is of the usable window. Root's whole band sits there. The Honest Part

READ THIS BEFORE THE NUMBERS

A chakra has no measured frequency. There is no instrument reading, no spectrum, no published measurement, because the thing being measured is not a physical object that radiates. The seven count and the welding of seats to nerve plexuses are a modern Western reading, which this product's own glossary already states, and that welding dates to 1927.

The frequency table used here is the Solfeggio set: 396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852, 963 hertz. Its source is nameable and it is not a measurement. It comes from Joseph Puleo and Leonard Horowitz, Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse, 1999, derived by numerology from a reading of Numbers 7 verses 12 to 83. It was not a chakra table when it was published. The seven seat mapping was attached by later writers and has no single citable author. I looked for a frequency table with anything behind it beyond assertion and there is not one.

So the band is a stated system, not a measurement, and the colours out of it are internally consistent rather than true. That is worth building. A consistent system beats seven colours somebody liked. It is not a measurement, and this product already says exactly that about the aura and the biophoton field. It says it here in the same voice.

The Derivation, Step By Step

The value of a derived colour is that somebody can follow it. A derivation nobody can follow is a picked colour with extra steps. Every number below is computed, and the pipeline was checked against tabulated CIE values and the sRGB primaries before it was used for anything.

The Bridge That Fails, Shown Rather Than Hidden

The obvious move from an audio frequency to light is octave transposition: double until it reaches the visible range. It does not survive contact. The stated table spans octaves. The usable visible window spans octaves. A wider span cannot fit a narrower one, so at one common transposition three of the seven seats land outside visible light altogether.

Transposing each seat by its own octave count instead keeps them all visible and destroys the ordering, which puts Throat at deep red and Crown at green. Either way the physical bridge is an analogy. Sound and light are different phenomena and an octave equivalence between them is a convention, so the honest move is to state it as one. What follows compresses the stated interval structure onto the visible window and says what that costs.

The Conversion Is Not Free

Of the wavelengths sampled across the usable window, are inside sRGB. Not most. None. Every pure spectral colour lies outside the triangle a screen can reach, so something has to give on every single one. Three strategies were measured against thirteen hue anchors whose colour name is not in dispute.

The second and third strategies return identical results to the last digit, because sliding toward white in chromaticity and adding equal energy white in linear light are the same operation up to a normalisation. There were never three choices, there were two. And the textbook choice loses. Clipping is the crude one and it preserves the thing the derivation exists for, which is hue.

AND HERE IS WHAT CLIPPING COSTS

Clipping pushes every channel it keeps to the edge, so every derived colour arrives at 100 saturation. The standing ruling is a muted palette argued from autonomic response, low to medium saturation. The winning strategy produces the one thing the palette is not allowed to be. That is not a tuning problem, it is the strategy's output. It is the reason hue is the only channel worth taking from this derivation. Sol found it.

Contrast, Against Each Element's Own Ground

Measured against the panel each colour actually sits on, not against the page. This distinction is not academic here: the shipped Root reads 4.81 against the page and 4.17 against the panel, and the panel is where it sits. Both numbers are in this repository, recorded against different grounds, and only one of them is the one that governs.

It Has To Work In Seven Lightings

A derived hue that reads on dark and vanishes on snow has solved half the problem. Each panel below carries its own real ground, composited the way the browser composites it, with the derived seven on it and the worst contrast in that lighting stated underneath.

Lumen is two grounds, not one: paper under the rails and a near black centre stage. A seat colour has to hold on both, which is why it appears twice.

WHY FIVE AND NOT ALL OF THEM

The full ladder returns one distinct colour per seated address with a contrast floor of to one in every lighting, so it clears the bar the derivation could not. It is still the wrong number. Nobody learns that many colours. This repository already carries the measurement that matters here, working memory of about four against the choices per screen it already has. Five steps per seat is a fivefold gain on seven, it is learnable, and every step clears the floor. Mika and Petra, together.

ONE OBJECTION TO THE LADDER, AND IT NEEDS YOUR RULING

Lightness implies rank. A pale swatch and a deep one read as less and more of something. The addresses inside a seat are not ranked, so a ladder states a hierarchy the data does not have. Hue says different without saying more. So the ladder only earns its place if position in the band means something, and nothing in the brief says what high or low in a band is. If it means nothing, this is decoration with a contrast cost. If it means something, say what, and the ladder should run in that direction and be read that way. Petra found it, and she is right to stop here.

What I Would Do With It

Keep the derivation. Do not take colour from it.

It reproduced the shipped hue order independently, and it landed within degrees of the shipped Root and Crown from a completely unrelated argument. That is evidence for the palette and it belongs in the record. As a colour generator it returns fewer than two thirds of the distinct colours hoped for, gives Root none at all, arrives at full saturation against a standing ruling for the opposite, and loses on contrast to the palette it would replace.

If position in the band ships, it ships as value.

Seat hue and saturation unchanged, five steps per seat, the ladder flipping direction on paper lightings. Every step clears 4.5 to one on its own panel in all seven lightings. The seven seat language survives intact, which the icon and colour house rule requires.

Answer one question before any of it is built.

What does high in a band mean. Until that has an answer the ladder encodes a rank that does not exist, and no amount of colour work fixes a scale with nothing on it. That is the only blocker and it is a ruling, not a build.

And the field anchors need an answer too.

Four addresses sit above and below the body with no seat. Any band scheme has to say what they wear. Today they take an accent. Under a band scheme they are the only addresses with no band, which makes them the visual exception rather than the anchors they are.
