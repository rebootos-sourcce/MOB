Atuned tutorial. Storyboard, 27 September. First draft, never built before

Your avatar in the middle, one full turn of the loop around it, done on your own story

The tutorial is for a person who has already been through onboarding and now needs to know how the instrument works. It teaches by doing, inside the real screens, one thing lit at a time. The only progress marker is the loop itself, closing around the avatar in the centre ball you asked to keep.

Every mechanic on these frames is one that ships today, with the file it lives in named on the frame. Nothing is invented. Where a ruling of yours is needed before it can be built, the frame carries a yellow chip and the question is written out in full at the bottom. Every line a person reads is written to your rule from today: as if they are ten, and never abstract.

Your ten year old rule, applied

Where plain words and precision pulled apart

Every tutorial line passed the house voice gate, check.py --line, with no hard failures. Whether a ten year old follows it was read by hand, out loud. These are the places the two rules disagreed, and what was chosen.

"Held" is the one product word the tutorial teaches. The rest of the product uses it everywhere, so the tutorial says it once, on the wheel, in plain words: "Held means it is heavy enough to show in full." Everything else is described, not named.

"Imprint" and "pattern" are not said. The bank's own label says "every imprint you hold", and the release counts "patterns". Both are hard words, and pattern means two things to a new person: a habit, and a unit the release is paid in. The tutorial says "everything your writing found" and shows the number on the Patterns box without naming it. Cost: the person learns the box by looking, not by being told.

The body names stay. "Throat", yes. "Vagus nerve" is there because the shipped card prints it, and a ten year old does not know it. It is data the instrument reads, not a sentence the tutorial writes.

The book's release order is in the caption, not the copy. Release lines on the left side, then the right, then the true lines on each side. Precise, and too much to read while doing it. The person reads one line at a time and is told one thing: keep some attention on your throat.

"Active Listening" and "No labelling" are the engine's own words for the practice. "No labelling" is abstract. It is data from the practice table, so it is kept and flagged for the copy seat rather than rewritten here.

Your loop words are kept. discover, play, flow, embody. Ruled, and yours.

The questions, each with the thing it is about

None is answered for you. Each one is a real gap: two of your rulings that pull against each other, or a ruling that was never turned into a design. Where it is about a screen, the screens are drawn.

A

Does the tutorial's release change anything?

"The tutorial lives in the profile, toggleable and replayable. It does not spend real charge."Your ruling, round AN5, 19 September.

"The avatar is the centrepiece... A person watches their avatar improve and the improvement is driven by releases and by going through the loop."Your ruling, 20 September, in CLAUDE.md. And backlog item A7: "Find a pattern, release it, watch it change."

Both are yours. A release that changes nothing cannot show the avatar changing, so the tutorial cannot do both. The storyboard follows AN5, because it is the ruling on the tutorial itself, and draws the other two here.

1Practice, as drawn

Nothing is spent and nothing moves. Cost: the turn ends with the avatar exactly as it started, and Derek and Sofia read it as a demo.

2Real, and small

A short real run, about five patterns out of the hundred a person gets once. The avatar shows "releasing" and the vault reads 1. Cost: it reverses AN5, and it spends a little of their allowance on a lesson.

3The last chapter waits

Practice in the tutorial, and the closing chapter opens the first time they run a real release on their own. Cost: the turn does not close in one sitting.

B

When does the tutorial first run?

"The tutorial turns off once seen."Your brief for onboarding and the tutorial. Nothing says when it is first seen.

1Straight after onboarding

One sitting, start to finish. Cost: about eight minutes of guided doing on day one. NN/g found people who sat through a tour rated the app harder to use than people who skipped it.

2Day two, at their when

It meets them on their return, at the time they chose on the embody screen. Day two is the ritual, by your ruling, so this is the ritual's first job. Cost: whoever does not come back never sees it.

3Only when asked

From Account, Run the tutorial now. Cost: almost nobody finds it. This is where the shipped onboarding sits today, switched off by default.

C

Does the tutorial grow the bar?

"The bar grows as they earn it."Your ruling, round AN8, 19 September. Not built. The locked state was never designed, and your standing rule says a control is never hidden without a sign of it.

1Each chapter opens its tab

The bar starts with the avatar and the three loop tabs, and the other five show as shut, each saying what opens it. Cost: a new locked state on the bar, and five lines of "what opens this".

2The bar is whole from the start

The tutorial does not touch the bar. Cost: AN8 stays unbuilt, and a stranger meets nine tabs at once.

D

"Touching every screen": inside the tutorial, or when each screen first has something of theirs?

"Day one done is touching every screen and running that loop once."Round AN3. The loop passes through the Avatar, Story, Field and Ritual tabs. Body, Compass, Knowledge, Games and Summary are not on it.

1A second lap, in the tutorial

Five more stops, one press each. Cost: five screens in a row with nothing to do on them, which is the card deck tour that measures worse than none.

2The outer ring, as drawn on U13

Each of the five lights once, the first time it has something of theirs to show. Cost: "every screen" is not done on day one for most people. Games and Knowledge may wait weeks.

E

What does the turn end on?

"A badge and points at the end."Round AN3. The word for the reward has been open since 20 September: marks (what ships), badges, patterns, points or karma.

U12 draws an empty slot where the reward goes. It cannot be labelled until the word is chosen. Either way: a word now and U12 is finished; no word yet and the tutorial ships with the slot empty, which is honest and flat.

F

The 63 questions: does the tutorial say how many and how long, and does it carry what the removed panels said?

From 19 September, question 7, never answered: "Does the product say sixty three and fifteen minutes up front, or does it let a person start and find out." And round IF, 27 September: the questions page's three explanatory panels were cut under your rule against text that explains a screen; completion ran twenty nine points higher with them present.

1Say it, in the tutorial, once

One tutorial line on the questions page: "63 questions. About fifteen minutes. Each one is asked three ways." It is gone when the tutorial ends, which is the "we have overlays for all of it" in your ruling. Cost: a person who skips the tutorial is never told.

2Say it on the page

One line, always there. The panel records put completion about thirty points higher with the length stated. Cost: text on a screen, against your rule.

3Say nothing

The page stays clean. Cost: people drop out part way, after the work, which is the most expensive place to lose them.

G

One line each, all still open

G1. Which number does the avatar lead with? CQ, expression, or both (open since rounds EG and EQ). The tutorial's first and last frames sit on the avatar, so they show whichever you pick. Drawn here with no number.

G2. Is the tutorial the overlay your rule points to? Your ruling against explaining text ends "we have overlays for all of it". The storyboard reads the tutorial's ring as that overlay: lines while it runs, none after. If you meant something else, every tutorial line needs a new home.

G3. Can the tutorial be left part way and picked up again? Drawn as yes, the same as the opening: the ring keeps the quarters already closed.

What to measure, so the next round can tell if it worked

Kept on the record as dated flags, nothing sent

1. Whether the second avatar line found a place in the body, and how many tries it took.

2. Leave, by chapter. The one to watch is U2, James's stop.

3. Whether the back arrow was ever pressed after U4. It is the only way to undo, and nobody has been shown it before.

4. Whether a real release follows the tutorial within 24 hours. This is the number that says whether the tutorial taught the loop.

5. Day two return at the chosen when, for people who did the tutorial and people who skipped it, side by side. NN/g found skippers can be the happier group.

6. Replays from Account. A replayed tutorial is either valued or confusing, and the chapter replayed says which.

Then five real people, already onboarded, watched at phone width.
