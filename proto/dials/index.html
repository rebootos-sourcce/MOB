Two dials, one design

Balance and orientation read the same kind of thing and are drawn in two shapes, so a person learns the shape twice. Both are a signed position on an axis with two named poles. Every figure below is computed off ../../engine.js at run time against the roster, so nothing on this page is a number somebody typed.

What each one reads today

Orientation is leanRead(r) at engine/verp.js:495. With no story scanned it returns 100 - r.malig, and malig is CQ>=50 ? 0 : (50-CQ)/50*100 at engine/compute.js:188, so above coherence fifty the dial has nowhere left to go. Balance is balance() at engine/compute.js:29, four outward axes meaned against five inward. The swing column is the spread of mirrorAt() across the eight compass axes, which is the only oscillation the engine can already compute.

One design, used twice

Same object both times. A thin line with a break at the centre, a ring mark at each end, a figure over the origin with a pill under it naming the side, a colour bar out of the break toward that side, and the swing as an interval above the line. The line is the axis: even at the centre, a hundred at either pole. The figure is the distance from even, so orientation is restated as a lean rather than as a share, which is the step that lets one shape carry both.

And one thing that is yours to rule

The figure above is the field's own orientation, 100 - 2 × malig, which is the reading the dial prints today. It pegs at a hundred for everybody at coherence fifty or over, which is why it disagrees with the swing so often. The same drawing takes a figure that cannot peg: the mean of the eight compass axes, on the same axis the swing is already measured on. Then the figure always sits inside the swing and the two never fight. It is a different measurement, so it is your call.
