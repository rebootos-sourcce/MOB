The Armature

A body reduced to the parts that carry a reading, and three channels that cannot be confused. The line work is the record and it only ever completes. The outer dotted ring at each seat is the reach, and the pegs move it under your hand. The grey calipers are today, and they are allowed to come back. Nothing on the graphic is text and nothing on it is a score.

What This Refuses To Draw

No face. No mass, no body type, no weight, no sex, no age, no clothing and no accessory of any kind. It refuses to draw a person who could be better looking, which is the one thing this product must never imply. It refuses to print the coherence number anywhere on the figure. And it refuses to let a peg move the figure: a peg moves only the dotted ring, so a claim you have not earned buys a reach you will not travel to, and the gap is drawn.

The Symbol Argument

An armature is what a figure is built on. It is a thing you add to, which is the correct object for a record. The body is not a metaphor here: every one of the 112 addresses already carries a named plexus and a location, the seat heights come straight out of PMBANDS in engine/data/practice.js, and a body chart is the clinically validated form for somatic self report rather than a decorative choice. The broken segment is the load bearing part of the argument. A drawing that is not finished is not a damaged drawing, and a technical elevation with parts still dashed reads as work in hand.

Not Bulky, And Nothing To Be Taught

Bulky is ink without reading. Four kinds of mark carry four readings and there is no fifth: line work is the record, the dotted ring is the reach, the solid ring is where the seat stands, the grey ticks are what is carrying. No mark is drawn twice, no mark is filled, and the heaviest stroke on the figure is 2.2 pixels. Nothing to be taught is the harder test and this is the only one of the three that passes it cold: a person knows what a body is, knows that a dashed line is not finished, and knows that something pressing in from outside is pressure. The one sentence anybody needs is the caption under the figure, and if the caption were removed the drawing would still be read roughly right.

The Honest Risk

A standing human figure invites identification with body shape however hard the drawing refuses it, and a seat drawn dark on a body reads as a statement about an organ. The mitigation is that the dark channel is grey calipers outside the ring rather than a dimming of the body, so the figure is never drawn as less than itself. That is a mitigation and not a proof, and it is the thing to test on a real person at grid level two before this ships.

Where Every Number Comes From
