Tracking and the two letter pair collisions, drawn at the sizes the mark is actually used at, with the gap stated in pixels and in em for every state. Then the favicon as a band of the seven seats, at sixteen, thirty two and one hundred and eighty pixels, in both rhythms. Then the dots on paper. Round 2: the nine cuts The first study

1. The tightest joint in this mark is one pixel, and in the top bar two of them are zero

His words: “the spacing is very important. So touching, not touching, these things are very critical.” So the first thing measured was whether the mark as it stands is touching. It is.

Two numbers per joint, because they answer two different questions. Solid counts pixels the rasteriser filled past half opacity, which is ink a person reads as ink. Any counts any ink at all, including the antialiased edge. When the second one reaches zero the two letters are touching in the render whatever the geometry says, because there is no pixel of ground between them.

In the top bar both the t to u joint and the e to d joint measure zero pixels of ground. They are touching.

The geometry is not wrong. The joint was solved by measured background area against a target of 3400 square units with a floor of 9 units, and the floor is what the e to d joint is sitting on. Nine units is 0.0692 em, which is 2.22 pixels in the funnel heading, 1.8 on the boot card and 0.9 in the top bar. A gap of nine tenths of a pixel has nowhere to land: the rasteriser puts half of it on one letter and half on the other and the ground never appears.

3. Touching and not touching, as two states of one cut

Two pairs in this mark decide when it closes, and they are not the two the eye picks. The t and the u look tight because a crossbar and a stem leave a tall narrow slot. The e and the d look open because the e’s aperture puts white on the wrong side of the joint. Measured, the e to d joint is the tightest in the mark at every tracking and it is the first to close.

The state column names the whole mark’s tracking. What this particular pair is doing at that tracking is the gap column beside it, which is the point: one knob, and the five joints reach zero at five different settings.

4. The favicon. It is the ring, and the ring is a band of seven

His words: “for the favicon, let’s do like the old ring. Seven colours in a band.” And on the rhythm: “either one, one, one, one, one, one, or one, two, one, two, one, two.”

That settles the thing the first study spent a section arguing. Every cut of the u with its two dots reads as a face at sixteen pixels, and the answer was to put the a there instead. With the ring, neither letter goes to sixteen pixels and the face is not a problem to solve. Also worth saying plainly: there is no favicon in the product today at all, so this is a build and not a swap.

The seven colours, taken off the live tokens

Not off the palette written in CLAUDE.md, because the two disagree and the code is the one that ships. The seven below are PAL in engine/data/canon.js, and the same seven are written a second time on .b-s1 to .b-s7 in shell/head.html, which is how the boot ring draws them now.

And what the document says, which is a different set:

Root #C4635ESacral #D19255Solar #D4BC70Heart #6FC5A3Throat #65B8D43rd Eye #8296DBCrown #A98BCE

Seven values, seven disagreements, and the drift is not small: Root is #D6524C in the code against #C4635E in the document. The ring below is drawn from the code. Which set is canonical is a question and it is at the end of this sheet.

Where the seam goes, and which way the band runs

Root at the bottom, climbing the right side to Crown, so the band is the spine closed into a loop and the loop closes at the body’s base. That is the project’s own ruling about the four part loop applied to the seven: it is a circle, never a list, and a list has an end.

The two rhythms he named, drawn, and the thing seven does to the second one

An alternating rhythm cannot close on an odd count. One, two, one, two round a ring of seven leaves two slots of the same weight meeting at the seam, and that is arithmetic rather than taste. Both readings are drawn and both are measured, and the seam is named in each.

Touching or not touching, at the favicon sizes

The same question as the joints above, asked of the arcs. A three degree gap between arcs costs the band 2 of its 12 solid pixels per arc at sixteen, which is 16.67 per cent, and 137 of 2157 at a hundred and eighty, which is 6.35 per cent.

At sixteen the arcs have to touch. A gap there is not a gap, it is a seat losing a fifth of the pixels it had to say its hue with. At one hundred and eighty a gap costs six per cent and is affordable, but a favicon that gaps at one size and butts at another is two drawings, and the sixteen is the one that sets the floor.

The band weight, and the one thing the old ring cannot lend

The boot mark’s ring is a hairline: stroke 1.6 on a circle of radius 62 in a 200 box, which is 1.29 per cent of its diameter. Scaled to sixteen pixels that is 0.21 of a pixel and there is nothing to colour. So the band takes its weight from the sixteen pixel end rather than from the boot mark: three pixels on sixteen, which is 18.75 per cent of the diameter, held at every size. At two pixels the arcs drop to 8 solid pixels each against 12, measured, which is the reason three is the floor and not two.

The boot mark’s core dot is 18 units across in a 200 unit box, 9 per cent of it. At sixteen pixels that is 1.44 pixels across, which is one pixel and a bit of another. It is not in the favicon, and that is a measurement rather than a preference.

Which palette the ring takes, since a favicon has no lighting

The tab strip is the browser’s ground and the product does not own it, so the ring cannot follow the seven lightings the way the wordmark does. It gets one palette, and that palette has two jobs at once: the whole band has to separate from an unknown ground, and the seven arcs have to separate from each other, because in a band they touch. At sixteen pixels each arc is about three pixels of arc length, so if two neighbours are close the antialiasing between them closes the difference entirely.

PAL_LIGHT is out on the measurement, and it is the one that looked safest. Its closest two neighbours are 20 units apart in rgb, against 47 for PAL and 61 for PAL_VIVID. At three pixels of arc that is not two colours, it is one colour with a slight change of mind, and the tile above shows it: the Snow palette ring at sixteen reads as a brown circle.

Between the other two, PAL_VIVID wins on both numbers: 2.35 to 1 worst case against an unknown ground where PAL is 1.62, and 61 units of neighbour separation against 47. PAL’s weak point is Solar, a light warm yellow that all but disappears on a light tab.

And there is an argument in the source that decides it. canon.js says of PAL_VIVID: full chroma, chosen to hold on paper and on the black stage both. That is the favicon’s exact problem, written down before the favicon existed. The palette that was made for an unknown ground is the one that should go on the one surface the product does not own.

The tension, named rather than buried: the house rule is a muted palette argued from autonomic response, and PAL_VIVID is the opposite of muted. The defence is that the rule governs the surfaces a person sits in front of for twenty minutes, and a favicon is a sixteen pixel identifier in somebody else’s chrome that has to be found at a glance. Different job, different instrument. It is still his call and it is at the end of this sheet.

What is recommended, and why

Equal arcs, touching, one drawing at all three sizes, Root at the bottom climbing the right side, and PAL_VIVID rather than PAL on the palette measurement above.

  • Because seven cannot alternate around a closed ring. The rhythm he asked for needs an even count. On seven it leaves 130.9 degrees of one weight meeting at the seam one way and 72 the other, and whichever way it is turned the ring has one place where the pattern restarts.
  • Because the seats are equal in the instrument. Giving three of them twice the arc says three seats weigh more, which the engine does not say. A colour system that contradicts the model it is drawn from is a colour system telling a lie quietly.
  • Because of the sixteen pixel end. Equal gives every seat 12 solid pixels. The alternating readings give the narrow seats 10 and 8, and at that count a seat is a coloured dot whose hue the antialiasing drags toward its neighbours.

If he wants the rhythm anyway, and it is his to want, the reading to take is two, one, two, one, two, one, two: the odd seat takes the wide slot, so Root and Crown are both wide and the seam falls between two wide arcs, which reads as a deliberate base to the ring rather than as a stutter. The narrow seats measure 10 solid pixels at sixteen against 8 the other way, so it is also the better of the two on the count.

Petra Nikau on the seam and on the equality of the seats. Sol Amadi on the pixel count and on what antialiasing does to a hue with three pixels to say it in.

5. The dots on paper. One rule, seven answers

His words: “when the white dots are on paper, make them dark grey.”

So the light inverts rather than disappearing. On the four dark grounds the dots are the brightest thing in the field. On the three paper grounds they are the darkest. That is one rule and it is the right one, and the only thing left to decide is which grey, which is a number rather than a taste.

The grey is derived, not picked

On the dark grounds the dot is pure white and the letter is the sky, and the separation between them is 2.17 to 1. That number is what makes the dots read as a mechanism sitting on the letter rather than as part of it. Inverting the light means keeping that separation and changing its sign, so the grey is the value that sits the same distance below the paper sky as white sits above the dark sky.

Taken: #3A3733. Its separation from the paper sky is 2.12 to 1 against the 2.17 the white has on the dark grounds, which is a match to within 0.05.

Against the three paper grounds it measures 11.04, 11.53, 11.84 on the bars and 9.94, 9.56, 11.84 on the pages. The lowest of the six is 9.56 to 1, which clears seven to one on a mark that is 1.9 pixels across in the top bar and needs the headroom.

It is warm by three points of red over green and eight over blue, which is not a decorative choice: the three paper grounds are warm off whites at #F8F7F3, #FCFCFB, #FFFFFF, and a neutral grey on a warm paper reads cold and foreign. A trace of the paper’s own temperature makes it read as ink on that paper.

And there is a grey that makes the dots disappear. At #6B6863 the dot and the paper sky are the same luminance, 1.01 to 1, so the dots stop being an event on the letter and become a slightly different hue of it. That is the hard floor under the word dark: anything lighter than about #6B6863 is not a dark grey for this purpose, whatever it is called.

6. What is his. Three questions from this sheet, and each one has a seat on it

Does the mark get one tracking, or one for the boot card and a looser one for the top bar?

As it stands, in the top bar the e to d joint and the t to u joint both measure zero pixels of ground. Plus six units of tracking gives every joint a pixel and costs 4.6 per cent of the mark’s width. Two trackings is two drawings to keep in agreement. One loose tracking is a mark that is visibly loose on the boot card, where there is room for it and nothing forcing it.

Bjorn Haraldsson, type and grid
Should the e and the d be allowed to touch on purpose at the small end, as a ligature, rather than be pushed apart?

They are touching now, by accident, and a joint that closes by accident reads as a defect. The same joint closed on purpose, with the two letters meeting at a stated point rather than merging in the antialiasing, is a decision somebody made and it reads as one. It would be the only ligature in the mark.

Petra Nikau, composition and symbol, against Bjorn Haraldsson, who wants the tracking
Which seven hex values are canonical, the ones in the code or the ones in CLAUDE.md?

They disagree on all seven. The code has Root at #D6524C and the document has #C4635E, and the same spread runs through the other six. The favicon, the boot ring, the key strip and every seat ring in the product are drawn from the code. The document is what a new seat reads first. One of the two has to move and it is not a design decision, it is a decision about which file is the source.
