The nine cuts, two rounds each

Nine cuts, two rounds each, and a stated floor every tile on this page clears. A round here is a change argued from what the last round measured, not a second sheet of the same thing. Every number is read off a Chromium raster of the same path data, and every actual size tile is that raster rather than a scaled drawing. Round 3: spacing, the favicon, the dots on paper The first study

1. Why D11 could not be told apart, and what that costs the whole study

His words: “D11 is not varied enough for me to tell.” He is right and it is the study’s fault, so it is measured rather than argued.

The d row offered a full ascender against an ascender cut four units shorter. Four units out of a 130 unit ascender box. Rasterised at the three sizes the mark is actually used at, that change moves 1 pixel in the top bar, 4 pixels on the boot card and 5 in the funnel heading, out of a mark that carries about 56 pixels of ink in the bar. A sheet that asks a person to rule on one pixel is asking for a coin toss and calling it a decision.

So this sheet holds to a stated floor, and every tile prints its number against it. A change ships as a choice only if it moves at least 8 pixels and the changed region measures at least 3 pixels in its longest dimension, at one times, at the size it is shown at. Below that the cut is still drawn, at eight times, with the number attached and marked refused. It is not offered as a choice.

Running that floor against all nine cuts turned up something larger than the d row, and it is the finding of this sheet. Read the last column.
