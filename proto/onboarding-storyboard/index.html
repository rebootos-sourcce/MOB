What is on the screen, and what is said over it

Your words, turned into ten steps. The screen carries a symbol; the voice carries the instruction. About two minutes, and the seven colour ring round the edge fills as it goes, so the person always sees how far along they are without a number.

Outside, what was looked at and what was taken

Borrowed moves, not borrowed looks

Apple Watch Breathe, and the Headspace breathing circle. A shape that grows and shrinks is followed without a word. Taken: the core breathes, four seconds in and six out. CSS-Tricks on the Breathe animation, a design read of Headspace.
Calm's first screen. It opens on one breath before it asks for anything, and the ask lands on a calmer person. Taken: the signal test comes before discover asks for a story. GoodUX on Calm's new user flow.
Bodily maps of emotions, Nummenmaa and others, PNAS 2014. 701 people coloured a body outline where a word or a film was felt, and the maps held across cultures. Taken: tap the outline where it lands, and let the answer be a place. PNAS.
Body map tools for felt sense. Pick a sensation, tap where it is. Taken, and cut down: no list of sensation words is offered, three glyphs instead, so nothing is suggested to the person. an interactive body map, Tangible Body Maps, TEI 2021.
Journey, thatgamecompany. No words, no instructions, and people still know what to do because the world shows them. Taken: the only large words are the two the person is thinking. Journey.
Where they stop

Four people, the new two minutes

Angela, 36, seeker.The signal test is the thing she came for, and she feels yes and no land in different places. Stops only if her tap does not light anything, which is why every tap lights the nearest seat.
Derek, 39, high performer.Two minutes with his eyes closed is where he checks whether this is a meditation app. Stops at "Stop" unless the ring shows him it ends. The seven colour timer is for him.
James, 57, C suite.Will not say a word aloud, and does not have to: every step works with a tap and silence. Stops at the bad day thoughts if they read as his. They drift onto the figure, not onto him.
Marcus, 44, creative director.Reads the drawing before anything else. Two words on the whole test is the proof it is not a brochure. No forced stop.
Yours to rule

Two new questions, then the ones still open from this morning

M

With the words off the screen, who says the instructions?

"We are visual only, so find the symbolic language to convey this."Today. The signal test still needs "think yes ten times" said somehow, and the screen no longer says it.

1A voice

Eyes can close, which the exercise wants. Cost: the voice must live inside the one file. The device's own voice is free and sounds like a machine; a recorded voice (the ElevenLabs route from the hosting notes) is about 1 MB for two minutes.

2One caption line at the bottom

Works with the sound off, which is most phones in public. Cost: words are back on the screen, one line at a time.

3Symbols only

Your rule to the letter. Cost: "think the word" and "where does it land" have no symbol a person reads the same way twice. Five people would have to be watched trying it.

What to measure

So the next round can tell whether the drawing worked

1. Did the tap land. Share of people who placed a mark for yes, and for no. A symbol nobody acts on is a symbol nobody read.
2. Did the three glyphs get answered. Moves, warm or cool, which way, each on its own. A glyph left on its dash tells which symbol failed.
3. Different places. How often yes and no land on different seats. That is the moment the test exists for.
4. Leave, by step. Especially at Stop, Derek's step, and at the bad day thoughts, James's.
5. Voice on or off, once question M is answered, against finishing the test.
6. Came back on day two within an hour of the chosen when. The number the whole turn exists to move.
Then five strangers at phone width, watched, with the sound off and then on.

The grade. This morning's storyboard: B plus, judgement. This one: A minus, judgement.
