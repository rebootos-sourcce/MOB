## Deriving the stem

The release stem for this deck concatenates both intensity ladders:

> *"I'm letting go of believing, perceiving, thinking, behaving, acting,
> feeling, speaking, and saying that I am tense, stressed, bothered, irritated,
> annoyed, frustrated, angry, mad, pissed, furious, enraged, concerned, worried,
> nervous, anxious, scared, afraid, terrified, horrified, paralyzed, that I am..."*

Three rules from the corpus decide the counterpart:

1. The Subject decks pair `I am letting go of…` with `I am embracing the truth that I am…`
2. The Little Books invert the fetter *literally*, term for term
3. A truth always replaces — never leaves a void

So the truth stem inverts both ladders, rung for rung, in order:

| | anger ladder | → | inverted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–11 | tense · stressed · bothered · irritated · annoyed · frustrated · angry · mad · pissed · furious · enraged | → | relaxed · at ease · untroubled · settled · patient · flowing · calm · clear · composed · steady · peaceful |

| | fear ladder | → | inverted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12–20 | concerned · worried · nervous · anxious · scared · afraid · terrified · horrified · paralyzed | → | assured · trusting · easy · quiet · brave · fearless · grounded · unshaken · moving |

**Derived stem:**

> *"I am embracing the truth that I am relaxed, at ease, untroubled, settled,
> patient, flowing, calm, clear, composed, steady, peaceful, assured, trusting,
> easy, quiet, brave, fearless, grounded, unshaken, moving, that I am..."*

## Where this could be wrong

**The stem is invented.** Nothing in the corpus authorises a truth stem that
carries an inverted ladder. The alternative is to reuse the Subject decks' plain
`I am embracing the truth that I am…` and let the fragments carry all the work.
That would be more conservative and less symmetrical. Your call.

**Two inversions are weak.** `nervous → easy` and `horrified → unshaken` were
the hardest rungs; neither is as clean as `tense → relaxed`. If the ladder gets
used, those two want better words.

**I broke one of your rules on purpose.** Fragment 20 reads *"Hopeful, and
already finding balance"* rather than a flat inversion of *"Hopeless that I'll
ever feel truly balanced."* A pure inversion — *"Hopeful that I'll one day feel
truly balanced"* — keeps the state in the future, which is the thing the deck is
trying to release. Every other truth in the corpus puts the state in the present
tense. I chose the rule over the mirror. Flag it if you disagree.

**Register check.** These sit in the teaching voice, not the field-note or
narrative voice. They are 5–14 words against a Subject-deck mean of 8.58 — a
little longer, because the escalation fragments they answer are longer than
Subject fragments.

**What I did not do.** I did not soften the deck. The release fragments in this
corpus are blunt, and `Limiting Belies` suggests the private register is blunter
still. These truths are plain rather than gentle, because the fetters are plain
rather than cruel.
