## R1 · PUBLISHING DIRECT, NOT LICENSING
**Ruled by Lance, 2026-09-15.** Overrides the recommendation in
`03_REVENUE.md` section 3.

> *Licensing seems like a stretch. And a much harder ask. Not to say it's not
> worth pursuing. But I think publishing direct allows us to just get the
> stuff into the hands of the people who need it. Without the gatekeepers.*

**What this means operationally.** The constraint moves. It is no longer
*find ten practitioners*. It is **build a top of funnel from near zero**,
and that is now the first job, not the third.

The data underneath the recommendation does not change and is not in
dispute. Ten organic subscribers in six months. 86% delivery. Opens halved
in nine weeks. Those are the conditions the acquisition work has to beat.

**The mission argument is the deciding one and it is sound.** A practitioner
tier puts a credentialing body between the work and the person who needs it.
Direct does not.

**Kept from the licensing thesis, without the business model.** A
practitioner-priced SKU needs no gatekeeper. It is a higher-priced product
for a professional buyer, sold the same way as everything else. That stays on
the ladder. It is a price point, not a pivot.
