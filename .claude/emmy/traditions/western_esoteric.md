## The frame: what "Western esotericism" means to scholars

Western esotericism is now an academic field (University of Amsterdam, Sorbonne, Exeter). Two names matter:

- **Antoine Faivre** defined it as a *form of thought* with four core traits and two secondary ones. Core: (1) **correspondences**, everything in the visible and invisible universe mirrors everything else; (2) **living nature**, the cosmos is alive and ensouled; (3) **imagination and mediations**, inner vision and intermediaries (angels, spirits, symbols) give access to higher worlds; (4) **transmutation**, the knower is changed by knowing. Secondary: (5) **concordance**, all traditions share one hidden truth; (6) **transmission**, knowledge passes from master to disciple through initiation.
- **Wouter Hanegraaff** argues the field is better understood as a historical category, the body of "rejected knowledge" that mainstream religion and science pushed aside, and reads Faivre's traits as an "enchanted" worldview set against Weber's disenchantment.

These six traits are a good lens for Emmy. Every tradition below uses most of them.

## 1. Hermeticism

### 1.1 Origin and history

- **Hermes Trismegistus** ("thrice-greatest Hermes") is not a historical person. He is a Greco-Egyptian figure formed in Ptolemaic and Roman Egypt by joining the Greek god Hermes with the Egyptian god Thoth, the god of writing, measure and wisdom. Texts were written in his name.
- **The Hermetica** are Greek texts composed in Roman Egypt, roughly 1st to 3rd century CE (sources give 100-300 CE). Scholars split them into two families: the **technical Hermetica** (astrology, medicine and botany, alchemy, magic) and the **philosophical or religious Hermetica** (dialogues on God, mind and salvation).
- **The Corpus Hermeticum** is a Byzantine collection of the philosophical treatises, numbered I to XVIII with no XV, so seventeen treatises. Treatise I is the **Poimandres**. Related texts: the Latin **Asclepius**, excerpts preserved by Stobaeus, and Coptic Hermetic texts found at Nag Hammadi in 1945, including the *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* [unverified].
- **Renaissance revival.** Marsilio Ficino translated the Corpus into Latin for Cosimo de' Medici (translation finished 1463, printed 1471); Lodovico Lazzarelli translated the last treatises (printed 1507). Renaissance readers believed Hermes was an ancient sage near the time of Moses, a "first theology" (prisca theologia) behind Plato and Christianity.
- **1614.** Isaac Casaubon showed from the Greek vocabulary that the philosophical treatises were written in the Christian era, not in ancient pharaonic Egypt. Modern scholars keep the late date but now see more genuine Egyptian continuity than Casaubon allowed.
- **Recent scholarship.** Garth Fowden (*The Egyptian Hermes*, 1986), Brian Copenhaver (English translation, 1992), and Wouter Hanegraaff (*Hermetic Spirituality and the Historical Imagination*, Cambridge 2022). Hanegraaff argues the treatises record a practical training: altered states, visions, spiritual rebirth and union with the divine, with "radical nonduality" at its core, not only philosophy.
- **The Emerald Tablet** is a short alchemical text attributed to Hermes. Its oldest known version is Arabic, inside the *Kitab Sirr al-Khaliqa* (Book of the Secret of Creation), attributed falsely to Balinas, the Arabic name for Apollonius of Tyana. Dates proposed range from late antiquity to the early 9th century; the most common estimate is late 8th or early 9th century, finished by the reign of al-Ma'mun (813-833). It also appears in a text of the Jabir corpus. No Greek original has been found. It entered Latin in the 12th century (Hugo of Santalla among the translators). Isaac Newton made his own English translation, now among his alchemical papers at King's College, Cambridge.
- **The Kybalion.** Published 1908 in Chicago by the Yogi Publication Society under the name "Three Initiates." It is generally attributed to **William Walker Atkinson** (1862-1932), a New Thought writer who used many pen names (including Yogi Ramacharaka) and ran that publishing house. Mitch Horowitz reports that Atkinson acknowledged sole authorship in a 1912 *Who's Who in America* entry; scholars (Deslippe, Chapel, Smoley, Horowitz) treat it as a **New Thought** book of the early 1900s, not an ancient text. Its seven principles do not appear as a set in any ancient Hermetic writing. Atkinson also wrote *Thought Vibration, or the Law of Attraction in the Thought World* (1906) [unverified].

### 1.2 Core structure

**The philosophical Hermetica, key pieces**
- **Poimandres (CH I).** Hermes has a vision of Poimandres, "the Mind (Nous) of sovereignty." God is Mind and Light. The primal Human (Anthropos) is made in God's image, looks down through the seven planetary spheres, falls in love with Nature and becomes embodied, so humans are double: mortal in body, immortal in essence. Salvation is the reverse journey.
- **The ascent (CH I, end).** At death the soul rises through seven zones (read by scholars as the planetary spheres from the Moon outward) and hands back at each one the energy it took on during its descent: increase and decrease; evil scheming; the deceit of desire; the arrogance of ruling; unholy daring and rash boldness; evil striving for wealth; lying falsehood. Freed, it reaches the **Eighth** (the Ogdoad) and then enters God [wording from memory of the Copenhaver translation; unverified].
- **Rebirth (CH XIII).** A master-disciple initiation. Twelve "irrational tormentors" of matter (tied to the twelve signs of the zodiac) are driven out by ten divine powers. Tormentors: ignorance, grief, incontinence, lust, injustice, greed, deceit, envy, treachery, anger, recklessness, malice. Powers: knowledge of God, joy, self-control, steadfastness, justice, generosity, truth, the good, life, light [lists from memory of the Copenhaver translation; unverified].
- **CH XI.** Mind tells Hermes that unless you make yourself equal to God you cannot understand God: knowing is becoming.
- **Asclepius.** The human as a "great miracle," a bridge between earth and the divine.

**The Emerald Tablet** (about 13 short verses, wording varies by translation): what is below is like what is above, and what is above is like what is below, to work the miracles of the one thing; all things come from the One by adaptation; its father is the Sun, its mother the Moon, the wind carried it, the earth nursed it; separate earth from fire, the subtle from the gross, gently; it rises from earth to heaven and descends again, receiving the power of above and below. "As above, so below" is a modern short form of the second verse, not a close translation.

**The Kybalion's seven principles** (axioms as printed in 1908):
1. **Mentalism**: "THE ALL is MIND; The Universe is Mental."
2. **Correspondence**: "As above, so below; as below, so above."
3. **Vibration**: "Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates."
4. **Polarity**: "Everything is Dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same; opposites are identical in nature, but different in degree; extremes meet; all truths are but half-truths; all paradoxes may be reconciled."
5. **Rhythm**: "Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall; the pendulum-swing manifests in everything; the measure of the swing to the right is the measure of the swing to the left; rhythm compensates."
6. **Cause and Effect**: "Every Cause has its Effect; every Effect has its Cause; everything happens according to Law; Chance is but a name for Law not recognized; there are many planes of causation, but nothing escapes the Law."
7. **Gender**: "Gender is in everything; everything has its Masculine and Feminine Principles; Gender manifests on all planes."

(The names and order are confirmed by search; the full axiom wording for 4 to 7 is from the text as commonly reproduced [unverified this pass].) The Kybalion also has a chapter on "Mental Transmutation" and calls the Hermetists "mental alchemists" [unverified].

### 1.3 How it sees

- **Knowledge that changes the knower (gnosis).** Hermetic knowledge is not information. It is a direct knowing through Nous that transforms the person. Ignorance is the first and root vice (it heads the CH XIII list).
- **The human as a small cosmos.** Microcosm and macrocosm share one pattern, so reading one reads the other.
- **Reasoning by correspondence.** Find the same pattern on another plane or at another scale. Analogy is the instrument, not decoration.
- **Ascent by subtraction.** The soul is not built up; it gives back what it took on during descent until only its essence remains.
- **The Kybalion's (modern) method.** Change states by "polarizing" (moving along a scale toward the opposite pole rather than fighting it), and escape the pendulum of rhythm by rising to a higher plane of awareness.
- **Questions Emmy can think with:** What is the same pattern at the next scale up or down? What was taken on during the descent, and what does giving it back restore? Is this a difference of kind, or the same thing at a different degree?

### 1.5 Status

- **Documented:** the Hermetica are Greco-Egyptian texts of the 1st to 3rd centuries CE; the Emerald Tablet is first attested in early medieval Arabic; the Kybalion is a 1908 American New Thought book.
- **What the tradition holds:** that Hermes was a primordial sage and that one ancient wisdom runs beneath all religions (the Renaissance prisca theologia). Casaubon's 1614 dating ended the historical form of that claim.
- **Science:** the Hermetica make religious and philosophical claims, not testable ones. The Kybalion borrows the vocabulary of early 1900s physics (vibration, planes, polarity) as metaphor and makes no measurable predictions.

## 2. Kabbalah

### 2.1 Origin and history

- **The word** means "receiving" or "tradition."
- **Forerunners.** Late antique Jewish throne and palace mysticism (Merkavah and Hekhalot literature) [unverified]. **Sefer Yetzirah** (Book of Formation): author unknown, traditionally ascribed to Abraham or Rabbi Akiva; scholarly dates range from the 1st to the 9th century CE, with much recent work favouring the 3rd to 6th. It introduces the ten **sefirot** (originally "numbers" or "countings") and the **32 paths of wisdom**: 10 sefirot plus the 22 Hebrew letters.
- **Medieval Kabbalah proper.** **Sefer ha-Bahir**, composed in Provence in the late 12th century and attributed to an ancient rabbi (pseudepigraphic), possibly with older layers; Gershom Scholem's 1923 study made it the turning point of Jewish mysticism. Circles in Provence and Gerona (Catalonia) developed the system through the 13th century [details unverified].
- **The Zohar** ("Radiance"), written in Aramaic and presented as the teaching of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (2nd century). Moses de León circulated it in Castile from the 1280s, claiming to copy an old manuscript. Scholem's conclusion, generally accepted, is that de León wrote most of it in the last two decades of the 13th century (the *Ra'aya Meheimna* and *Tikkunim* sections are later). Some current scholars describe a circle of authors rather than one [unverified].
- **Safed, 16th century.** Moses Cordovero systematised the earlier Kabbalah. **Isaac Luria** (the Ari, 1534-1572) settled in Safed around 1570, wrote almost nothing, and died within about two years; his student **Hayyim Vital** recorded the teaching (the *Etz Hayyim*, "Tree of Life").
- **Later Jewish developments:** Sabbateanism (17th century) and Hasidism (18th century) both drew on Lurianic ideas [unverified].
- **Christian Cabala.** Giovanni Pico della Mirandola's 900 Theses (1486) used Kabbalistic letter and number readings to argue for Christian doctrine such as the Trinity. Johannes Reuchlin wrote *De verbo mirifico* (on deriving the name of Jesus from the divine name) and *De arte cabalistica* (1517), framing Kabbalah as a universal language fitting Neoplatonism and Christianity; Joseph Dan notes Reuchlin's sources were eclectic and light on the Zohar. Athanasius Kircher's *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652) carried this into later occult use. Christian Knorr von Rosenroth's Latin *Kabbala Denudata* (1677-1684) [dates unverified] was the source for Mathers's English *Kabbalah Unveiled*.
- **Hermetic (occult) Qabalah.** 19th-century occultism, especially Éliphas Lévi and then the Golden Dawn (1888), made the Tree a master filing system: tarot trumps, planets, signs, colours, gods and elements assigned to the sefirot and paths. Crowley's *777* (1909) prints the tables; its 1955 preface says about ninety per cent of the Hebrew, the four colour scales, and the tarot order and attributions were as taught in the Golden Dawn. Dion Fortune's *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935) [unverified] popularised it.
- **Spelling convention** (common, not universal): *Kabbalah* for the Jewish tradition, *Cabala* for the Christian, *Qabalah* for the Hermetic or occult.

### 2.2 Core structure

- **Ein Sof** ("without end"): the infinite God beyond any attribute, unknowable in itself.
- **The ten sefirot** (with common translations), top to bottom:
  1. **Keter**, crown (sometimes replaced in counts by **Da'at**, knowledge)
  2. **Chokhmah**, wisdom
  3. **Binah**, understanding
  4. **Chesed**, loving-kindness, expansion
  5. **Gevurah** (also **Din**), strength, judgment, restraint
  6. **Tiferet**, beauty, harmony, the balancing centre
  7. **Netzach**, endurance, victory
  8. **Hod**, splendour
  9. **Yesod**, foundation, the channel
  10. **Malkhut**, kingship, the receiving world, also the **Shekhinah** (indwelling presence)
- **Three columns.** Right: Chokhmah, Chesed, Netzach. Left: Binah, Gevurah, Hod. Centre: Keter, Tiferet, Yesod, Malkhut. Right is traditionally linked with expansion and the masculine, left with restraint and the feminine, the centre with balance. Hermetic Qabalah calls them the pillars of Mercy, Severity and Mildness.
- **The 22 paths.** Channels joining the sefirot, each given a Hebrew letter. Sefer Yetzirah sorts the letters into 3 "mothers," 7 "doubles" and 12 "simples," linked to elements, the seven planets and the twelve zodiac signs or months [the letter-class correspondences are from general knowledge; unverified]. In Hermetic Qabalah the paths are numbered 11 to 32 and each also gets a tarot trump. Jewish and occult arrangements of the paths on the diagram differ.
- **The four worlds** (names from Isaiah 43:7), in descending order: **Atzilut** (emanation), **Beriah** (creation), **Yetzirah** (formation), **Assiah** (action; both spiritual and physical). **Adam Kadmon** (primordial human) is sometimes counted as a fifth, transcendent level.
- **The sefirot on the body.** The passage *Patach Eliyahu* (Tikkunei Zohar, introduction 17a): Chesed is the right arm, Gevurah the left arm, Tiferet the torso, Netzach and Hod the two legs (or thighs), Yesod the end of the body, the sign of the covenant, Malkhut the mouth ("Oral Torah"); above them Chokhmah is the brain and Binah the heart. The body here is the image of the divine pattern (Adam Kadmon), not a physical map of where things are stored.
- **Lurianic doctrine.** **Tzimtzum**: the infinite withdraws "from itself into itself" to leave a space for a world. **Shevirat ha-kelim**: the vessels meant to hold the divine light shatter, scattering sparks into the lower worlds, where they are held by husks (**kelipot**) [kelipot unverified]. **Tikkun**: repair, carried out through human action (commandments, prayer, intention) that lifts the sparks; complete tikkun would transform the world as we know it. Luria also teaches **partzufim**, "faces" or configurations of the sefirot [unverified].
- **Levels of soul** commonly taught: nefesh, ruach, neshamah, with chayah and yechidah above [unverified].

### 2.3 How it sees

- **Reality as emanation.** The infinite steps down by stages into matter. Everything is some mixture of the divine attributes, and every event can be read as attributes in balance or imbalance.
- **Right and left in balance.** Unlimited Chesed floods and dissolves; unlimited Gevurah becomes cruelty. Tiferet is the centre that holds both. Most Kabbalistic diagnosis asks which side is overflowing and which is withheld.
- **Text as code.** Letters, numbers and divine names carry layers of meaning; scripture is read at several depths, from plain sense to secret [the four-level PaRDeS scheme, unverified].
- **Humans act on the upper worlds.** Human intention and action are not small; they repair or damage the divine order.
- **Questions Emmy can think with:** Which quality is overflowing here and which is withheld? Where is the balancing centre? Where is light trapped in a shell, and what act frees it? At which of the four levels (pure source, idea, form, action) is this happening?

### 2.5 Status

- **Documented:** Sefer Yetzirah late antique (date disputed); Bahir late 12th century Provence; Zohar late 13th century Castile; Luria 16th century Safed; Christian Cabala from 1486; Hermetic Qabalah from the 19th century.

- **Respect note:** Jewish Kabbalah is part of a living religion. Christian Cabala and Hermetic Qabalah are adaptations that many Jewish scholars and practitioners treat as separate systems. Emmy keeps the three distinct and does not present occult tables (tarot on the paths, for example) as Jewish teaching.
- **Science:** theological and interpretive claims; nothing to test empirically.

## 3. Western astrology

### 3.1 Origin and history

- **Mesopotamian omens.** *Enuma Anu Enlil*, a compendium of 70 or more cuneiform tablets assembled over centuries from roughly the second millennium BCE, read the sky as signs for the king and the state, not for private persons.
- **The zodiac.** The circle of twelve equal 30-degree signs appears in Babylonian texts by about the 5th century BCE (one source gives an earliest example around 419 BCE).
- **The first personal horoscope** known is a Babylonian cuneiform chart for 29 April 410 BCE. The earliest charts placed planets by nearby stars rather than by sign.
- **Hellenistic Egypt, 2nd to 1st century BCE.** After Alexander's conquests, Babylonian zodiac, Greek geometry and Egyptian time-keeping (decans) combined into horoscopic astrology: the rising degree (horoskopos, the Ascendant) and the houses. Whole-sign houses were the standard from about the 1st century BCE into the early medieval period.
- **Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos** (Claudius Ptolemy, Alexandria, c. 150 CE) gave astrology a natural-philosophy rationale and fixed the **tropical zodiac**: signs keyed to the equinoxes and solstices, with Aries beginning at the spring equinox. Ptolemy knew of precession but did not let it move the signs.
- **Medieval and early modern.** Persian and Arabic astrologers developed quadrant house systems and passed the art to Latin Europe in the 12th century; it declined in learned circles after the 17th century [general history; unverified detail]. Placidus houses became the default in 20th-century books and software.
- **New planets.** Uranus (1781), Neptune (1846) and Pluto (1930) were added by modern astrologers, with new rulerships [dates from general knowledge].
- **Psychological astrology.** Alan Leo (a Theosophist, late 19th to early 20th century) shifted astrology toward character; Dane Rudhyar's *The Astrology of Personality* (1936) founded "humanistic" astrology; Liz Greene and Howard Sasportas founded the Centre for Psychological Astrology in London in 1983. Jung shaped all of this. Since the 1990s, translations of Hellenistic texts have revived older techniques such as whole-sign houses.

### 3.2 Core structure

- **Tropical zodiac:** twelve 30-degree signs measured from the spring equinox. **Sidereal zodiac** (used in Indian Jyotish) is measured against the stars. Precession has pulled the two about 24 degrees apart, so the tropical signs no longer line up with the constellations of the same names, by design.
- **Signs:** Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius, Pisces. Each has an **element** (fire, earth, air, water), a **modality** (cardinal, fixed, mutable) and alternates **masculine/feminine** (or positive/negative).
- **Planets:** the seven classical "planets" (Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn), each ruling signs; modern astrology adds Uranus, Neptune and Pluto. Also used: the lunar nodes, the Ascendant and the Midheaven.
- **Houses:** twelve areas of life counted from the Ascendant (1 self and body; 2 resources; 3 siblings and communication; 4 home and roots; 5 children and pleasure; 6 illness and work; 7 partners and open rivals; 8 death and shared resources; 9 travel, religion, higher learning; 10 career and standing; 11 friends and hopes; 12 hidden things and confinement) [house meanings from general knowledge]. Systems differ: whole-sign (oldest), Placidus (quadrant), and others.
- **Aspects:** angles between planets. Ptolemy's five: conjunction (0 degrees), sextile (60), square (90), trine (120), opposition (180) [unverified this pass]. Modern astrology adds minor aspects.
- **Timing:** transits (current sky against the birth chart), progressions, and planetary returns (for example the Saturn return at about 29.5 years).

### 3.3 How it sees

- **Time has quality.** The sky at a moment is a signature of that moment; what begins then carries it. This is "as above, so below" applied to time.
- **Reading is synthesis:** *what function* (planet), *in what style* (sign), *in what part of life* (house), *in what relation* (aspect).
- **Cycles:** life is read in recurring planetary cycles and returns.
- **Modern psychological reading:** the chart is a map of inner potentials and archetypes (Jung), not a forecast of fixed events.
- **Questions Emmy can think with:** What function is active, in what style, in which area of life, in tension or in harmony with what? Where in its cycle is this pattern?

## 4. Numerology and gematria

### 4.1 Origin and history

- **Ancient number philosophy.** The Pythagoreans (from the 6th century BCE) held, on Aristotle's report, that numbers are the principles of things, inspired by the discovery that musical harmony follows small whole-number ratios. The **tetractys** (1+2+3+4 = 10, drawn as a triangle of dots) was sacred; abstract qualities "had" numbers (justice with 4 and the square, marriage with 5). Scholars dispute how much of this is Aristotle's reading rather than Pythagorean teaching. Later Neopythagoreans and Neoplatonists wrote whole books on the qualities of the numbers 1 to 10 [unverified detail].
- **Letters as numbers.** Greek alphabetic numerals developed at Miletus (graffiti from the 6th century BCE); summing a word's letters is **isopsephy**. The earliest claimed example of the practice is an inscription of the Assyrian king Sargon II (8th century BCE) saying a wall at Khorsabad was built to the numerical value of his name. Jewish use of letters as numbers is attested on coins of Alexander Jannaeus (78 BCE); numerical **gematria** appears in rabbinic statements from the 2nd century CE, used as a memory aid and supporting interpretation. Kabbalists later used it heavily.
- **Modern numerology is early 20th century and American.** L. Dow Balliett's *The Philosophy of Numbers* (1908) set out the English-alphabet system and popularised the master numbers 11 and 22. Julia Seton and her daughter Juno Jordan (who studied with Balliett) developed and spread it. Who coined the word "numerology" is disputed. The "Pythagorean" label is modern branding; the system descends from Balliett, not from Pythagoras.
- **The "Chaldean" system** was popularised by Cheiro (William John Warner, 1866-1936), who said he learned palmistry, astrology and numerology in India; his *Book of Numbers* is usually dated 1926 [unverified]. No documented line connects it to ancient Chaldea (Babylonia); the name is a claim, not a record.

### 4.2 Core structure

- **"Pythagorean" (Western) letter chart:** A to I = 1 to 9, J to R = 1 to 9, S to Z = 1 to 8.
- **"Chaldean" chart:** values 1 to 8 by sound, 9 held sacred and never given to a letter. A=1 B=2 C=3 D=4 E=5 F=8 G=3 H=5 I=1 J=1 K=2 L=3 M=4 N=5 O=7 P=8 Q=1 R=2 S=3 T=4 U=6 V=6 W=6 X=5 Y=1 Z=7. Usually applied to the name in daily use; also reads two-digit "compound" numbers.
- **Core numbers (methods vary by school):**
  - **Life Path:** the full birth date reduced to one digit (e.g. 14 Dec 1990: 12 to 3, 14 to 5, 1990 to 19 to 10 to 1; 3+5+1 = 9). Many schools keep **11, 22, 33** unreduced as master numbers; schools disagree on whether to reduce each part first or the total.
  - **Expression or Destiny:** the full birth name. **Soul Urge or Heart's Desire:** the vowels. **Personality:** the consonants. **Birthday:** the day of birth.
  - **Personal Year:** birth month + birth day + current year, read as a position in a **nine-year cycle** [unverified detail].
- **Gematria:** Hebrew letters valued 1 to 400 (aleph 1 ... tav 400). Words with equal totals are read as linked; the classic rabbinic example is wine (yayin) = 70 = secret (sod), "when wine goes in, the secret comes out" (Talmud, Eruvin 65a) [unverified]. Methods include standard value, ordinal value, reduced value and letter substitution.

### 4.3 How it sees

- **Numbers are qualities, not quantities.** Each digit carries a character; a name or date is read as a combination of characters.
- **Life as a cycle.** Numerology places a person at a stage of a repeating cycle (start, growth, completion).
- **Hidden links.** Gematria finds kinship between words that share a value; it is a way of reading a sacred text more deeply, not a forecast.
- **Number as structure.** In the Pythagorean strand, ratio and harmony are what make the world orderly.

## 5. Alchemy as inner work, the Golden Dawn, and Theosophy

### 5.1 Alchemy

**Origin and history**
- Alchemy arose in Greco-Roman Egypt, where Greek natural philosophy met Egyptian metalworking. Early named authorities are Maria the Jewess and Pseudo-Democritus. **Zosimos of Panopolis** (c. 300 CE) is the earliest alchemist whose writings survive in substantial form; he traced the art to pharaonic Egypt, a claim without evidence.
- **Arabic alchemy:** the corpus under the name **Jabir ibn Hayyan** (Jabir himself c. 721-815; the corpus is later and composite [unverified]) taught that metals are made of sulphur and mercury in different proportions and developed the idea of the elixir or stone.
- **Latin Europe** from the 12th century. **Paracelsus** (1493-1541) recast the principles as the **tria prima**, salt, sulphur and mercury, and mapped them onto the person: salt as body, sulphur as soul, mercury as spirit (his medical use of the theory dates to *Opus Paramirum*, 1531). Newton and Boyle both practised alchemy [unverified detail].
- **Alchemy as inner work.** Some early modern authors (Jacob Boehme and the Rosicrucian circle, 17th century) read alchemy as a spiritual process. Mary Anne Atwood's *A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery* (1850 [date unverified]) and C. G. Jung's *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944 [unverified]) made the "inner alchemy" reading dominant in the 20th century. Historians **Lawrence Principe and William Newman** (2001) argue that the purely spiritual reading is largely a 19th and 20th century construction and that most historical alchemists did real laboratory work; others reply that alchemy also carried genuine religious meaning. Honest summary: both the laboratory and the spiritual sides are documented; the claim that alchemy was "always really" psychological is modern.

**Core structure**
- Four elements (fire, air, water, earth) and their qualities; the **tria prima**; seven metals paired with the seven planets (lead with Saturn, tin with Jupiter, iron with Mars, gold with the Sun, copper with Venus, quicksilver with Mercury, silver with the Moon) [unverified].
- **The Great Work** in colour stages: **nigredo** (blackening, decomposition), **albedo** (whitening, purification), **citrinitas** (yellowing), **rubedo** (reddening, completion) [unverified this pass].
- Operations (lists vary): calcination, dissolution, separation, conjunction, fermentation, distillation, coagulation. Motto: **solve et coagula**, dissolve and recombine. Goal: the philosopher's stone and **transmutation**.

**How it sees:** the worker and the work change together; a fixed form must be broken down before its essence can be recombined at a higher purity; nothing is lost, only transformed; heat and time are the tools; the dark, decaying phase is necessary, not failure. ### 5.3 Theosophy
