# PART ONE · THE VEDIC AND INDIAN SYSTEMS

## 1.1 The Vedas

**1. Origin and history**
- Four Vedas: **Rigveda** (hymns), **Samaveda** (chants set to melody), **Yajurveda** (ritual
  formulas), **Atharvaveda** (spells, healing, household rites).
- Each Veda has layers: **Samhita** (the hymn or mantra collection), **Brahmana** (ritual
  explanation), **Aranyaka** ("forest" texts, more symbolic), **Upanishad** (philosophy).
- Dates are inferred, not recorded. Britannica's student encyclopedia: Rigveda c. 1500 BCE,
  Yajur and Atharva c. 1200 BCE, Sama c. 1100 BCE; Brahmanas c. 900 BCE; Aranyakas c. 800-600
  BCE; early Upanishads c. 700-500 BCE. Other scholars give the Rigveda a wider window of
  c. 1800-1100 BCE, with its core books 2-9 often placed c. 1500-1200 BCE.
- Composed and preserved orally for centuries, with elaborate memorization methods. The whole
  corpus counts as **shruti**, "what was heard": revealed, not authored, in the tradition's view.
- Six auxiliary sciences, the **Vedangas**, grew around the Vedas: phonetics, ritual, grammar,
  etymology, meter, and **jyotisha** (astronomy and calendar for timing rites; see 1.9).

**2. Core structure**
- Gods (devas) such as Agni (fire), Indra (storm, strength), Soma (the ritual drink and the
  moon), Varuna (cosmic law), Surya (sun), Vayu (wind).
- **Rta**: the cosmic order that keeps the seasons, the sacrifice and moral life aligned. Later
  replaced in emphasis by **dharma**. [bg]
- The fire sacrifice (yajna) as the central act.

**3. How it sees**
- The world is a web of hidden **correspondences** (bandhu) between ritual, body and cosmos. Do
  the right act, with the right sound, at the right time, and the cosmos stays in order. [bg]
- Sound is causal. A mantra spoken correctly does work; it is not a description.
- Thinking move for Emmy: "What order is this act keeping, or breaking? Is the timing right?"

## 1.2 The Upanishads and Vedanta

**1. Origin and history**
- Early prose Upanishads, **Brihadaranyaka** and **Chandogya**, c. 700-500 BCE (pre-Buddhist
  for the most part). Then Taittiriya, Aitareya, Kaushitaki; then verse Upanishads such as Katha,
  Isha, Shvetashvatara, Mundaka; then Prashna, Mandukya (some of these into the last centuries
  BCE). About 13 are "principal". [bg on the internal order]
- Later lists run to 108 Upanishads (the Muktika canon), many of them medieval, including the
  "Yoga Upanishads" that carry chakra and nadi teachings. [bg]
- **Vedanta** ("end of the Veda") systematizes them. Main schools: **Advaita** (non-dual;
  Shankara, c. 8th century CE), **Vishishtadvaita** (qualified non-dual; Ramanuja, c. 11th-12th
  century), **Dvaita** (dual; Madhva, 13th century). [bg]
- Modern carriers: Vivekananda (1893 Chicago Parliament), Ramana Maharshi (1879-1950,
  self-inquiry "Who am I?"). [bg]

**2. Core structure**
- **Brahman**: the one ultimate reality. **Atman**: the Self. The core claim: Atman is Brahman
  ("tat tvam asi", you are that, Chandogya 6.8.7; "aham brahmasmi", I am Brahman,
  Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10). [bg on verse numbers]
- **Nirguna Brahman** (without qualities) and **Saguna Brahman** (with qualities, as God).
- **Neti neti**, "not this, not this" (Brihadaranyaka 2.3.6): the Self is found by negating
  everything it is not. [bg]
- **Four states** (Mandukya): waking, dreaming, deep sleep, and **turiya**, "the fourth", the
  witness behind all three. [bg]
- **Karma and rebirth**: "as one acts, so one becomes" (Brihadaranyaka 4.4.5). At death the self
  moves to a new body "like a caterpillar that reaches the next blade of grass" (4.4.3). [bg]
- **The heart and its channels**: "A hundred and one are the channels of the heart; one of them
  passes up to the crown of the head. Going up by it, one reaches immortality" (Katha 6.16;
  same in Chandogya 8.6.6). The Self is in the "small lotus" of the heart (Chandogya 8.1) and is
  a "thumb-sized person" in the heart (Katha). [bg on wording; the 101 count is confirmed by the
  Prashna parallel below]
- **Sat-Chit-Ananda** (Being-Consciousness-Bliss) as a name for Brahman crystallizes in later
  Upanishads and Vedanta, built on older lines such as "In the beginning this was Being (sat)
  alone" (Chandogya 6.2.1). [bg]
- Method: **shravana, manana, nididhyasana**: hear the teaching, reflect on it, meditate on it
  until it is direct knowledge. [bg]

**3. How it sees**
- The person is a Self wrapped in mistaken identifications. Suffering comes from **avidya**,
  not-knowing what one is.
- Reading a person: what are they identified with? Body, breath, mind, intellect, or joy?
- Reading a moment: which state is awareness in (waking, dream, sleep), and is the witness present?
- Thinking move for Emmy: subtract. "If this is not you, what remains?"

## 1.3 The koshas (five sheaths)

**1. Origin and history**
- Source: **Taittiriya Upanishad 2.1-2.5** (roughly 6th-5th century BCE; dates rough). The text
  describes a self "made of food", then within it one "made of breath", and so on inward.
- The word **kosha** ("sheath") as the standard label, and the doctrine of five sheaths
  (pancha kosha) as a meditation map, is the later Vedanta reading, used in texts such as the
  Vivekachudamani (attributed to Shankara; authorship disputed) and Vidyaranya's Panchadashi
  (14th century). [bg]

**2. Core structure** (outer to inner)
| Sheath | Literal | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Annamaya | made of food | the physical body |
| Pranamaya | made of breath | vital energy, the five vayus |
| Manomaya | made of mind | sense-mind, emotion, reaction |
| Vijnanamaya | made of understanding | intellect, judgement, the sense of being the doer |
| Anandamaya | made of bliss | the joy of deep sleep, the subtlest covering |
The Self (Atman) is none of the five. It is what they cover.

**3. How it sees**
- A person is layered. Each layer can be mistaken for "me".
- Method: discrimination (viveka). Locate the layer, see it as an object, step back from it.
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Which sheath is this person speaking from right now?" A pain complaint
  speaks from the food sheath; a racing story speaks from mind; "I know best" speaks from intellect.

## 1.4 Prana, the vayus, the nadis, and kundalini

**1. Origin and history**
- **Prana** (breath, life) is already central in the Upanishads, for example the contest in
  which the senses leave the body one by one and only breath cannot (Brihadaranyaka 1.5,
  Chandogya 5.1). [bg]
- **Nadis** (channels): Brihadaranyaka 2.1.19 speaks of 72,000 channels running from the heart.
  Prashna Upanishad 3.6-3.7: in the heart arise 101 nadis, each with 100 branches, each of those
  with 72,000 more; one channel leads the departing self upward at death. Katha 6.16 gives the 101
  channels of the heart (one secondary source misquotes this as 108).
- **Ida, pingala, sushumna** as the three key channels appear in tantric and hatha yoga texts.
  The **Hatha Yoga Pradipika** (Svatmarama, 15th century, sometimes 14th-15th; a compilation of
  earlier hatha texts) teaches that breath control purifies the channels until "the wind easily
  pierces and enters the aperture of the Sushumna".
- **Kundalini** as a coiled power at the base of the spine develops in Shaiva and Shakta tantra
  (roughly 8th-11th centuries for the early texts) and becomes central in hatha yoga
  (roughly 11th-15th centuries). [bg] Scholar Christopher Wallis argues that in the sources
  kundalini is a dynamic process more than a dormant serpent waiting to be woken.

**2. Core structure**
- **Five vayus** (winds): **prana** (inward breath, chest and head), **apana** (downward and
  outward: elimination, birth), **samana** (balancing, navel, digestion), **udana** (upward:
  speech, effort, the exit at death, Prashna 3.7), **vyana** (pervasive, circulates through the
  nadis). Five minor upa-pranas: naga, kurma, krikara (or krikala), devadatta, dhananjaya.
- **Three main nadis**:
  - Ida: left, lunar, cooling, ends at the left nostril.
  - Pingala: right, solar, heating, ends at the right nostril.
  - Sushumna: central, along the spine, from base to crown. Kundalini rises here.
  - Ida and pingala are often drawn crossing at each chakra; that picture is mostly from modern
    illustration. The medieval texts mostly place them simply left and right. [bg]
- Pranayama (breath regulation), kumbhaka (retention), bandhas (locks), mudras.
- Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.2: when breath moves, mind moves; when breath is still, mind is still. [bg]

**3. How it sees**
- Health and awareness are matters of **flow** through channels. Blockage is the problem;
  breath is the handle.
- Reading a person: which side dominates (heating, active pingala; cooling, receptive ida)?
  Where is the flow stuck?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "What does the breath show? Where does it not reach?"

## 1.5 The chakras: what is ancient, what is modern

This is the section where popular belief and documented history differ most. Emmy should know
the difference cold.

**1. Origin and history** (in order)
1. **Cakra** means "wheel" in the Rigveda (the sun's wheel, chariot wheels). It is not yet a body
   center. [bg]
2. **Heart lotus and channels** in the Upanishads (1.2, 1.4): body-centers are implied but not
   a chakra system.
3. **Buddhist tantras, c. 8th century onward**: the **Hevajra Tantra** has four centers: navel,
   heart, throat, head. These map onto the four bodies of a Buddha and four joys (see 2.3).
4. **Hindu (Shaiva and Shakta) tantras, c. 9th-11th centuries**: many competing systems. The
   **Kubjikamata Tantra** (dated variously 9th-11th century; recensions differ on five or six
   centers) has a system close to the later six. The **Kaulajnananirnaya** (attributed to
   Matsyendranatha) lists more. Counts across texts run 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 12 and beyond.
5. **Function in the sources**: Wallis and other scholars stress that tantric chakras were
   mainly **prescriptive**: places where the practitioner installs mantras, deities, letters and
   elements by visualization. They were templates for practice more than a fixed anatomy.
6. **Purnananda's Sat-chakra-nirupana** ("Description of the Six Centers"), dated 1577 (some
   say c. 1550): six chakras plus the crown, with petals, letters, seed syllables, elements and
   deities. This text is the main ancestor of the modern seven.
7. **Sir John Woodroffe (pen name Arthur Avalon), *The Serpent Power*, 1918 or 1919**: a rough
   translation of the Sat-chakra-nirupana with long commentary. It brought the six-plus-crown
   system to the West. Woodroffe reported, and discussed, the idea that the Adhara (root) chakra
   "is the sacrococcygeal plexus and that the Svadhisthana is the sacral plexus and so forth".
   Modern scholars criticize the translation's accuracy.
8. **Vasant G. Rele, *The Mysterious Kundalini*** (1927 [bg on the year]): identified the chakras
   with the physical nerve plexuses and kundalini with the right vagus nerve.
9. **Theosophy**: C. W. Leadbeater's *The Inner Life* (1910) and *The Chakras* (1927) gave
   clairvoyant colors and moved some locations (for example, a spleen center). His colors were
   not the rainbow.
10. **C. G. Jung's 1932 Kundalini seminar** read the chakras as stages of psychological
    development. [bg]
11. **The rainbow**: Christopher Hills, *Nuclear Evolution* (usually dated 1977; some sources
    give earlier), mapped each center to one rainbow color. Kurt Leland's history *Rainbow Body*
    (2016) concludes that the most common Western version "came together as recently as 1977".
12. **Psychological themes** (survival, sexuality, power, love, expression, intuition, spirit)
    were popularized by Anodea Judith, *Wheels of Life* (1987), and Caroline Myss, *Anatomy of
    the Spirit* (1996). [bg] Gland associations (adrenal, gonads, pancreas, thymus, thyroid,
    pituitary, pineal) arise in the Theosophical era; who first made them is not settled.
13. **Extra centers** above and below the body (Earth Star, Soul Star, Stellar Gateway, Gaia
    Gateway, and 12-chakra systems) are late 20th-century New Age additions. [bg: exact origin
    not verified]

**2. Core structure** (the Sat-chakra-nirupana seven, via Woodroffe) [bg on details]

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Muladhara | base, perineum | 4 | earth | LAM | yellow square; kundalini coiled here |
| Svadhishthana | genital root | 6 | water | VAM | crescent |
| Manipura | navel | 10 | fire | RAM | triangle |
| Anahata | heart | 12 | air | YAM | six-pointed star of two triangles |
| Vishuddha | throat | 16 | space (akasha) | HAM | circle |

**3. How it sees**
- In the original tantric use: the body is a temple of installed powers. You **build** the
  chakra by visualization and mantra. The question is "what am I installing here?"
- In the modern Western use: the chakras are a **diagnostic** map of psychological functions.
  The question is "which center is blocked?"
- Thinking move for Emmy: know which of the two Lance means. MOB's chakra is closer to the
  modern descriptive line, with its own mechanics (plexus, field, node, release).

## 1.6 Samkhya

**1. Origin and history**
- Traditional founder: the sage **Kapila** (legendary, undated).
- Classical text: **Samkhya Karika** of **Ishvarakrishna**, commonly dated c. 4th-5th century CE.
  [bg] Older Samkhya ideas appear in the Upanishads (Katha, Shvetashvatara) and the
  Mahabharata, including the Bhagavad Gita. [bg]
- **Samkhya** means "enumeration" or "counting". It is one of the six orthodox schools, paired
  with Yoga.

**2. Core structure**
- Two eternal realities: **purusha** (pure consciousness, the witness, many in number) and
  **prakriti** (nature, matter, everything that changes).
- **Three gunas**, the strands of prakriti: **sattva** (clarity, light), **rajas** (activity,
  passion), **tamas** (inertia, heaviness).
- **25 tattvas** (principles): purusha; prakriti; buddhi or mahat (intellect); ahamkara (the
  "I-maker", ego); manas (mind); 5 sense capacities; 5 action capacities; 5 subtle elements
  (tanmatras: sound, touch, form, taste, smell); 5 gross elements (space, air, fire, water, earth).
- **Satkaryavada**: the effect already exists in the cause.
- Liberation (**kaivalya**, isolation) comes from discriminating knowledge that purusha is not
  prakriti.

**3. How it sees**
- Enumerate. Break experience into its components until the observer stands apart from all of
  them. This is the most "mechanical" of the Indian schools.
- Reading a person: which guna dominates their state, their food, their speech, their work
  (Bhagavad Gita chapters 14 and 17 apply the gunas to everything). [bg]
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Is this heavy (tamas), restless (rajas), or clear (sattva)?"

## 1.7 The Yoga Sutra of Patanjali

**1. Origin and history**
- Author: **Patanjali**. Dating is debated. Philipp Maas dates the text, with its earliest
  commentary as one work (the Patanjalayogashastra), to about **400 CE** (often given as
  325-425 CE), because it answers the Buddhist philosopher Vasubandhu (c. 320-400 CE); J. H.
  Woods proposed a similar date in 1914. Edwin Bryant argues for a date shortly after the turn of
  the Common Era. Other estimates run from the 2nd century BCE to the 5th century CE. A date of
  400 BCE is not supported by current scholarship.
- About 195 or 196 sutras in four chapters: Samadhi, Sadhana, Vibhuti (powers), Kaivalya.

**2. Core structure**
- 1.2 **yogash chitta vritti nirodhah**: yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind.
- Five **vrittis**: valid knowledge, error, verbal construction (vikalpa), sleep, memory. [bg]
- Practice and non-attachment (**abhyasa, vairagya**, 1.12). [bg]
- Five **kleshas** (afflictions, 2.3): ignorance (avidya), I-am-ness (asmita), attraction (raga),
  aversion (dvesha), clinging to life or fear of death (abhinivesha). [bg]
- **Samskaras**: impressions left by experience that seed future fluctuations. [bg]
- **Eight limbs**: yama (restraints: non-harm, truth, non-stealing, continence, non-grasping),
  niyama (observances: purity, contentment, discipline, self-study, surrender to Ishvara),
  asana, pranayama, pratyahara (withdrawal of the senses), dharana (concentration), dhyana
  (meditation), samadhi (absorption). [bg]
- **Pratyaya**: the content of a cognition; dhyana is "one continuous flow of pratyaya" (3.2). [bg]

**3. How it sees**
- The mind is a field that keeps rippling. Impressions (samskaras) keep it rippling. Practice
  stills it; non-attachment stops new impressions forming.
- Reading a person: which klesha is driving? Which vritti is active right now?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Is this a fresh perception, or an old impression replaying?"

## 1.8 Ayurveda (brief)

**1. Origin and history**
- **Charaka Samhita** (internal medicine): present form c. 1st century CE per Britannica, a
  redaction of an older treatise attributed to Agnivesha.
- **Sushruta Samhita** (surgery): probably originated in the last centuries BCE and fixed later.
- **Vagbhata**, *Ashtanga Hridaya* and *Ashtanga Samgraha*: commonly placed c. 6th-7th century
  CE; sources range from c. 400 to the 8th-9th century. [bg on the consensus]

**2. Core structure**
- **Three doshas**: **vata** (air and space: movement), **pitta** (fire and water:

- Seven dhatus (tissues), agni (digestive fire), ama (undigested residue), ojas (vigor). [bg]
- **Marma** points: Sushruta lists 107 vital points where injury is dangerous, at junctions of

  texts. [bg]

## 1.9 Jyotish (Vedic, sidereal astrology)

**1. Origin and history**
- **Vedanga Jyotisha**: the calendar text for timing Vedic rites, built on the Moon and the
  **nakshatras** (lunar mansions). Dating is disputed: some scholars place its astronomy at
  1400-1200 BCE; the extant text is usually dated to the final centuries BCE. It is about
  calendar and sacrifice timing, not horoscopes.
- The nakshatras themselves are named in Vedic texts (lists of 27 or 28). [bg on the specific texts]
- **Horoscopic astrology** (12 signs, houses, planetary rulers, the birth chart) came from
  **Hellenistic Greek astrology**. David Pingree dated a Sanskrit translation by Yavaneshvara to
  149/150 CE and its versification by Sphujidhvaja as the **Yavanajataka** to 269/270 CE, and
  argued it underlies nearly all later Indian horoscopy. Later scholarship (Bill Mak) has
  revised some of these dates. The Greek transmission itself is the mainstream view. Early
  translators carried the 12 Western signs straight into Sanskrit.
- Classical authors: Varahamihira (6th century CE: *Brihat Jataka*, *Brihat Samhita*). [bg] The
  **Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra**, attributed to the ancient sage Parashara, is the central text
  of modern practice; its extant form is a later compilation. [bg; dating not verified this pass]
- Astrophysicist Jayant Narlikar has argued publicly that horoscopic astrology "came with
  Alexander".

**2. Core structure**
- **Nine grahas** ("graspers"): Surya (Sun), Chandra (Moon), Mangala (Mars), Budha (Mercury),
  Guru or Brihaspati (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani (Saturn), **Rahu** and **Ketu** (the
  north and south lunar nodes, the eclipse points). No outer planets.
- **Twelve rashis** (signs), sidereal: Mesha (Aries), Vrishabha, Mithuna, Karka, Simha, Kanya,
  **Tula** (Libra, "the balance"), Vrishchika, Dhanu, Makara, Kumbha, Mina. Rulers: Mars (Aries,
  Scorpio), Venus (Taurus, Libra), Mercury (Gemini, Virgo), Moon (Cancer), Sun (Leo), Jupiter
  (Sagittarius, Pisces), Saturn (Capricorn, Aquarius). [bg]
- **Sidereal zodiac and ayanamsa**: Jyotish measures signs against the fixed stars; Western
  astrology uses the seasons (tropical). The gap (ayanamsa) is about 24 degrees now (Lahiri
  ayanamsa, India's official standard). [bg] Practical effect: most people's Western Sun sign
  moves back one sign in Jyotish.
- **Twelve bhavas** (houses), **lagna** (ascendant), **vargas** (divisional charts, the
  navamsha or D9 most used), **yogas** (planetary combinations), **gochara** (transits). [bg]
- **Panchanga** (five limbs of the day: tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga, karana) and **muhurta**
  (choosing an auspicious moment). [bg]
- **27 nakshatras**, each 13°20' of sidereal longitude, each split into 4 padas of 3°20'. Lords
  follow the Vimshottari order (verified); deities are from the classical lists [bg].

## 2.1 Bön

**1. Origin and history**
- **Tradition holds**: Bön was taught by **Tonpa Shenrab Miwoche** in the land of Olmo Lungring
  and spread through **Zhangzhung** in western Tibet. Bön dates for him run from 18,000 years ago
  to the 2nd millennium BCE; academic summaries list dates from 1917 BCE to the 6th or 4th
  century BCE, and note his life story closely resembles the Buddha's.
- **Zhangzhung** was a real kingdom on the western Tibetan plateau, later absorbed by the Tibetan
  empire (7th century). [bg on the date]
- **Documented**: after persecution in the 8th-9th centuries, Bön re-emerged when **Shenchen
  Luga** (995-1035) rediscovered hidden texts in **1017**. Organized "Yungdrung" (eternal) Bön
  takes shape in the 10th-11th centuries.
- **Scholarly debate**: Per Kvaerne uses "Bön" only for the tradition from the 10th-11th
  centuries and calls it "an unorthodox form of Buddhism". Samten Karmay argues an organized
  pre-Buddhist Bön existed. Rolf Stein argued the pre-Buddhist religion was not then called bön.
  Geoffrey Samuel calls Bön "essentially a variant of Tibetan Buddhism" keeping pre-Buddhist
  elements.
- Main monastery Menri (founded 1405; refounded in India 1967). [bg] Bön has its own Dzogchen
  lineage, the Zhang Zhung Nyen Gyud. [bg]

**2. Core structure**
- The **Nine Ways** (vehicles) of Bön, from divination and healing rites up to Dzogchen. [bg]
- **Five elements** central to practice (2.6).
- Spirits of place and element (lu, nyen, sadak), handled by ritual. [bg]
- **La** (bla): a soul or life-essence that can be lost through shock or fright and called back
  by "la-gug" retrieval rites. [bg]
- Subtle body: three channels. Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche's own *Nine Breathings of Purification*
  handout: the **right white channel** represents male energy and method and opens at the right
  nostril; the **left red channel** represents female energy and wisdom. One secondary account
  says the colors swap for women; the handout does not say so.
- **Nine Breathings**: exhale through the right white channel to clear anger, through the left
  red channel to clear attachment, and through the central channel to clear ignorance (three
  breaths each).

**3. How it sees**
- Health and fortune depend on balance with the elements and the beings of place.
- Reading a person: which element is weak or excessive? Has their la been shaken loose by a
  shock? What has the environment taken from them?

## 2.2 Vajrayana in Tibet

**1. Origin and history**
- **First diffusion**: King **Songtsen Gampo** (7th century) invites Buddhist teachers. King
  **Trisong Detsen** (8th century) invites **Shantarakshita** (abbot from Nalanda) and
  **Padmasambhava** (Guru Rinpoche). **Samye**, the first monastery, is built c. 775-779 (sources
  vary). The Samye debate favors the Indian "gradual" approach over the Chinese "sudden" one.
  The **Nyingma** ("ancient") school traces itself to this period.
- Persecution under King Langdarma, c. 838-842; the empire collapses.
- **Later diffusion**: from the late 10th century. **Atisha** arrives in 1042; his disciple
  Dromton founds the Kadampa. **Marpa** the translator (1012-1097 [bg]) studies with **Naropa**;
  **Milarepa** (c. 1052-1135 [bg]); **Gampopa** (1079-1153 [bg]) founds the **Kagyu** on a
  Kadampa base. **Sakya** founded 1073. [bg] **Tsongkhapa** (1357-1419) founds the **Gelug**. [bg]
- Four main schools today: Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug (plus Bön, often counted as a fifth).
- Nyingma also carries **terma**: "treasure" teachings hidden (tradition says by Padmasambhava)
  and revealed later by **tertöns**.

**2. Core structure** [bg for most of this list]
- Mahayana base: emptiness (shunyata), compassion, the bodhisattva vow, **buddha nature** (every
  being already has awakened nature, covered by temporary obscurations).
- Tantra: deity yoga, mantra, mandala, guru yoga, empowerment. Four classes of tantra (action,
  performance, yoga, highest yoga).
- Two stages: **generation** (visualize oneself as the deity) and **completion** (work with the
  subtle body: channels, winds, drops; 2.3).
- Highest views: **Mahamudra** (Kagyu) and **Dzogchen** (Nyingma and Bön): the mind's nature is
  already awake; rest in it. **Rainbow body** ('ja' lus): Dzogchen's claimed sign of complete
  realization at death.
- **Vajra and lotus (or bell)**: the vajra (diamond, thunderbolt) stands for method or
  compassion, often male; the lotus or bell for wisdom, often female; their union is the goal.
  "Om mani padme hum" (Avalokiteshvara's mantra) is usually glossed "the jewel in the lotus".
- **Five Buddha families**: five poisons (anger, pride, desire, jealousy, ignorance) are
  transformed, not discarded, into five wisdoms.

**3. How it sees**
- The nature of mind is already clear light. The problem is obscuration, not lack.
- Emotion is raw energy that can be **transformed** into wisdom instead of suppressed.
- Reading a person: which of the five poisons dominates (and therefore which wisdom is
  latent)? What is covering what is already there?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "This anger is also clarity, mis-aimed. What is it seeing?"

## 2.3 The Tibetan subtle body: tsa, lung, tigle

**1. Origin and history**
- Comes from the Indian Buddhist highest yoga tantras (Hevajra, Chakrasamvara, Guhyasamaja,
  Kalachakra, c. 8th-11th centuries [bg]), carried into Tibet in both diffusions, and shared in
  form with Bön.
- Tibetan practice of body movement with breath is **trul khor** (magical wheel); **tsa lung**
  names the channel-and-wind exercises.

**2. Core structure**
- **Tsa** (Skt. nadi): channels. **Lung** (Skt. prana, vayu): wind, the moving energy. **Tigle**
  (thig le, Skt. bindu): drops, the "sparks of life" that circulate in the channels, carried by
  the wind (Tsoknyi Rinpoche's summary).
- **Three main channels**: **uma** (central, Skt. avadhuti), **roma** (Skt. rasana) and
  **kyangma** (Skt. lalana), right and left. Central often visualized blue, just in front of the
  spine. Colors and sides of the side channels vary by lineage and by sex (Bön handout in 2.1:
  right white, left red). [Buddhist color assignments not verified this pass]
- **Khorlo** (chakras), usually four or five, not seven. Gampopa's four (as summarized for tummo):
  navel, the emanation wheel, 64 spokes; heart, the dharma wheel, 8 spokes; throat, the
  enjoyment wheel, 16 [bg]; crown, the great-bliss wheel, 32 [bg]. Five-chakra systems add the
  secret place; Kalachakra uses six. Channel "knots" at the centers, especially the heart,
  constrict the central channel. [bg]
- **Five root winds** (also used in Tibetan medicine) [bg]: life-sustaining (srog 'dzin, head and
  chest: breath, swallowing, clarity of mind); upward-moving (gyen rgyu, chest and throat:
  speech, effort, memory); pervasive (khyab byed, heart, whole body: movement); fire-accompanying
  (me mnyam, stomach: digestion); downward-clearing (thur sel, lower body: elimination, birth).
  They parallel the Indian five vayus. Five branch winds govern the senses.
- **Drops**: the white drop (from the father, at the crown), the red drop (from the mother, at
  the navel), and the indestructible drop at the heart, which holds the very subtle mind and
  wind until death. [bg]
- Principle: **mind rides on wind** like a rider on a horse. Steady the wind and the mind
  steadies. [bg]
- Completion-stage aim: draw the winds into the central channel, where they dissolve and the
  clear light mind appears.

**3. How it sees**
- Disturbed mind is disturbed wind. Wind out of its channel produces agitation; wind gathered in
  the center produces bliss and clarity.
- Reading a person: where is the wind stuck, scattered or rising? Where is the knot?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Before working the thought, work the wind (the breath and the energy
  under it)."

**4. Correspondences with MOB**

- CANDIDATE

  - **Winds into the central channel** and MOB's kundalini chain: "Spine clear enough for the

**5. Status**

## 2.4 Tummo (inner heat)

**1. Origin and history**
- Sanskrit **candali** ("fierce woman"); in the Indian tantras, for example the Hevajra. [bg]
- First and foundation of the **Six Yogas (Six Dharmas) of Naropa**: tummo (inner heat), gyulu
  (illusory body), milam (dream), ösel (clear light), bardo (in-between), phowa (transference of
  consciousness at death). Naropa's dates are given as 1016-1100 or 1016-1111.
- Lineage: Tilopa to Naropa to Marpa to Milarepa. Milarepa is famed for living in the Himalayan
  cold wearing only cotton ("repa", cotton-clad). [bg]

**2. Core structure** [bg for practice details]
- Posture, **vase breathing** (holding the breath low in the belly), and visualization: a flame
  (a short syllable A) at the navel blazes up the central channel and melts the drop (HAM) at
  the crown; the drop descends through the centers, producing four joys.
- Heat is a by-product. The aim is bliss joined with emptiness.

**3. How it sees**
- Gather the scattered winds into the center and the body's own fire burns away obstruction.
- Thinking move for Emmy: "What would this look like if the energy were gathered instead of
  scattered?"

**4. Correspondences with MOB**
- CANON: the word tummo does not appear. L5210: "a yogi sat on a rock, froze his body, and
  turned his mind into a furnace to burn up every thought until only awareness remained." This
  is one of the two stories that started the work ("Everything that follows came from following
  that thread", L5212).
- CANDIDATE
  - The yogi of L5210 matches tummo legends (Milarepa type). For Lance: is this story tummo?

  - Rising fire and descending drop, and MOB's rising vital energy root to crown. Note the

**5. Status**

  breathing) producing heat and a neurocognitive component (visualization) sustaining it.

## 2.5 The bardo teachings

**1. Origin and history**
- **Bardo** means "in-between". Indian Buddhist Abhidharma debated an intermediate existence
  (antarabhava) between death and rebirth; some schools accepted it. [bg]
- **Bardo Thodol** ("Liberation through Hearing in the In-between"), part of the Zhitro
  (peaceful and wrathful deities) cycle: tradition says Padmasambhava composed it in the 8th
  century and Yeshe Tsogyal buried it; the tertön **Karma Lingpa** (1326-1386) revealed it in the
  14th century.
- W. Y. Evans-Wentz published it in English as *The Tibetan Book of the Dead* in 1927, from a
  translation by Kazi Dawa Samdup. [bg] Later translations by Francesca Fremantle and Chögyam
  Trungpa (1975), Robert Thurman (1994), Gyurme Dorje (complete, 2005). [bg]

**2. Core structure**
- **Six bardos** (Karma Lingpa's root verses): life (from birth to death), dream, meditation,
  dying (chikhai), dharmata or luminosity (chonyid), becoming (sidpa).
- **Dying**: the elements dissolve in order (earth into water, water into fire, fire into wind,
  wind into consciousness) with inner signs (mirage, smoke, sparks, a flame); then three visions,
  white appearance, red increase, black near-attainment; then the **clear light** of death. [bg
  on the sequence details]
- **Chonyid**: peaceful and wrathful deities appear; they are recognized as the mind's own display.
- **Sidpa**: a mental body moves by karmic wind, drawn toward parents and a womb. The in-between
  lasts up to **49 days** (seven weeks), with rites each week. [bg]
- **Phowa**: transferring consciousness out through the crown at death. **Tukdam**: a master
  rests in the clear light after clinical death, body said to stay fresh. **Tulku**: a master who
  chooses a rebirth and is recognized again. [bg]
- Buddhist doctrine of **no permanent self** (anatman): what continues is a mindstream carried by
  very subtle wind, with karmic imprints (bakchak, Skt. vasana). [bg]

**3. How it sees**
- Every gap (between death and birth, sleep and waking, one thought and the next) is a chance to
  recognize mind's nature. Unrecognized, habit takes over and drives the next form.
- Reading a person: what is driving them in this in-between? Habit or recognition?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "This transition is a bardo. What will carry through it unless it is seen?"

## 2.6 The five elements

**1. Origin and history**
- Indian: the five **mahabhutas**: space (akasha), air (vayu), fire (agni or tejas), water
  (apas), earth (prithvi). Taittiriya Upanishad 2.1 gives their emergence in that order: from the
  Self space, from space air, from air fire, from fire water, from water earth. [bg] Samkhya
  places them last of the 25 tattvas (1.6).
- Tibetan: **jungwa nga**: earth (sa), water (chu), fire (me), wind (lung), space (namkha).
  Especially central in Bön (Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche, *Healing with Form, Energy and Light*,
  2002 [bg]).
- Not to be confused with the Chinese **five phases** (wood, fire, earth, metal, water), which
  Tibetan "black" astrology uses (2.8).

**2. Core structure** [bg]
- Each element has a quality: earth stability, water cohesion and flow, fire warmth and
  ripening, wind movement, space openness.
- They build the body (Tibetan medicine: wind is lung; fire is tripa; earth and water are
  beken), and they dissolve in order at death (2.5).
- In Bön, each element is also a quality of mind that can be lost and retrieved in practice.

**3. How it sees**
- Read anything (a body, a mood, a room) as a balance of five qualities.
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Too much fire? Not enough earth? Where is the space?"

**4. Correspondences with MOB**
- CANON
  - L6270-6272: "Elemental Meditation. Elements: Water, fire, air, space, earth. How you are
    similar and different from each. Ends with letting go of believing you are any element."

  - L3757: "The elements, the body, the mind..." listed among the conceptual koshas.

**5. Status**

## 2.7 Tibetan medicine, Sowa Rigpa (brief)

**1. Origin and history**
- Root text: the **Gyushi** (rGyud bzhi, *Four Tantras*). Attributed to **Yuthok Yonten Gonpo
  the Elder** (8th-9th centuries in the tradition's dating) and edited into its present form by
  **Yuthok the Younger** (1126-1202). Some accounts say it was hidden and rediscovered in the
  11th century.
- **Desi Sangye Gyatso** (1653-1705 [bg]) wrote the *Blue Beryl* commentary and commissioned a
  famous series of medical paintings; a 1920 copy kept in Ulan-Ude was published in facsimile
  with a translation of the *Blue Beryl* in 1992.
- In exile: the Men-Tsee-Khang, Dharamsala (1961 [bg]). India recognized Sowa Rigpa in its
  AYUSH system (2010 [bg]).

**2. Core structure**
- Four tantras: Root (summary), Explanatory (anatomy, physiology, pharmacology), Instructional
  (diagnosis and treatment), Subsequent (pulse, urine, compounding).
- **Three nyepa** ("faults" or "dynamics", often "humors"): **lung** (wind), **tripa** (bile),
  **beken** (phlegm).
- Root causes: the **three mental poisons**. Desire or attachment feeds lung; anger or aversion
  feeds tripa; ignorance or delusion feeds beken. [bg on the exact pairing]
- Diagnosis: looking (including tongue and urine), pulse, questioning. Pulse and urine are the
  specialties.
- **Lung disorders** are the stress, worry and nervous complaints of the system. [bg]

**3. How it sees**
- The body's illness grows from the mind's poisons. Treat diet, behavior, medicine and therapy
  in that order. [bg]
- Reading a person: which dynamic is out (wind, bile, phlegm), and which poison is feeding it?
- Thinking move for Emmy: "Which emotion is fueling this body problem?"

**4. Correspondences with MOB**

**5. Status**
- Tradition holds: a complete medical science.

## 2.8 Tibetan astrology, tsi (brief)

- The **rabjung** 60-year cycle (12 animals with 5 elements) starts in 1027; the Chinese
  sexagenary cycle runs three years ahead of it.
- Bön holds that both branches come from the Kalachakra.
- Practiced alongside medicine; the Men-Tsee-Khang trains both. [bg]

**2. Core structure** [bg]
- Kartsi: planets, lunar mansions, eclipses, the calendar.
- Nagtsi: 12 animals, 5 Chinese elements (wood, fire, earth, metal, water), 8 trigrams

- A personal reading weighs five forces for the year: **la** (soul), **sok** (life force),
  **lu** (body, health), **wang** (power), **lungta** (wind horse, luck and uplift).

**3. How it sees**
