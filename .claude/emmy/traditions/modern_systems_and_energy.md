## 2. Gene Keys

### 2.1 Origin and history

- **Author:** Richard Rudd (British). His own biography describes a spontaneous awakening in 1996,
  after which he became an early student of Human Design under Ra Uru Hu. [V] Secondary sources say he
  spent about seven years studying, teaching and writing about Human Design, with one dating it to
  1997 to 2004. [V, secondary]
- **The book:** *The Gene Keys: Unlocking the Higher Purpose Hidden in Your DNA*, first published in
  2009. [V, genekeys.com] A wider trade edition came from Watkins in 2013. [K]
- **Lineage:** Rudd's own account says Human Design and the I Ching informed "the greater synthesis
  that became The Gene Keys". [V] The organization now runs the Golden Path program and other
  courses. [V]
- **The DNA framing** rests on a numerical parallel: 64 I Ching hexagrams and 64 genetic codons. It was
  first noted by Gunther Stent (1969) and developed by the physician Martin Schönberger, *Verborgener
  Schlüssel zum Leben* (1973), published in English as *The I Ching and the Genetic Code*.
  [V, secondary]
- **Origin story versus documented history:** the book is presented as coming from contemplation and
  inner realization. What is documented is the authorship, the 2009 date, and the debt to Human
  Design and the I Ching.

## 3. Crystal energy and crystal healing

### 3.1 Origin and history

**Older stone lore** [K unless marked]
- Stones have been used as amulets in Mesopotamia, Egypt, China and India for thousands of years.
- Theophrastus, *On Stones* (about 315 BCE), is the first Greek treatise on minerals.
- Pliny the Elder, *Natural History* (completed 77 CE), devotes Book 37 to gems, including uses
  attributed to them as remedies.
- Medieval lapidaries: Marbode of Rennes, *Liber lapidum* (late 11th century), and Hildegard of
  Bingen's *Physica* (12th century), which includes stones used in healing. [V, secondary, for
  Hildegard]
- Indian Jyotisha prescribes gems for planets (navaratna, the nine gems).
- George Frederick Kunz, *The Curious Lore of Precious Stones* (1913), compiled this lore for
  modern readers. [V]

**Modern crystal healing**
- 19th-century occultism, especially Theosophy's teaching that matter and spirit are continuous, laid
  the groundwork. [V, secondary]
- The practice peaked with the New Age movement of the 1980s, when crystals were among the most
  popular items at New Age stores and fairs. Drawing on the Atlantis myth, the channeler Frank Alper
  proposed crystals as healing tools. Britannica's account spells the name "Alpert". [V, Britannica]
- Influential figures [K]: Katrina Raphaell, *Crystal Enlightenment* (1985), and Marcel Vogel, an IBM
  research scientist who promoted specially cut quartz for healing.

**Where the stone-to-chakra charts come from**
- The seven-chakra system reached the West mainly through John Woodroffe (Arthur Avalon), *The
  Serpent Power* (1918 or 1919; sources differ). [V]
- The rainbow color scheme (root red through crown violet) is a 20th-century Western development.
  It is attributed variously to Leadbeater (1927) or to Christopher Hills, *Nuclear Evolution* (1977).
  Kurt Leland's *Rainbow Body* (2016) traces this history. [V] Stone charts mostly match stones to
  chakras by these colors.

### 3.2 Core structure

**A common stone-to-chakra chart** [K. Charts vary by author. Matching by color is the usual logic.]

| Chakra | Common stones |
|---|---|
| Root | red jasper, garnet, hematite, black tourmaline, smoky quartz |
| Sacral | carnelian, orange calcite |
| Solar plexus | citrine, tiger's eye |
| Heart | rose quartz, green aventurine, jade, malachite |
| Throat | blue lace agate, aquamarine, sodalite |
| Third eye | amethyst, lapis lazuli, sodalite |
| Crown | clear quartz, amethyst, selenite |

**Methods.** Stones are laid on the body over the chakras, held, worn, set out in grids, or used in
"elixirs". Some stones are toxic in water; malachite contains copper. [K] Stones are "cleansed" and
"charged" (with moonlight, sound or smoke) and "programmed" with intention.

**Claimed mechanism.** A stone carries a vibration or frequency that interacts with the body's energy
field. Quartz is said to amplify intention. Piezoelectricity is often cited as support.

**The real physics of crystals**
- **Piezoelectricity:** Pierre and Jacques Curie found in 1880 that squeezing certain crystals,
  quartz most strongly, produces an electric charge. Lippmann predicted the reverse effect (an electric
  field deforms the crystal) in 1881, and the Curies confirmed it. [V]
- **Quartz oscillators:** in a feedback circuit, a quartz crystal vibrates at its natural resonant
  frequency, which is set by its cut and size. This has been used for timekeeping since the 1920s.
  [V] Watch crystals typically run at 32,768 Hz (2^15), which binary dividers reduce to exactly
  1 Hz. [K]
- Every solid object has natural mechanical resonant frequencies, set by its size, shape and material.
  They ring when struck or driven. A crystal resting in the hand is not driven and has not been
  measured emitting a sustained frequency. [K] Piezoelectric output needs changing mechanical stress.

### 3.3 How it sees

- Matter carries subtle energy. Correspondence works by color, form (points, spheres, skulls) and
  mineral family. The practitioner's sensitivity is the instrument. Intention plus stone amplifies.
  Like resonates with like.
- **What Emmy learns to think with:** the logic of correspondence, and reading by attending to felt
  response. Emmy also holds the documented power of expectation and suggestion over felt sensations

### 3.5 Status

## 4. Resonance, frequency and sound

Five topics, each with history, structure and evidence, followed by shared sections on how this
family of ideas sees and how it corresponds with MOB.

### 4A. Solfeggio frequencies

- **Documented history:** around 1025 to 1033, Guido of Arezzo used the hymn *Ut queant laxis* (for
  John the Baptist, text attributed to Paul the Deacon, 8th century) as a memory aid for singers. The
  first syllable of each line gave ut, re, mi, fa, sol, la. [K] These pitches were relative, with no
  fixed pitch standard. Pitch was not measured as a frequency until the 17th century (Mersenne), and
  the hertz unit was adopted in 1930. [K] "Ut" later became "do", and "si" (later "ti") was added. [K]
- **The modern "Solfeggio frequencies"** are 396, 417, 528, 639, 741 and 852 Hz. Joseph Puleo derived
  them by reducing verse numbers from the Book of Numbers, chapter 7, to digits. They were published in
  Leonard Horowitz and Puleo, *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse* (1999). [V] Horowitz
  promoted 528 Hz as "the love frequency" and as repairing DNA. [V] Later sellers added 174, 285 and
  963 Hz to make nine tones. [K] No historical record links these hertz values to Gregorian chant. [V]
- **Evidence**
  - Akimoto et al. 2018 (*Health*): 9 participants heard 528 Hz and 440 Hz music on separate days.
    After 528 Hz, cortisol fell significantly and oxytocin rose. After 440 Hz, no marker changed
    significantly. This is a very small study. [V]
  - Babayi and Riazi 2017 (*Journal of Addiction Research and Therapy*): a cell-culture study, not a
    human one. [V]
  - A 2026 randomized trial of 162 students (528 Hz, 432 Hz, control): 528 Hz raised salivary BDNF
    and CREB markers compared with control, with no difference in Stroop test performance. The lead
    author said the data do not support claims that a frequency heals. [V, secondary]
  - No peer-reviewed evidence shows DNA repair by 528 Hz in a living body. [V] 396 and 528 Hz
    do match notes of a just-intoned C scale, which is a musical fact, not evidence of healing. [V]

### 4B. 432 Hz

- **Documented history**
  - Concert pitch varied widely for centuries. Baroque pitch was often around A = 415 Hz. [V,
    secondary] France fixed its *diapason normal* at A = 435 Hz in 1859. [K] The 1885 Vienna conference
    recommended 435 Hz. [V] Verdi is often said to have won an 1884 regulation at 432 Hz; only one
    source in this pass supports that, and it is contested. [V, single source]
  - In May 1939 a conference at BBC Broadcasting House in London agreed on A = 440 Hz. The ISO
    adopted it as Recommendation R 16 in 1955 and as ISO 16 in 1975, which is still current. [V]
  - The Schiller Institute campaigned for "Verdi tuning", C = 256 Hz and A about 432 Hz (the
    Pythagorean ratio 27:16), without success. [V] The campaign's start in the late 1980s is [K].
- **Claims:** 432 is "natural" or "in tune with the universe" and opens the heart. In the strongest
  version, 440 was imposed to cause harm, often linked to Nazi Germany. [K] The documented record of
  A = 440 runs through an international standards conference and the ISO, and this pass found no
  documentary support for the conspiracy.
- **Size of the difference:** 432 and 440 Hz are about 32 cents apart, less than a third of a
  semitone. (1200 x log2(440/432) = 31.8 cents.)
- **Evidence**
  - Calamassi and Pomponi 2019 (*Explore* 15:283-290): a double-blind crossover pilot study with 33
    healthy volunteers. With 432 Hz music, mean heart rate was 4.79 beats per minute lower than with
    440 Hz, at p = 0.05, which is borderline. Blood pressure did not differ significantly, and
    respiratory rate showed a small trend (p = 0.06). The authors called for larger trials. [V,
    secondary] The study's originator reported that 440 Hz music triggered his migraines, a possible
    source of bias. [V]
  - 2025 *PeerJ* study of kickboxers (double-blind crossover): 440 Hz warm-up music gave the lowest
    mean heart rate and 432 Hz came second, so it did not reproduce the 2019 direction. [V]

### 4C. Schumann resonance

- **Documented history:** Winfried Otto Schumann gave the first rigorous treatment in 1952. He predicted
  that electromagnetic waves resonate in the cavity between Earth's surface and the ionosphere. He and
  H. L. König attempted measurements in 1952 to 1954. Balser and Wagner (MIT Lincoln Laboratory) made
  the first definitive measurements in 1960. [V]
- **Structure:** lightning, about 50 strikes per second worldwide [K], excites the cavity. The
  fundamental is about 7.83 Hz, with higher modes near 14.3, 20.8, 27.3 and 33.8 Hz. [V] The exact
  values shift with ionospheric height and conductivity, time of day, season, and solar and
  geomagnetic activity. [V] It is an electromagnetic phenomenon, not a sound and not a biological
  signal. [V] Its magnetic amplitude is very small, on the order of a picotesla. [K]
- **Claims:** it is called "Earth's heartbeat" and a natural tuning reference for the human brain,
  because 7.83 Hz sits near the theta/alpha boundary. Claims also circulate that it is "rising" or
  "spiking".
- **Evidence:** measured values move by fractions of a hertz around the mean. This pass could not
  search the geophysics literature on a long-term rise and found no peer-reviewed report of one. The
  "spike" images online are commonly misread spectrograms. [K] That the frequency overlaps an EEG band
  is a numerical coincidence. Proposed biological couplings remain speculative. [K]

### 4D. Binaural beats and brainwave entrainment

- **Documented history:** Heinrich Wilhelm Dove described binaural beats in 1839. [K] Gerald Oster,
  "Auditory beats in the brain", *Scientific American*, October 1973, revived interest. [K] Commercial
  use followed, notably Robert Monroe's Hemi-Sync from the Monroe Institute. [K]
- **Mechanism (established):** two tones of slightly different frequency, one in each ear through
  headphones (for example 200 Hz left and 210 Hz right), are heard as a beat at the difference
  (10 Hz). The beat is not in the air. The brainstem (superior olivary complex) constructs it, and it
  works best with tones roughly below 1,000 to 1,500 Hz. [K] Monaural and isochronic beats are related
  methods. [K]
- **EEG bands** (conventions; the boundaries vary by lab) [K]: delta about 0.5 to 4 Hz, theta 4 to
  8, alpha 8 to 12, beta 13 to 30, gamma above 30.
- **Evidence**
  - Garcia-Argibay, Santed and Reales 2019 (*Psychological Research*), a meta-analysis: binaural beats
    had pooled effects on memory, attention, anxiety and pain perception. A later review cites a
    medium effect on pain during surgery, from only three surgical studies. [V, secondary]
  - Ingendoh, Posny and Heine 2023 (*PLOS ONE*), a systematic review of EEG evidence: of 14 studies,
    5 supported entrainment, 8 contradicted it and 1 was mixed. Methods varied too much to pool the
    results. [V]
  - **Bottom line:** some subjective and behavioral effects are reported. The claim that the brain
    reliably locks onto the beat frequency is not established.

### 4E. Cymatics

- **Documented history:** Ernst Chladni, *Entdeckungen über die Theorie des Klanges* (1787), drew a
  bow across sand-covered metal plates. The sand gathered along the nodal lines, where the plate does
  not move, forming "Chladni figures". [K] Hans Jenny (1904 to 1972), a Swiss physician influenced by
  Rudolf Steiner's anthroposophy, coined "cymatics" (from the Greek *kyma*, wave) in *Kymatik* (1967),
  and a second volume followed. He drove plates and membranes covered with sand, liquids and pastes
  using oscillators and a "tonoscope". [K]
- **Physics (established):** these are standing waves. The pattern depends on frequency, the plate's
  shape and material, and how it is held, and higher frequencies give more complex figures. The
  patterns are real and reproducible. [K]
- **Claims beyond physics:** that sound shapes all form, and that "cymatic" therapy devices heal
  (for example Peter Guy Manners' cyma therapy). [K] This pass found no controlled trials.

### 4.3 How it sees (shared across 4A to 4E)

- The universe as vibration: health is harmony, illness is dissonance, like resonates with like, and
  numbers carry meaning (numerology applied to hertz). Sound is a lever for changing state.
- Real physics sits underneath part of this. **Sympathetic resonance** is real: a struck tuning fork
  drives a matched fork through the air. [K] **Entrainment** is real in coupled oscillators: Huygens
  saw two pendulum clocks fall into step in 1665. [K] For any claim, the test is whether the coupling
  exists in the body and has been measured.
- **What Emmy learns to think with:** the physical vocabulary (frequency, resonance, entrainment,
  standing wave, node, beat), and the habit of asking in each sentence whether a word is literal
  (measured) or analogical (felt).

## 5. Biofield, energy medicine, and heart-rate variability coherence

### 5.1 Origin and history

Everything in this section is [K] unless marked. The web-search budget ran out before this
section could be re-verified.

- **"Biofield":** the term was adopted at a 1992 workshop of the NIH Office of Alternative Medicine
  (Chantilly, Virginia) and published in the 1994 report *Alternative Medicine: Expanding Medical
  Horizons*. The report defines it as "a massless field, not necessarily electromagnetic, that
  surrounds and permeates living bodies and affects the body". Later work includes Beverly Rubik, "The
  biofield hypothesis" (*Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine*, 2002), and Jain et al.,
  "Biofield science and healing: history, terminology, and concepts" (*Global Advances in Health and
  Medicine*, 2015). The NIH's complementary-health center (NCCIH) files Reiki and Therapeutic Touch
  under biofield or energy therapies.
- **Reiki:** Mikao Usui, Japan, 1922, after a retreat on Mount Kurama. Chujiro Hayashi carried it on,
  and Hawayo Takata brought it to Hawaii in the late 1930s; most Western lineages descend from her.
  The practitioner places hands on or just above the body, and students receive "attunements" through
  levels.
- **Therapeutic Touch:** Dolores Krieger and Dora Kunz, early 1970s.
- **Quantum-Touch:** Richard Gordon, 1990s.
- **Biophotons:** Alexander Gurwitsch proposed "mitogenetic rays" in 1923, and Fritz-Albert Popp coined
  "biophoton" in the 1970s. Ultra-weak photon emission from living tissue is real. Photomultipliers
  and cooled cameras measure it. It is far too faint for the eye and is attributed mostly to oxidative
  metabolism. Kobayashi et al. (2009) imaged it from the human body and found a daily rhythm. Its role
  as a signaling or information field is not established.
- **The heart's fields:** the heart's electrical activity is recorded by the ECG (Einthoven, 1903). Its
  magnetic field was first recorded by Baule and McFee in 1963, and with SQUID magnetometers by David
  Cohen around 1970. At the chest it is tens of picotesla: roughly 100 times the brain's magnetic
  field, and roughly a million times weaker than Earth's field (about 50 microtesla). It falls off
  steeply with distance, and clinical magnetocardiography measures it centimeters from the chest,
  usually in shielded rooms. HeartMath states that it is detectable several feet away.
- **HeartMath:** the Institute of HeartMath, a nonprofit founded in 1991 by Doc Childre in Boulder
  Creek, California, popularized "heart coherence".
- **HRV and resonance breathing:** heart-rate variability is the beat-to-beat change in heart rate.
  Paul Lehrer and Evgeny Vaschillo developed HRV biofeedback at a "resonance frequency" of breathing,
  about 4.5 to 7 breaths per minute (typically near 6, which is 0.1 Hz). At that rate the breathing
  rhythm and the blood-pressure reflex reinforce each other.

### 5.2 Core structure

- **HeartMath coherence** [K]: in the HRV power spectrum, a coherent state shows one dominant peak
  near 0.1 Hz (searched within 0.04 to 0.26 Hz). The coherence ratio is peak power divided by (total
  power minus peak power) (McCraty et al., *The Coherent Heart*, 2009). The core technique, "Quick
  Coherence", combines heart-focused paced breathing with a renewing feeling. The devices are emWave
  and Inner Balance.
- **Standard HRV measures** (Task Force guidelines, 1996) [K]: time-domain measures such as SDNN and
  RMSSD, and frequency bands LF (0.04 to 0.15 Hz) and HF (0.15 to 0.4 Hz). A chest strap gives these
  numbers, and so, with less accuracy, does a phone camera reading pulse (PPG).
- **Reiki structure** [K]: three degrees (I, II, master), symbols, attunements and set hand positions.
- **"Biofield"** is an umbrella term for research, not an instrument or a single measured quantity.

### 5.3 How it sees

- **Energy medicine:** health is the flow of a life energy (ki, qi, prana) that can be blocked and

- **HeartMath:** emotion shows up as rhythm. Paced breathing plus a positive feeling gives an orderly
