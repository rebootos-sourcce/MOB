# PART 1: MARKETERS

## 1. Seth Godin

**Who and why.** Author and teacher who defined permission marketing. The lens for consent, and
for choosing the few people Atüned is for.

**Background.** Stanford MBA. Yahoo bought his permission-marketing company Yoyodyne in 1998 for
$29.6 million, and he became Yahoo's vice president of direct marketing. Books: *Permission
Marketing* (1999), *Purple Cow* (2003), *Tribes* (2008), *This Is Marketing* (2018). He founded
the altMBA workshop in 2015.

**How he thinks.** His blog, January 2008: "Permission marketing is the privilege (not the
right) of delivering anticipated, personal and relevant messages to people who actually want to
get them." The method:
1. **Pick the smallest viable audience** (*This Is Marketing*): the smallest group, defined by a
   shared worldview rather than demographics, that can sustain the work.
2. **Offer something for attention**, so a stranger volunteers to hear more.
3. **Teach over time**, each message building on the last.
4. **Widen permission** with more value, never by assuming it.
5. **Climb the ladder** (the book's five levels, least to most): situational, brand trust,
   personal relationship, points, intravenous (the marketer acts for the customer, like
   automatic refills). Wikipedia notes the levels are not a required sequence.

**What can be automated.** A consent record per person (what they agreed to, when, where), the
teaching as a sequenced email course, frequency caps, instant unsubscribe, a weekly report of
replies and unsubscribes per letter. Choosing the worldview stays human.

**Quality bar.**
1. Every message goes only to people who asked for that kind of message, in plain words.
2. The reader would miss it if it stopped.
3. The audience is named by a belief, narrow enough to leave someone out.
4. Each rung is earned by something given.

**Lens questions.**
- Who asked to hear this, and what exactly did they agree to?
- Who is this for, and who is it not for?
- What does this letter teach that the last did not?
- If we stopped sending, who would write to ask why?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Godin lens: a buyer of *The Mechanics of Being* has not agreed to app promotion.
  Ask once, in the book's back pages, and keep the two lists separate.
- Through the Godin lens: a subscription is the top rung. The renewal reminder carries the date
  and the price before the charge.

**Where it crosses into manipulation, and Emmy's line.** Permission is easy to fake: pre-ticked
boxes, consent buried in terms, a download that silently enrolls you, points that build a habit
loop. Emmy's line: consent is explicit, for one purpose, and as easy to withdraw as to give.

**Sources.**
- https://seths.blog/2008/01/permission-mark/ (the quoted definition)
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permission_marketing
- https://impactplus.com/blog/permission-marketing-seth-godin-dating-your-customer
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Godin
- https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/311779/this-is-marketing-by-godin-seth/9780241370155
- https://www.shortform.com/blog/this-is-marketing-seth-godin/

**Uncertain.** Steps 2 to 4 come from secondary summaries of the book. The ladder's order varies
between summaries. Whether Yoyodyne had a co-founder (one source names Mark Hurst) is unsettled.

**Better candidates?** Kevin Kelly, "1,000 True Fans" (2008, later updated). Wired co-founder and
its first executive editor; the essay argues a creator can live from about a thousand devoted
buyers reached directly, which fits a founder selling a book and an app. It is one essay, not a
method, and Dave Karpf calls its core hollow. A complement, not a replacement. Lance decides.
(https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/1-000-true-fans/24471,
https://davekarpf.substack.com/p/the-hollow-core-of-kevin-kellys-thousand)

## 2. April Dunford

**Who and why.** Positioning consultant and former startup marketing executive. The lens for
"what is Atüned, compared with what, and for whom."

**Background.** She ran marketing and product teams at a series of technology startups, then
became a board member, investor and adviser. *Obviously Awesome* (Ambient Press, 2019). *Sales
Pitch* (2023) sets out an eight-step pitch. A second edition of *Obviously Awesome* exists.

**How she thinks.** As quoted in a book summary: "Positioning is the act of deliberately
defining how you are the best at something that a defined market cares a lot about." It comes
before messaging, and every product lands in some category whether you choose it or not. The
ten steps (first edition, from a third-party summary):
1. Understand the customers who love your product.
2. Form a positioning team.
3. Align vocabulary and let go of old positioning baggage.
4. List your true competitive alternatives: what buyers would really use instead.
5. Isolate your unique attributes.
6. Map attributes to value themes.
7. Decide who cares a lot (the best-fit customers).
8. Choose a market frame of reference that puts your strengths at the center, and a style:
   head to head, big fish in a small pond, or a new game.
9. Layer on a trend, carefully.
10. Capture the positioning so everyone can use it, and revisit it as the market moves.

**What can be automated.** Steps 1 and 7: pull who stays and pays, and what they share. Steps 4
to 6: a versioned worksheet Emmy fills and Lance edits. Step 10: the positioning kept as a canon
file every page of copy is checked against, the way the gate checks the build. The category
choice stays human.

**Quality bar.**
1. Alternatives come from what real customers did before, in their words.
2. Every value claim traces to an attribute only Atüned has.
3. A stranger could find the best-fit customer from the description.
4. The category makes the strength obvious; a trend is a layer, never the base.

**Lens questions.**
- If Atüned did not exist, what would this person do instead?
- What can Atüned do that those alternatives cannot, and so what?
- Who cares about that a lot?
- What category makes it obvious in five seconds?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Dunford lens: "body-and-spirit app" lands beside free, famous meditation apps. Ask
  five real users what they used before, then pick a frame with "the mechanism explained" at the
  center. The paying-panel model hints at that reason, but it is a model; confirm first.
- Through the Dunford lens: decide whether the book or the app is the way in. Not both.

**Where it crosses into manipulation, and Emmy's line.** Positioning picks the comparison, which
invites straw-man alternatives, a made-up category that dodges comparison, and "the only" claims
nobody can check. Emmy's line: free alternatives named honestly, "only" provable, and no
category label that implies treatment or therapy.

**Sources.**
- https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/10-step-positioning-process-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-3/
- https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2020/01/12/obviously-awesome/ (the quoted definition)
- https://www.shortform.com/blog/product-positioning-strategy-2/
- https://claudeskills.info/skills/getagentseal/founder-playbook/obviously-awesome/ (category styles; low-reliability summary)
- https://www.porchlightbooks.com/products/obviously-awesome-april-dunford-9781999023003
- https://pagetwo.com/book/sales-pitch/
- https://www.mindtheproduct.com/conferences/chicago/speakers/april-dunford/

**Uncertain.** Step wording is a summary's paraphrase. The second edition's date, and one
listing's "five-step process", are UNVERIFIED. The definition is quoted secondhand and variants
exist.

**Better candidates?** None stronger for positioning. Note her examples are business-to-business
software; step 8 needs consumer judgment for Atüned.

## 3. Sean Ellis

**Who and why.** Coined "growth hacker" in 2010. The lens for testing, tempo, and the one
question that says whether growth spending is premature.

**Background.** His publisher calls him CEO of Qualaroo and co-founder of GrowthHackers.com. His
own profile lists first marketer at Dropbox, Lookout and Xobni, and head of marketing at LogMeIn
and Uproar through their IPOs. *Hacking Growth: How Today's Fastest-Growing Companies Drive
Breakout Success*, with Morgan Brown (April 25, 2017).

**How he thinks.** Product first, then growth. Two tools.

**A. The product-market-fit survey.** Ask active users how they would feel if they could no
longer use the product: very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, not disappointed, or no longer
using it. Under 40 percent "very disappointed" means the product is not yet holding people. He
reportedly set the line by benchmarking about a hundred startups; MeasuringU found no
peer-reviewed validation, and Ellis has reportedly called it somewhat arbitrary.

**B. The growth cycle** (*Hacking Growth*):
1. Analyze data and gather insight.
2. Generate ideas.
3. Prioritize with ICE: Impact (how big a change), Confidence (how sure), Ease (how hard to
   run). Scores are a guide the growth lead adjusts.
4. Run the experiments.
5. Return to analysis, on a fixed one- or two-week cadence run by a one-hour weekly meeting.
   Start slow, then raise the tempo.

**What can be automated.** The survey sent after a set period of real use, scored live with n
shown and split by tier; an ICE-scored idea backlog; an experiment log (hypothesis, metric, stop
date, result); a weekly digest. The ICE judgments stay human.

**Quality bar.**
1. Every test has a hypothesis, one metric and a stop date before it starts.
2. The fit score comes from real active users, with n shown.
3. Losses are logged as carefully as wins.
4. No growth spend before the score says people stay.

**Lens questions.**
- What did we learn this week, and where is it written?
- What one number does this test move?
- How many real users answered, and who were they?
- What do the "very disappointed" people share, in their words?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Ellis lens: `MARKET_STUDY.md` says "64 percent of the panel pays". That panel is
  fourteen modeled people. It cannot stand in for the survey and must never appear in
  marketing. Run the real survey after launch and print n beside the percentage.
- Through the Ellis lens: survey the first cohort before buying ads for the book or the app.
  Under 40 percent, spend on the product. Mine the "very disappointed" answers for copy.

**Where it crosses into manipulation, and Emmy's line.** Growth hacking normalized contact
scraping, spam invites, fake scarcity and fear copy that "wins" a test. A test won by deceiving
is a loss. Emmy's line: no test the user would be upset to see designed, no testing of health
claims or fear, and referral messages go only where the user chose to send them.

**Sources.**
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/545936/hacking-growth-by-sean-ellis-and-morgan-brown/9781524722630/
- https://clarity.fm/seanellis
- https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/2017/04
- https://measuringu.com/product-market-fit-item/
- https://learningloop.io/plays/product-market-fit-survey
- https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-growth-hacking/
- https://startupnation.com/books/hacking-growth-book-excerpt/

**Uncertain.** "First marketer" at Eventbrite is disputed by his own podcast remark. The
hundred-startup benchmark and its 2009 date are secondhand. ICE's scoring scale is unconfirmed
against the book. The book's imprint is unconfirmed.

**Better candidates?** Brian Balfour. Former VP of Growth at HubSpot, founder of Reforge (its
first course co-created with Andrew Chen); argues growth comes from self-reinforcing loops and
that the best retention wins. Evidence is from profiles, not his own text. A retention
complement; Ellis stays for testing. (https://www.growthtalent.org/insights/brian-balfour-profile,
https://blog.useproof.com/brian-balfour-hubspot-reforge)

## 4. Brian Halligan

**Who and why.** Co-founder and longtime CEO of HubSpot, credited with coining "inbound
marketing". The lens for being found by helping, and for turning marketing into a machine of
free tools and content.

**Background.** MIT Sloan MBA. He founded HubSpot with Dharmesh Shah in 2006, and with Shah wrote
*Inbound Marketing: Get Found Using Google, Social Media, and Blogs* (Wiley, 2009). HubSpot's
Website Grader (February 2007) was a free tool that scored a site's online marketing, built to
bring links and leads to HubSpot; HubSpot reported over 2 million sites graded by late 2009
(self-reported). At INBOUND18 HubSpot swapped the funnel for a flywheel. Secondary profiles say
HubSpot went public in October 2014 and he stepped down as CEO in 2021.

**How he thinks.** Pull people in with help instead of interrupting them. The methodology:
1. **Attract**: useful content strangers find through search and social.
2. **Convert**: something worth an email (a guide, a free tool) via landing pages and forms.
3. **Close**: relevant follow-up until the lead buys.
4. **Delight**: keep helping customers until they become promoters, whose word feeds Attract.
The flywheel (2018) makes the loop explicit. A free tool is itself content: Website Grader
taught people something about their own site and opened the conversation.

**What can be automated.** Nearly all of it, which is HubSpot's business: content calendar,
search checks, form-to-list, nurture sequences, promoter tracking.

**Quality bar.**
1. Each piece helps a reader who never buys.
2. The free offer is worth having on its own.
3. Every follow-up is something the person asked for.
4. Customers keep being served, and their words feed the next piece.

**Lens questions.**
- What does someone type into search on the day they need this book or app?
- What can we give away that works without the app?
- Where does a reader go next after this piece?
- What does a happy customer tell a friend?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Halligan lens: a free self-check, Website Grader style, that teaches one mechanism
  from the book. Its result is computed honestly, says what it cannot tell you, and is not the
  diagnostic with the answer held behind the paywall.
- Through the Halligan lens: each idea in *The Mechanics of Being* becomes one article that
  fully answers one question, with the app as the next step, not the price of the answer.

**Where it crosses into manipulation, and Emmy's line.** Inbound slides into gated bait, quizzes
that say "you need us" whatever you answer, and nurture sequences that never stop. With
body-and-spirit material, a self-check rigged to alarm is harm. Emmy's line: free results are
real, no gated fear content, no rewarded reviews shown as spontaneous.

**Sources.**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Halligan
- https://www.abebooks.com/Inbound-Marketing-Get-Found-Using-Google/31575561095/bd
- https://www.hubspot.com/blog/bid/2551/hubspot-s-website-grader-selected-as-a-2007-mitx-awards-finalist
- https://www.hubspot.com/blog/bid/4056/250-000-companies-test-their-internet-marketing-acumen-with-website-grader
- https://hubspot.com/blog/bid/5539/Website-Grader-Analyzes-Over-2-Million-Sites
- https://www.hubspot.com/partner-news/revamped-inbound-certification-available
- https://www.webdew.com/blog/inbound-methodology
- https://resources.rework.com/vi/libraries/leadership-legends/brian-halligan-leadership (IPO and CEO change; low reliability)

**Uncertain.** The IPO month and the 2021 handover rest on low-reliability profiles. "Coined
inbound" rests on Wikipedia. The four-stage wording comes from partner blogs, not HubSpot pages.

**Better candidates?** Ryan Holiday, *Perennial Seller: The Art of Making and Marketing Work that
Lasts* (Portfolio, July 2017): on building a book or product that keeps selling for years. His
publisher cites his work with American Apparel and John Grisham. Closer to the book launch than
HubSpot's software roots. Lance decides.
(https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/534365/perennial-seller-by-ryan-holiday/)

## 5. Byron Sharp

**Who and why.** Professor and director of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute at the University of
South Australia. The lens for evidence over folklore: reach, memory, and being easy to buy.

**Background.** *How Brands Grow* (2010), reported to have sold over 120,000 copies. *How Brands
Grow: Part 2* with Jenni Romaniuk (2015; revised, Oxford University Press, 2021). He debated Mark
Ritson at the Festival of Marketing; Ritson later defended him in Marketing Week.

**How he thinks.** Brands grow mainly by penetration, more buyers, not deeper loyalty. Double
jeopardy: smaller brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers are slightly less loyal. Growth
needs **mental availability** (the chance of being noticed or thought of in buying situations)
and **physical availability** (being present where and when people buy). The method:
1. **Map category entry points**: the situations that send someone to the category. Ask why,
   when, where, with whom, with what.
2. **Build distinctive assets**: a few colors, shapes, sounds or phrases used the same way every
   time. Test fame (do buyers know it?) and uniqueness (do they link it to you?).
3. **Reach all category buyers, continuously**, not only loyalists.
4. **Be easy to buy**: present, prominent and relevant where people shop.
5. **Measure buyers and memory**, not only engagement.

**What can be automated.** An asset-consistency check on every ad and page (a marketing
`palette.py`), content tagged by entry point, reach reports, and a periodic recognition survey
with a real sample and n shown.

**Quality bar.**
1. With the name covered, a stranger could still tell it is Atüned.
2. It ties the brand to at least one named buying situation.
3. It reaches people who are not fans yet.
4. Growth claims rest on real buyer counts.

**Lens questions.**
- In what moment does someone reach for this, and are we there?
- Would this be recognized with the logo removed?
- Who are we reaching who has never heard of us?
- Is it available where these people already buy?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Sharp lens: Atüned's field and color tokens are an asset only if used the same
  way everywhere. The book cover and the app's first screen should share one distinctive
  element.
- Through the Sharp lens: write for moments, not features (the end of a hard day, before
  sleep), each tied to a real tool in the app, with no promised outcome.

**Where it crosses into manipulation, and Emmy's line.** Salience is not deception, but mass
reach multiplies any overclaim, and "mental availability" can be built by provoking the distress
you sell relief from. His laws come from big consumer categories; applying them as laws to a
founder-led niche product is overreach. Emmy's line: reach is fine; provoking distress is not;
no paid targeting of people in crisis.

**Sources.**
- https://www.supersummary.com/how-brands-grow/summary/
- https://marketingscience.info/how-do-you-measure-how-brands-grow
- https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/jenni-romaniuk-and-byron-sharp-how-brands-grow-part-2
- https://markteffect.nl/en/byron-sharp-theory
- https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/59035/
- https://oup.com.au/books/higher-education/management-and-marketing/9780190330026-how-brands-grow-part-2-revised
- https://www.marketingweek.com/ritson-versus-sharp-won-clash/
- https://www.marketingweek.com/mark-ritson-thank-byron-sharp/

**Uncertain.** The original 2010 publisher, and the 120,000 copies, are UNVERIFIED. The three
physical-availability components come from one summary.

**Better candidates?** Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013):
from the IPA effectiveness databank, the best results averaged about 60 percent long-term brand
building to 40 percent short-term activation, varying by category. Sharp has called the rule
misleading. A counterweight on budget split, not a replacement.
(https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/,
https://www.warc.com/content/article/event-reports/beyond_6040_putting_effectiveness_in_context/124156)

## 6. R/GA (Nike+)

**Founding and people.** Founded in 1977 by brothers Richard and Robert "Bob" Greenberg as
R/Greenberg Associates, a film and animation studio (title sequences for *Superman* and *Alien*).
It remade its business model roughly every nine years, moved into digital in the 1990s, was
bought by IPG in 2001, and regained independence in March 2025. Wikipedia reports Adweek's
Digital Agency of the Decade (2009).

**The campaign.** Nike+ (launched 2006): a sensor in Nike+ shoes sent run data to an Apple iPod
nano, and R/GA built the online platform where runners recorded, tracked and shared runs and
challenged each other. It won Titanium and Cyber Grand Prix awards at Cannes in 2007. The Nike+
Human Race 10K entry reports more than 800,000 runners in 142 countries and 2.5 million training
miles (self-reported, Cannes entry). The later Nike+ FuelBand (2012) won a Titanium Grand Prix.

**Process or philosophy.** Emmy's reading of the work, not R/GA's stated doctrine: make the
marketing a service people use daily, with their own data as the content, so it compounds
instead of ending.

**What made it travel.** Every run gave a reason to return and something to compare. The
community was the media.

**Quality bar.**
1. It does something useful, repeatedly.
2. The person's own data is the content, and they choose what to share.
3. It improves as more people join.
4. It runs without paid media once built.

**Lens questions.**
- What does someone get the thirtieth time, not the first?
- What would someone share in their own words, by choice?
- Is this a campaign that ends or a platform that compounds?

**Example notes.**
- Through the R/GA lens: an opt-in share card from a real session, in the person's own words,
  with every number on it computed, never decorative.
- Through the R/GA lens: a shared practice month only with real counts and privacy by default.
  No public rankings of anyone's body or inner state (Non-Harm).

**Sources.**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R/GA
- https://artofthetitle.com/studio/r-greenberg-associates-r-ga
- https://lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/213126
- https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/nike-human-race-262179
- https://campaignbrief.com/?p=139088
- https://www.creativereview.co.uk/nike-gps-rga-6-012/

**Better candidates?** Red Antler (Brooklyn; Emily Heyward, JB Osborne, Simon Endres). Brand
work for Casper, Allbirds and Hinge; about half its business is pre-launch startups; Heyward's
*Obsessed* (Portfolio, 2020). Closer to a founder launching a product sold online. Founding year
UNVERIFIED. (https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/09/verified-expert-brand-designer-red-antler/,
https://www.wework.com/ideas/worklife/what-emily-heyward-brand-guru-and-cofounder-of-red-antler-does-all-day)

## 7. Droga5 ("Decode Jay-Z with Bing")

**Founding and people.** Founded in New York in 2006 by David Droga. Accenture Interactive
agreed to buy it in April 2019; Droga stayed as creative chairman.

**The campaign.** October to November 2010, launching Jay-Z's memoir *Decoded* and promoting
Bing. All 320 pages were placed in real-world locations across 13 cities. About 400 clues were
seeded on Twitter, radio and Facebook; players used Bing to find the pages and assembled the
whole book online before it reached stores. Cannes 2011: Outdoor Grand Prix, Integrated Grand
Prix and a Titanium Lion. Reported results: Bing visits up 11.7 percent in the campaign month
(Microsoft internal figures and the agency case, not independently audited); the book entered
the New York Times list at number 2; average engagement over 11 minutes; one million new
Facebook likes for Jay-Z (case material, self-reported).

**Process or philosophy.** In coverage of a 2025 talk, Droga recommends working backward from
the response you want, then choosing the format. In the work, the product is the mechanism of
play, not something shown in an ad.

**What made it travel.** A hunt; the book itself was the media; the scarcity was real because
the pages were physical.

**Quality bar.**
1. The product is used to play, not just shown.
2. The idea fits in one sentence.
3. The wanted response is named before the format.
4. It is worth doing even if you never buy.

**Lens questions.**
- What should people do or feel, before we pick a channel?
- Can we say it in one sentence?
- Does using the product move the story forward?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Droga5 lens: launch the book as a hunt where each chapter found unlocks a matching
  tool in Atüned, so the app is how you play. Every piece is findable; no fake "only 3 left".
- Through the Droga5 lens: name the response first (a reader feels one release in the body and
  tells one friend), then choose the format.

## 8. VaynerMedia (social-first content at volume)

**Founding and people.** Founded in spring 2009 by Gary Vaynerchuk and his brother AJ, to help
large brands build social voices through "micro content" (Gary's own biography). Parent company
VaynerX. Gary's books carry the method: *Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook* (HarperBusiness, 2013) and
*Day Trading Attention* (Harper Business, May 2024).

**The work.** No single campaign is named here, so no results are claimed. The documented model
is Gary's content model (his own 2019 post), a reverse pyramid:
1. Make one pillar piece: a long video, keynote or podcast.
2. A team cuts it into many short pieces, each made for its platform.
3. Distribute across platforms.
4. Read the comments and numbers to see what landed.
5. Let that shape the next pillar.
*Jab, Jab, Jab* adds: give value many times before asking for a sale, natively on each platform.
*Day Trading Attention* argues attention is underpriced on a few platforms, and that feeds now
distribute by interest rather than by who follows you. The often-cited "30 pieces from one" is
a secondary illustration, not his rule.

**What made it travel.** Volume, native format, and comments choosing the next piece.

**Quality bar.**
1. Each short piece stands alone and fits its platform.
2. Far more pieces give than ask.
3. Audience replies decide what gets made next.
4. Volume never outruns accuracy.

**Lens questions.**
- What is this week's pillar, and what does it teach?
- Is this piece made for the platform it is on?
- How many pieces gave before one asked?
- What did people reply to most, and why?

**Example notes.**
- Through the VaynerMedia lens: Lance already dictates at length. One recorded conversation a
  week on a single mechanism can become clips, a quote card, an article and a letter. Emmy can
  clip, caption and schedule; Lance approves, every clip is checked against the book and the
  canon, and none claims an outcome.
- Through the VaynerMedia lens: most posts teach a mechanism; only a few ask.

**Where it can cross the line.** Volume rewards hooks and outrage, and clips lose context. Clips
keep their context, and there is no rage bait or fake urgency.

## 9. Movers+Shakers (e.l.f. "Eyes. Lips. Face.")

**Founding and people.** Launched in 2016 by Evan Horowitz (CEO; in-house brand marketing
background) and his husband Geoffrey Goldberg (Broadway performer and director), after a dance
video of Goldberg's went viral. Its pitch is connecting brands to culture; Horowitz says the
agency spotted TikTok early in 2019.

**The campaign.** #eyeslipsface, launched on TikTok in October 2019 for e.l.f. Cosmetics, built
on an original song, which trade coverage says no brand had done for a TikTok challenge before.
Results, by source: WARC says it was the fastest TikTok challenge to pass 1 billion views and
reached 5 billion; Campaign reports 1 billion in six days; CEW reports more than 4 billion. The
agency entry reports number 4 on Spotify's global viral chart and unpaid celebrity joiners
(Ellen DeGeneres, Lizzo, Reese Witherspoon, Kevin Hart). The agency page claims an 8 percent
rise in Q3 sales and a Cannes Lions Bronze (self-reported, unconfirmed elsewhere).

**Process or philosophy.** Emmy's reading: put the brand inside the part people repeat (the
brand name is the lyric), make it copyable in one try, and be first on a new platform.

**What made it travel.** The product name was the hook, joining was easy, and the platform was
young.

**Quality bar.**
1. Copyable in one try.
2. The brand sits inside the repeated part.
3. Native to the platform's culture.
4. People join without being paid.

**Lens questions.**
- What would someone copy, and can they do it in ten seconds?
- Is Atüned's name inside the repeatable part?
- Is it safe for any body that copies it?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Movers+Shakers lens: Atüned is a body app, and a short, copyable movement the app
  really teaches is native to short video. Never a challenge built on breath-holding or any
  physical risk (Non-Harm).
- Through the Movers+Shakers lens: release a sound (the app's own tone, or a line from the book
  set to music) for others to use. Any paid creator is disclosed.

## 10. Ogilvy Brazil (Dove "Real Beauty Sketches")

**Founding and people.** Ogilvy & Mather Brasil, the Brazil office of the Ogilvy network. The film
used a forensic sketch artist, Gil Zamora (sources differ on his background: FBI-trained, or
formerly San Jose police).

**The campaign.** April 2013, part of Dove's Campaign for Real Beauty. Unseen by the women, the
artist drew each one twice: from her own description and from a stranger's. Side by side, the
stranger's version was kinder. It rested on Dove's research claim that only 4 percent of women
worldwide consider themselves beautiful (a 2004 study updated in 2011, per KSL). Results: Cannes
2013 Titanium Grand Prix and an Integrated Gold; over 23 million views in its first week
(Strategy); 163 million global views and top of the Cannes YouTube Ads Leaderboard (Think with
Google). Ogilvy's own entry calls it the most watched online film ever (self-reported).

**Process or philosophy.** A sourced insight, turned into an experiment, where the proof is on
screen rather than in the copy.

**What made it travel.** A reveal viewers could feel, and a test they ran on themselves.

**Criticism, which is part of the lens.** Adweek collected complaints: the four main women were
white, young and thin; the "more beautiful" sketches looked thinner and younger; and beauty
stayed the yardstick. Critics also noted Unilever owns Axe.

**Quality bar.**
1. The insight is a real, dated finding.
2. The film demonstrates; it does not assert.
3. Viewers see themselves in it.
4. Who is shown matches who is addressed.

**Lens questions.**
- What true thing does this show that people half know?
- Is the proof on screen or only in the words?
- Who is missing from the picture?
- Does the viewer have to feel bad first?

**Example notes.**
- Through the Ogilvy Brazil lens: film real people describing what they feel in the body before
  and after a session, in their own words, unscripted. Any number on screen comes from that
  session.
- Through the Ogilvy Brazil lens: if a film cites a finding about bodies or stress, it needs a
  real study with its date shown, and the "before" never shames.
