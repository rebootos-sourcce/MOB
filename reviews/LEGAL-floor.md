## 1. Where the FDA line sits, and which side this product is on. Size S.

The FDA does not regulate a product like this as a device as long as two things
hold: the intended use is general wellness, and the risk is low. The policy is
*General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices*, and it is enforcement
discretion rather than an approval.

The guidance splits general wellness intended uses into two categories. The
first is a claim about sustaining or offering general improvement to functions
associated with a general state of health, with no reference to a disease or a
condition. Self esteem, sleep hygiene, physical fitness and stress sit here.
The second category references a disease or condition but only where it is well
understood and accepted that healthy lifestyle choices play a role in the
outcome.

A product leaves the policy when it claims to diagnose, treat, mitigate or
prevent a disease, or when it interprets physiological data for clinical
decision making.

- FDA, General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices: https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices
- The guidance PDF: https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download
- The two categories, as summarised: https://innolitics.com/articles/fda-guidance-general-wellness-policy-for-low-risk-devices/ and https://natlawreview.com/article/fda-finalizes-general-wellness-guidance
- FDA's own software decision step for lifestyle functions: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/step-3-software-function-intended-maintaining-or-encouraging-healthy-lifestyle
- Inclusion in the policy is not a finding that the product works: https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IN/PDF/IN12657/IN12657.1.pdf
- FDA revised the general wellness guidance in January 2026. I could not read it. Counsel should: https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products and https://www.honigman.com/alert-3236

**What this means here.** The instrument is on the right side of the line
today, and it stays there on what it *says about itself*, not on what it
computes. Intended use is read off the copy. The exposure is vocabulary: the
product uses anatomical and quasi clinical words and names fetters, saboteurs
and complexes, and a stranger can read that as a finding about a condition. The
fix is not softer words. It is one block of copy that states what the reading is
and is not, placed where the reading is. That is section 11, block B.

This is a requirement of the same kind as a load bearing wall: nobody sends a
letter about it, and the whole structure rests on it.

## 2. What the FTC expects of the claims. Size L, because it is a review and not a paste.

The FTC's *Health Products Compliance Guidance*, December 2022, replaced the
1998 supplement guidance and expressly covers health related apps, not only
pills. It asks for competent and reliable scientific evidence behind a health
benefit claim, and it says that as a general matter that means randomized
controlled human testing.

- FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
- The PDF: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf
- That it now covers health apps and devices, not just supplements: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-03-02-ftc-revises-health-products-compliance-guidance and https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-releases-health-products-compliance-guidance.html
- FTC health claims hub: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/health-claims

The FTC's *Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials* took effect 21
October 2024 and carries penalties, unlike the Endorsement Guides it codifies.
And a disclaimer does not cure an unrepresentative result: "results not typical"
and "individual results may vary" are not enough on their own.

- The rule, questions and answers: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- Federal Register text: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials
- Effective date and the penalty point: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials
- That a typicality disclaimer does not do the work: https://www.proskauer.com/alert/ftc-guides-governing-endorsements and https://www.internetlegalattorney.com/average-results-ftc-disclosure/

**What this means here, and it is the largest legal exposure in the product.**
`DECISIONS.md` carries a throughput argument that reads as an efficacy claim:
patterns released per run against patterns released per therapy session, and the
line that a run is worth some number of years of therapy. Inside the codex that
is a modelling assumption. On a sales page it is a health benefit claim about a
paid product, and the FTC's stated standard for that is clinical evidence. The
pricing and funnel documents are where it will surface.

This is legal requirement territory, enforced by the FTC, and no disclaimer at
the bottom of the page fixes it. It is a claims review before the funnel ships,
not a copy block. Nothing in section 11 addresses it, deliberately.

## 3. What a "not medical advice" disclaimer does, and what it does not. Size S.

It does real work and it is narrower than people assume.

What it does. It sets the intended use, which is what the FDA policy in section
1 turns on. It rebuts the claim that a person was told they were receiving care.
It is the standard of the field, and its absence is conspicuous.

What it does not do. It does not exclude liability for negligence causing
personal injury or death, most states will not let a party disclaim gross
negligence, and a court will read a disclaimer against the party that wrote it
if the circumstances are unreasonable. It also does not reach a person who never
opened the page it sits on, which is the argument for putting the short form
where the reading is and not only in a legal sheet.

- Limits on what a medical disclaimer can exclude: https://seqlegal.com/free-legal-documents/medical-disclaimer/
- That most states do not permit disclaiming gross negligence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disclaimer
- The argument for inline placement over a single buried page: https://termly.io/resources/articles/medical-disclaimer-examples/
- Common practice examples and elements: https://usercentrics.com/guides/website-disclaimers/medical-disclaimers/ and https://www.websitepolicies.com/blog/medical-disclaimer

**Requirement class.** Not a statute. It is the practice that keeps the product
inside the FDA policy and it is the first thing a plaintiff's lawyer looks for.
Treat it as required.

## 4. Crisis and safety. Size S to build, and it is the one nobody regrets.

There is no United States statute that orders a self report tool to print a
crisis line. What exists is a settled practice and a body of guidance, and the
practice is uniform because the alternative is indefensible.

A product that asks a person to write about shame, rage and despair will receive
crisis disclosures. In this build nobody is on the other end, and that fact has
to be said rather than implied.

- SAMHSA 988 key messages: https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/988/key-messages
- The 988 Lifeline suicide safety policy: https://988lifeline.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/FINAL_988_Suicide_and_Crisis_Lifeline_Suicide_Safety_Policy_-3.pdf

- Mental health apps and 988, in Psychiatric Services: https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ps.20240485

What comparable products do. Wysa carries an explicit line that it is not

identifiable information from a California consumer through a commercial site or
service, and this build collects nothing: no network, no server, no copy.

## 6. Privacy the moment a record leaves the device. Size L, and it starts before the record store does.

This is where the obligations actually attach, and three of them have no size
threshold at all.

**Washington My Health My Data Act.** No revenue threshold, no minimum number of
consumers, and almost no entity level exemptions. It requires consent to collect
consumer health data, a separate consent to share it, and a signed authorization
to sell it. It requires a consumer health data privacy policy published behind
its own distinct link on the homepage, and that policy may not carry anything
the Act does not require, so it cannot be a section of the general policy. Most
provisions came into force 31 March 2024. It carries a private right of action
through the state consumer protection act, which is why it is the one to plan
against rather than the one to discover.

- Washington Attorney General: https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy
- The statute: https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373&full=true
- No thresholds, few exemptions, wide sweep: https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2024/lp/navigating-privacy-compliance-will-your-business-be-subject-to-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act and https://bassberry.com/news/washington-my-health-my-data-act/
- The separate and distinct homepage link, and that it must not carry extra material: https://www.klgates.com/The-Countdown-to-Complete-Your-Consumer-Health-Data-Privacy-Policy-Under-the-Washington-My-Health-My-Data-Act-3-19-2024 and https://hintzelaw.com/blog/wa-my-health-my-data-act-pt8-notice
- The AG FAQ, and the note that the guidance complicates a single global policy: https://cdp.cooley.com/washington-attorney-general-publishes-updated-faq-for-my-health-my-data-act/ and https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/new-washington-consumer-health-privacy-policy-guidance-may-complicate-broader-compliance-efforts
- Practical compliance steps: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/03/alerts-technology-hltc-my-health-my-data-act-mhmda and https://www.wilmerhale.com/-/media/files/shared_content/editorial/publications/documents/20240405-american-bar-association--compliance-steps-for-washingtons-my-health-my-data-act.pdf

**Nevada SB 370.** In force the same day, 31 March 2024. Same shape: consent to
collect, separate consent to share, written authorization to sell. It is
narrower in one respect, because information is consumer health data only when
the entity actually uses it to identify health status.

- https://bassberry.com/news/nevada-consumer-health-data-law-takes-effect-on-march-31-2024/
- The difference from Washington: https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/a-kinder-gentler-consumer-health-data-bill-nevadas-sb-370.html and https://syrenis.com/resources/nevada-sb-370/
- Connecticut got there first, July 2023: https://www.healthlawadvisor.com/nevada-joins-washington-and-connecticut-to-protect-consumer-health-data-privacy

**FTC Health Breach Notification Rule.** Amended, effective 29 July 2024, and it
reaches health apps and similar services that are not covered by HIPAA. The
amendment matters more than the breach notice does: an unauthorized disclosure
is now a breach of security, so a voluntary disclosure the person did not
authorize, an advertising pixel for instance, is a reportable event. Notice to
the FTC alongside notice to individuals for 500 or more people, and no later
than 60 days from discovery.

- FTC on the amended rule: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/04/updated-ftc-health-breach-notification-rule-puts-new-provisions-place-protect-users-health-apps and https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/health-breach-notification-rule
- Federal Register: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/30/2024-10855/health-breach-notification-rule
- Scope and the unauthorized disclosure point: https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/07/final-changes-to-health-breach-notification and https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/08/ftc-updated-health-breach-notification-rule

**What the enforcement record says.** The FTC has already made this specific

barring it from sharing health data for advertising, because it disclosed

use. GoodRx, the first HBNR case, 1.5 million dollar penalty.

- BetterHelp, final order: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising
- GoodRx: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising

The owner's ruling that we never sell anybody's data, and the split between the

**HIPAA does not apply** to data a person gives a consumer product directly,
with no provider or plan behind it. That changes if the platform starts handling

- Consumer collected health data is not protected health information absent a covered entity or business associate relationship: https://www.accountablehq.com/post/does-hipaa-protect-health-information-in-apps-wearables-and-employer-wellness-programs and https://www.accountablehq.com/post/who-is-not-a-covered-entity-under-hipaa-examples-explained

**GDPR and the UK, if a person in Europe can sign up.** Stories about shame and

9(2)(a), which needs an express statement and cannot be inferred from use. A
data protection impact assessment is expected for special category processing at
scale. Add the Article 13 notice contents, the Article 27 representative if
there is no establishment in the Union, and 72 hour breach notification.

- Article 9 text: https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/ and https://gdpr-text.com/read/article-9/
- The exceptions, and explicit consent as the operative one: https://gdprhub.eu/Article_9_GDPR
- ICO on the special category rules and what explicit consent requires: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/
- A DPIA is expected for special category processing: https://www.legiscope.com/blog/gdpr-article-9-special-categories.html

The cheapest answer at launch may be to not accept European sign ups until the
notice, the consent flow and the assessment exist. That is an owner decision,
not a researcher's.

Written text is not biometric data, and the Commission's own guidance says
inferring sentiment from text is outside the prohibition. Article 50
transparency duties applied from 2 August 2026. Worth one line to counsel and no
more.

- Article 50: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/ and https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/
- What an emotion recognition system is, and that text is not biometric data: https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/what-is-an-emotion-recognition-system-under-the-eus-artificial-intelligence-act-part-1 and https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/

## 7. Terms of use, and whether anyone is bound by them. Size M.

Terms are not required by statute. They are the only thing that carries the
liability limit, the age floor, the licence, the governing law and the warranty
disclaimer, so a paid product ships with them.

They bind only if a person had reasonable notice and took an act that
unambiguously showed assent. A checkbox that starts unchecked, next to legible
linked terms, at the moment of sign up. A pre checked box or a link in the
footer alone weakens it.

- The two part test, notice and manifestation of assent: https://caldwelllaw.com/news/enforceability-online-wrap-agreements-us-uk-japan/ and https://ironcladapp.com/journal/contract-management/6-components-of-clickwrap-enforceability
- Unchecked box, active assent, and that arbitration and liability limits ride on the same notice: https://www.lcwlegal.com/news/dont-just-click-browsewrap-or-clickwrap-agreements-for-arbitration-provisions-can-be-enforceable/ and https://www.termsfeed.com/blog/3-key-legal-cases-clickwrap/
- Courts scrutinising the mechanism: https://www.fitchlp.com/blog/2024/01/clickwrap-agreements-come-under-scrutiny-in-massachusetts/

**Age.** A terms clause saying 18 or older does not by itself put a product
outside COPPA. COPPA reaches services directed to children under 13 or with
actual knowledge of under 13 users, and the FTC's own guidance says a service can
be child directed even if its terms forbid children.

- FTC COPPA FAQ: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions
- The rule: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa
- That terms alone do not exempt a service: https://www.iubenda.com/en/blog/guide-coppa-mobile-apps/
- Reported: a bill extending protection to under 17 passed the Senate in March 2026. Not law as far as I can establish, and counsel should check: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Online_Privacy_Protection_Act

This product is plainly not directed at children. An 18 floor, stated in the
terms and not contradicted by the marketing, is the clean position. Copy is
block E.

## 8. Subscription and auto renewal. Size M, and it lands with the paywall.

**The federal rule is gone.** The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's Negative
Option Rule, the click to cancel rule, on 8 July 2025, days before compliance
was due, on procedural grounds. There is no federal negative option rule to
comply with today. The FTC still polices deceptive subscription practice under
its existing authority and under ROSCA, and it has moved to revive a rule.

- Vacatur: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-11-click-to-cancel-just-got-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-entirety-of-ftcs-negative-option-rule and https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-days-before-compliance-deadline
- That existing authority and the state patchwork remain: https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250801-eighth-circuit-vacates-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-rule-but-federal-and-state-regulators-likely-to-remain-active
- The FTC moving to revive it: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur

**California's own law did not go anywhere, and it is stricter.** AB 2863 amended
the Automatic Renewal Law with effect from 1 July 2025. It covers free to paid
conversions. Disclosure must be clear and conspicuous and must come before
billing information is confirmed, stating that it renews until cancelled, how to
cancel, the amount or range, and the frequency. Consent to the renewal term must
be its own separate step and cannot be bundled into acceptance of the terms or
pre checked. Cancellation must be available by the same route used to subscribe.
Annual reminders are required. Records of consent must be kept for at least
three years, or one year after termination, whichever is longer.

- Bill text: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2863
- Requirements and the July 2025 date: https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/california-expands-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-now-in-effect and https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2024/10/california-latest-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-in-july-2025
- The separate consent step, the annual reminder and the record keeping: https://dtolaw.com/news/californias-automatic-renewal-law-new-requirements-businesses-should-know-about/ and https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/californias-auto-renewal-law-takes-effect-on-july-1

`DESIGN-billing.md` already puts checkout and cancellation in Stripe Checkout and
the Customer Portal, which gets most of this for free. What Stripe does not
supply is the separate consent step and the disclosure block in our own words.

## 9. Practitioner access, and what it changes. Size L.

Three separate things, and only one of them is ours.

**It does not by itself make us a HIPAA business associate.** A consumer product
holding data a person gave it directly is neither a covered entity nor a
business associate. That changes if the platform creates, receives, maintains or
transmits protected health information *on behalf of* a covered entity, for
instance if a clinic buys seats and uses the panel in its own practice. Then a
business associate agreement is required and the whole security rule attaches.
The trigger is a commercial arrangement with a provider, not a practitioner
logging in as an individual.

- When an app becomes a business associate: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html and https://www.crowleylawllc.com/hipaa-covered-entity-vs-business-associate/
- Consumer direct data is outside HIPAA until that relationship exists: https://www.accountablehq.com/post/do-fitness-apps-need-to-be-hipaa-compliant-when-it-applies-and-when-it-doesn-t
- HHS on apps designated by the individual: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access-right-health-apps-apis/index.html

**It raises the unlicensed practice question for the practitioner, and the
product should not help them cross it.** State boards treat administering,
scoring and interpreting tests of mental abilities or personality as evaluation
within the practice of psychology, and they have sanctioned coaches whose
services matched a licensed scope regardless of the label on the tin.

- Oregon board on unlicensed practice, including what evaluation covers: https://www.oregon.gov/psychology/Documents/Unlicensed_Practice_FAQ.pdf
- The legal line between coaching and psychotherapy: https://www.goodtherapy.org/blog/Psychotherapy-vs-Coaching-Legal-Distinction and https://www.littlehealthlawblog.com/coaching-vs-therapy-why-mental-health-practices-that-offer-both-must-keep-the-services-clearly-separated/
- Enforcement against coaching that matched a licensed scope: https://www.sportspsychology.org/articles/unlicensed-sports-psychology-practice-2026-06-11/

Practical consequence: never describe the panel as an assessment of mental
functioning, never call an output a clinical finding, and say in the
practitioner terms that the practitioner is responsible for staying inside their
own licence. `DECISIONS.md` already states that a practitioner is also a coach,
which makes this live rather than theoretical.

**It is a consequential data grant, and the consent is the product.** Consent
under the consumer health data laws in section 6 must be freely given, specific
and opt in, sharing needs its own consent separate from collection, and the
person must be able to withdraw. What `CLAUDE.md` already requires, explicit
consent, a visible list of who has sight, revocation, never a silent default, is
the same thing the statutes require. Build it that way and the compliance
artefact is the screen. Copy is block H.

One more thing counsel should rule on: a practitioner who is a licensed
clinician may have mandatory reporting and duty to warn obligations in their
state, and the panel will show them somebody's despair. What the product tells
the practitioner about that, and what it tells the person about what their
practitioner may be obliged to do, is a real decision and it is not mine.

## 10. Platform requirements, which do not apply yet. Size S when they do.

These are contract terms with a store, not law, and they bite only if the
product is ever wrapped and submitted. It is one HTML file on the web today, so
none of this applies now.

Apple reviews medical and health apps with greater scrutiny, requires disclosure
of data and methodology behind accuracy claims, and expects an app to remind
people to check with a doctor before making medical decisions. Google Play
requires regulatory proof or a disclaimer for medical functionality, requires
apps to tell people to consult a professional, and runs a health apps
declaration form.

## 12. Where each piece goes, against what is built. Size S each unless noted.

**The footer, `atuned_src/shell/body.html`.** There is no footer today and the
last thing in the body is the release rail. The footer goes after the final tab
host and **outside every tab host**. This is not style. A tab renderer writes the
whole `innerHTML` of its host, so a footer placed inside one is deleted on the
next render, which is the same defect `CLAUDE.md` records for `#sum` and `#know`
holding folded surfaces. Static markup, so it survives with scripts off, which
is the second lesson already in `tools/monitor.js`: the boot sheet fades on a CSS
animation and uncovers a shell, and a footer written by a renderer would be
absent exactly when the noscript notice is what a person can see.

**The footer styles, `atuned_src/shell/head.html`.** One rule block beside the
existing sheet and noscript styles. Muted, ring not fill, and it must not
compete with the instrument. On the phone it is the last thing in the scroll,
which is where a person looks for it.

**The sheets, `atuned_src/ui/panels.js`, next to `helpSheet()`.** Blocks B, C, D
and E are sheets and the mechanism already exists: `sheetOpen(html)` takes markup
and `helpSheet()` is the pattern to copy, using `pm-eye`, `sh-h`, `sh-sec` and
`sh-p`. If the four sheets read as their own concern, a new
`atuned_src/ui/legal.js` after `panels.js` in `atuned_src/MANIFEST` is correct,
and MANIFEST order is the only thing that decides it.

**Help, `atuned_src/ui/account.js`, `accHelp()`.** Two new groups, built from
`accGroup` and `accAct`, which already exist and need nothing new:

    accGroup('Contact', accAct('Write to us','achelpc',{btn:'Copy'}), <block F note>)
    accGroup('Legal', accAct('What this is not','aclegal',{btn:'Open'})
                    + accAct('If you are in danger now','accrisis',{btn:'Open'})
                    + accAct('Terms of use','acterms',{btn:'Open'}), '')

Wire the three openers beside the existing `achowto` handler, which already does
exactly this with `sheetOpen(helpSheet())`. The Copy button on the contact row
should copy the address and report through `status()` like every other write in
this product, because a control must never claim success before it has it.

**Privacy, `atuned_src/ui/account.js`, `accPrivacy()`.** Block D goes above the
existing `What Is Held Here` group, and it replaces nothing. The lead line "We
never sell anybody's data. Ever." stays exactly where it is and exactly as it
reads. The Who Has Sight group already carries the consent posture and needs no
change until the practitioner ships.

**The composer, `atuned_src/ui/storyui.js`.** The one line from block C goes
under the textarea, beside the counter, permanent, not a popup and not a
dismissible notice. This is the one placement that is load bearing for safety,
because it is the screen where a person writes about despair.

**The gates.** A footer that a renderer can delete is exactly the defect
`tests/functional.js` was written to catch, so it wants an assertion that the
footer is present after every surface has rendered. `tools/monitor.js` will pick
up the markup size change on all nine surfaces and that is the diff that proves
it. `tests/design.js` gate 7 watches the network and must stay green, so
**nothing in this work may add a request**, which rules out a hosted policy page
loaded into the app and rules out a mailto that fetches anything.

## 13. The contact route, honestly.

The owner asked for contact us in help or the profile, and Help already has Ask a
question and Report something broken. Those two controls write to an outbox on
the device. That is the right design for feedback with no backend and the copy
already says so.

**It is not a contact route.** A queued form is a note a person writes to
themselves. Nothing leaves, nobody reads it, and no answer comes back. A contact
route is an address a person can write to from outside the product, which works
whether or not our build ever gains a network.

So: print an address. `hello@[domain]` or `support@[domain]`, in Help, in the
footer, and in the terms. One address is enough to start, and `privacy@[domain]`
is worth having separately before the record store lands, because the privacy
laws in section 6 all require a route for a person to exercise a right and that
route should not be the same inbox as feature requests.

Keep the outbox. Say what each thing is, which block F does. When the network
seam opens at sign in, the outbox drains to the same address and the copy stops
having to explain itself.

## 14. What needs a lawyer, and what does not.

**Safe to ship as drafted, in my judgement, and none of it makes a claim about
anybody's health or anybody's obligations.**

- Block A, the copyright line. Size S.
- Block B, What this is not. Size S. It is a statement of what the product does
  not do, which is the safest kind of statement there is.
- Block C, the crisis block. Size S. It follows the safe messaging rules in
  section 4: no means or method, help is available, and it does not claim to be
  a service.
- Block D, the device only privacy statement. Size S. Every sentence is a fact
  about this build that the code can prove.
- Block F, the contact copy. Size S.

**Drafts for counsel, and not to ship on my say so.**

- Block E, Terms of use. Size M. The liability limit, the governing law, the
  arbitration question and the age floor are all legal choices with consequences.
- Block G, the subscription disclosure and the consent step. Size M. California's
  Automatic Renewal Law is prescriptive and a private plaintiff's bar reads it.
- Block H, the practitioner grant. Size L.
- The full privacy policy at the record store, and the separate consumer health
  data policy that Washington requires behind its own homepage link. Size L.
- A breach plan, because the amended HBNR makes an unauthorized disclosure a
  breach and a 60 day clock starts at discovery. Size M.
- Whether European or United Kingdom sign ups are accepted at launch, and if so
  the explicit consent flow, the Article 13 notice, the impact assessment and the
  Article 27 representative. Size L.
- The claims review in section 2. Size L. **This is the item I would put in front
  of counsel first.** The therapy equivalence and the patterns per session
  arithmetic are health benefit claims about a paid product the moment they reach
  a page a stranger reads, and the FTC's stated standard for that is clinical
  evidence. A disclaimer does not fix it.
- Whether the product's own vocabulary keeps it inside the FDA general wellness
  policy, given the January 2026 revision I could not read.
- The mandatory reporting and duty to warn question in section 9.
- The age floor, and whether anything about the product could be read as directed
  at people under 13.
