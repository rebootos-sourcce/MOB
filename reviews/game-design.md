# The game design document

Ngozi Achebe-Lindgren, game director. Written against the code as it stands on
19 September, booted and looked at, not read from memory. Every number in the
audit was measured in a running build and the probe is named beside it.

Short and decided. Every section ends in a decision or a named open item with
an owner.

---

## The core loop, in one sentence

> **Say what happened, watch where it lands in the body, run the line at that
> address until the contraction stops, and say the next thing to an instrument
> that has moved.**

Four beats. Say, see, run, say again. The fourth beat is the loop and it is the
only one the product does not currently make visible: a story told in week four
is read against a body that week one did not have, so the same event comes back
different. That is the reason to return and nothing on any screen says it.

**Every feature is measured against that sentence.** If a thing does not help a
person say, see, run, or notice that the instrument has moved, it is not in the
loop and it needs a separate argument. Both games below are measured against it
in their own sections. The ladder is measured against it in the audit and it
fails, because the ladder counts a ritual overlay that appears in no beat of the
loop.

**What the loop is not.** It is not open, check, close. That is the shape of
every product with a counter in it and it is the shape this one must not take,
because the thing being checked would be a reading of a nervous system.

## Session shape

Most products are built for one length and are hostile at the other. This one
has to hold three, because the person carrying the most charge has the least
time and the person with an hour is usually the one in the middle of something.

### Two minutes

**What it gives.** One address closed. A hand of eight cards in the sort, or one
rerun of ground already opened, which the code guarantees is free forever at
`engine/schema.js:253`.

**The hard rule: a two minute visit requires no typing.** Typing is the twenty
minute act. A product that can only be entered through a text box is a product
with one session length. Today the story box is the only real way in, so the two
minute visit does not exist. The sort is how it starts existing.

**What it must never do.** Leave a half finished thing on the screen. A hand is
eight cards and eight cards is the unit. Twenty four is three hands, not one
long one.

### Twenty minutes

**What it gives.** One story told, parsed, and one release run over the
addresses it lit. This is the loop at full width and it is the session the
product is already built for.

The twenty minute visit is the only one that moves the reading, and that is
correct. Charge does not move because somebody opened an app.

### An hour

**What it gives.** Structure, not charge. The intake at 63 questions, the six
purpose values, the thirty commitments, the compass, the age ladder, the
blueprint. These are the parts that make every later reading sharper, and they
are the parts a person does once.

**The rule that keeps the hour from being hostile.** An hour must never be
required to get a reading, and a reading must always state how much it does not
know. `accuracy()` at `engine/compute.js:176` already returns a percentage with
an interval and it is the honest answer to "why should I answer sixty three
questions". Put the interval on screen and the hour sells itself. Hide it and
the hour reads as a form.

### And the shape across the three

    2 minutes    one address closed          no typing        the sort
    20 minutes   one story, read and run     typing           the loop
    1 hour       structure                   entry            intake, purpose

**Neither end is punished.** Nothing accrues while a person is away and nothing
is lost by stopping. The two minute visit does not nag toward twenty. The hour
does not run out of material: 112 addresses, most never opened.

## Progression

**Ruled: there is no number that is the person going up.** The coherence band
moves in both directions and a person whose week got worse must never read as
demoted by the instrument that was supposed to read them. That is already in
`RESEARCH-ladder.md` section 7 and it stands.

So progression is three things, and a level is what you build when you cannot
find any of them.

### Depth

The field separates as a person goes into it. The wheel already atomises: the
core resolves into the triad at 1.45, the seven seats at 2.30 and the twenty one
laws at 3.40, and each is a real decomposition rather than a decoration.
Progression here is that more of the field becomes separable, because the person
has told the instrument enough for the parts to have values worth drawing.

**Nothing to build.** It exists. It needs saying out loud once, on the surface,
in one sentence: the interior resolves as you fill it.

### Access

112 addresses, nine poled axes, six masks, seven seats, twelve archetypes,
twenty one laws, thirty three saboteurs. Most of it never opened. Ground opened
is access, it only goes up, and it is the one quantity in this product that a
bad week cannot touch.

**Rule.** Ground opened is printed alone, never against the horizon. The engine
computes `cleared` at `engine/schema.js:303` and the product does not say it.
That discipline is right and it holds.

### Understanding

This is the one that is missing and it is the one with the highest ceiling.

The instrument infers where charge sits. The person feels where charge sits.
Those are two independent readings of the same body and the product currently
holds only one of them. Once it holds both, the gap between them is a measure of
how well a person can read themselves, and closing that gap is progression that
is neither a level nor a count.

This is the *Obra Dinn* model: you do not get stronger, you get better at
reading, and the product can prove it because it kept both halves.

**Both games below exist to build this quantity.** The sort collects the
person's call on where a thing lands. The line collects their call on which word
in their own account carries the load. Neither is scored against the engine,
because a person is not wrong about their own body. The two readings are shown
side by side and the disagreement is the finding.

**One refusal attached.** The gap is never printed as an accuracy percentage of
the person. Not "you were right 62 percent of the time". Two lists, side by side,
and the person draws the conclusion.

The first ninety seconds decide the first week. This is written for the app, not
the quiz, so it opens on Summary with a dash in the ring and the four doors,
which is the correct opening and already shipped.

The panel constraints this is built against, from `RESEARCH-icp.md`: Marcus
closes the tab in four seconds if the instrument looks like a dashboard, four of
eight reject the word help, James refuses at the moment a questionnaire turns
into a body without saying so, and the panel does not punish a stated limit.

    0:00  The four doors and one line. No number, no band word, no ring value.
          The line is mechanical: this reads where charge sits in your body and
          what holding it costs. The verb is read, never help.

    0:20  The person picks the story door. It is the widest door and it should
          be visibly the widest. The other three are there so the person who

     to   The prompt is one line and it asks for an event, not a feeling.

## Why somebody opens it on day thirty

Four answers. Three of them are true today and unsaid. The fourth is the one
worth building.

**One. The instrument knows more about them than anything else they own.** By
day thirty it holds a birth date, twenty one laws asked three ways, six purpose
values, thirty commitments, a blueprint, a story history and a charge vector
across 112 addresses. Nothing else on their phone has that and nothing else can
be asked the questions it can answer. The moat is the profile and it compounds.

**Two. The record shows movement they did not have to remember.** `ui/record.js`
already renders any two snapshots and the distance between them. A person cannot
remember what their own chest felt like three weeks ago. The instrument can.
This is the strongest evidenced mechanism in `RESEARCH-ladder.md`, self
monitoring at d = 0.40, and it is already built and sitting on one screen.

**Three. There is ground they have never opened.** Not as a completion bar. As a
fact: most of the architecture has never had a line spoken at it, and the person
knows it because the field is drawn and most of it is dark.

**Four, and this is the one to build. The same story reads differently.** The
loop's fourth beat. A person who tells the instrument about their mother on day
one and again on day thirty gets two different routes through the body, because
the body they told it to has changed. Nothing else in the product is this
specific and no competitor has the parts to make it.

**What that costs to build.** `pathOf` already computes the route and nothing
renders it. Two routes side by side is one canvas and one comparison. It is the
cheapest large thing in this document and it belongs in the line, which is game
two.

**What day thirty is not.** It is not a counter of days. A person who returns on
day thirty after eleven days away has to find the instrument, not a scoreboard
with a hole in it. The audit below is largely about that.

# The two games

Both are the instrument at practice speed. Both read the person's own field and
neither invents a pattern. Neither could be made by anybody who does not have a
parsed story, a charged body and a poled axis set, which is the test the brief
set and it is the right test.

**Verified first.** The games tab is not empty. It carries two half games today:
a twenty four card letting go run and an eight pair memory match. The run is
worth keeping and is specified below as the shell for game one. **The match is
the disqualified design.** It is a memory game with fetter icons on it: the card
faces carry no information from the person's field, the pairing is icon
matching, and the only field dependent content is the block that opens after the
match is already made. The game is an interstitial standing in front of
information. It should be replaced by game two and its reveal block should be
kept, because the reveal block is good and belongs in Knowledge.

## Game one. The sort

### The rule

Cards are dealt from the addresses the person is actually carrying. One card at
a time, face up, with the address, the seat, and the line at it. One question:

> Does this land in the body.

Three answers. **It lands. It does not. Cannot tell.**

**The answer does not open for four seconds.** The three controls are present
and inert, and they come live together with no countdown, no bar and no sound. A
person who answers in three hundred milliseconds answered from the word, not
from the body, and the whole value of this game is on the other side of that
wait. The ruling in `DECISIONS.md` is that the barrier is the point. This is the
barrier, built as a floor on haste rather than as a ceiling on time.

**Cannot tell is a real answer and the copy says so.** It routes the address to a
later hand and it is never counted as a miss. A forced binary makes people lie
to a diagnostic instrument, which is the most expensive thing a person can do
here.

### The prediction layer

Off for the first hand, offered on the second and remembered after that.

Before the seat is shown, the person calls it: seven seats, seven controls. Then
the card turns and shows where the instrument placed it.

**This is the skill and it is the reason to play twice.** Naming where a charge
sits in your own body before anything tells you is the exact competence the
product exists to build, and it has a real ceiling. A person is slow and wrong at
it in week one and fast and close in week six.

**Framed as a disagreement, never as an error.** The copy is "the instrument put
it at the throat, you put it at the heart", and the seat glyphs are shown
together. A person is not wrong about their own body. Where the instrument placed
it is a separate claim and it is the one that can be wrong. `DECISIONS.md`
already records two axes cards naming addresses the engine carries under other
names, so the disagreements are worth reading.

### The loop

    deal a hand of eight
      -> card up, four second floor, three answers
      -> optional seat call before the turn
      -> card down, next card
    hand ends
      -> two piles, side by side, no numbers on them
      -> the pile that lands is the run queue, one control
      -> deal again, or run it, or stop

**The decision that makes it a game.** It is not the answer, which is honest
reporting. It is what the person does with the pile: run the whole thing, run the
heaviest three, or deal another hand and find something heavier first. The
allowance makes that a real tradeoff, because a run is at most twenty five
patterns and new ground is what a plan buys. That is the Sid Meier condition and
it is already in the economy rather than invented for the game.

### Session length

    one hand, eight cards, no prediction        90 seconds
    one hand, eight cards, with prediction      2 minutes
    a full pass, twenty four                    5 to 6 minutes
    stopping anywhere                           answers stick, nothing lost

The two minute session in the shape table is this, and it requires no typing.

### What it teaches

To tell a felt contraction from a thought about one. That is interoceptive
discrimination and it is trainable, it is the prerequisite for every other thing
this product asks of a person, and nothing in the build currently trains it.

Second order, over sessions: where charge sits. The prediction layer is the
teacher and the calibration is the report card, and the report card is about the
reading rather than about the person.

### What it reads from the person's own field

    compute().loaded                 the addresses carrying, n.sq >= 4
    n.b, n.cf, n.k, n.i              seat, fetter, name, identity
    cardDepth / cardLine / axLine    the owner's own catalogued sentence
    C3 strict syntax                 where no card exists, and it says so

The pool logic in `ui/games.js:36` already does this correctly, including the
fall back that gives a light field a full hand from its heaviest addresses. Port
it, do not rebuild it.

### What it writes

**`meter.kept`.** An address enters it when the person has marked that it lands,
at least once. This is the field `DESIGN-progression.md` section 3.3 requires
before the markers can be surfaced honestly, and it is the structural fix for the
buyable marker: money buys the chance to open ground, attention is the only thing
that moves a marker, and a sweep of addresses that land on nothing moves nothing.

**The seat calls**, as a short list per address, for the calibration.

**Never a score.** How much a person marks is not a number the product prints.
It qualifies ground and nothing else.

### What it refuses

No clock that punishes. No hand that must be finished. No score on the sort. No
accuracy percentage of the person. No streak of hands. No "you have not sorted in
a while". Nothing is lost by stopping mid hand.

**One safety gate.** Below the crisis floor the sort does not deal from the
heaviest addresses. It deals from the lightest, or it does not offer, per the
crisis design in `DESIGN-progression.md` section 4. Handing a person in acute
distress a deck of their own worst addresses at speed is the worst available
version of this game.

### Build size

    engine/sort.js         deal, mark, calibration read        ~90 lines
    ui/sortui.js           the card, the floor, the piles      ~210 lines
    shell css              ~110 lines
    schema                 meter.kept, meter.calls, boundary   ~40 lines
    tests/engine.js        12 assertions
    tests/functional.js    6 assertions, including the floor

**About 450 new lines and one schema bump.** Two days of build, one of test. No
canvas, so no frame rate risk. The schema bump is the real cost: the blank meter
is written in four places and `DESIGN-progression.md` section 0.3 records that
they already disagree, so that has to be fixed first or this adds a fifth.

**Dependency.** None. It ships alone and it unblocks the markers.

## Game two. The line

### The rule

One of the person's own committed stories plays back, one word at a time, as a
travelling point on the body figure. It moves between the seat centroids the
sniffer already records, at reading pace, and it goes deeper into the figure
where the word carried more.

One input. **Press when you feel the drop.**

At the end, the route is drawn whole, with the person's presses on it and the
two ends the engine found: the heaviest word and the lightest.

### Why only this product can make it

`parseStory().path` at `engine/sniff.js:139` returns an ordered walk through the
body with a character offset, a word, a seat, a pair of coordinates, an amount
and a depth for every step, plus the dwell seat, the span, the net drop, and both
ends of the charge. The file's own comment says it is a record that nothing reads
and no number moves because of it.

It is the single most game shaped object in this codebase and it is drawn
nowhere. Nothing without a sniffer, a nerve map and a person's own text can make
this.

### The loop

    pick a story from your own history
      -> the route plays, one word at a time, on the figure
      -> press when it drops
    the route draws whole
      -> your presses against the two ends the engine found
      -> the word you pressed on is a control: run the line at that address
    play another story, or run the address, or stop

### The second round, and it is the day thirty mechanic

Any story can be played again later. The route is recomputed against the current
field, so the same words walk a different body. Two routes, side by side, with
the date on each.

**This is the fourth beat of the core loop, made visible.** It is the only thing
in the product that shows a person that the instrument has moved without making
a claim about why, because it is their own sentence both times and the words did
not change.

**And it stays inside the causation refusal.** The path is not in the arithmetic
and this does not put it there. Two drawings of the same sentence is a record,
not a claim, and the record's own sentence applies: what moved them is not in
this record.

### Session length

    a three sentence story          35 to 50 seconds
    a paragraph                     90 seconds
    a long entry                    up to 3 minutes
    one story is one round          there is no next round unless asked

**The pace is reading pace and it is the person's.** A control sets it slower or
faster and it is remembered. This is an accessibility control, so it is free on
every plan, like the lightings.

### What it teaches

That a story is not uniformly charged. It has one word carrying most of it, that
word sits at an address, and the address is runnable. That is the ruling in
`DECISIONS.md` on the key, taught by playing it rather than by reading it: one
thought line, at an address, by way of a channel.

It is also the best onboarding the release protocol will ever get. A person who
has watched their own sentence walk down their own body and stop understands why
the run targets an address. Nothing written can do that in forty seconds.

### What it reads

    parseStory(text).path            steps, at, word, seat, x, y, amt, depth
    .kink and .floor                 both ends, already reported separately
    .dwell, .span, .net, .drop       the shape of the route
    SEATXY                           measured centroids, no invented anatomy
    story.entries[].text             the person's own words, on device

### What it writes

A short mark list per story entry: the step indices the person pressed on, and
the date.

**And it answers an open question the owner named.** `DECISIONS.md` records that
whether the block sits at the highest charge or the lowest is open, and that the
code assumed highest by a sort order. This game collects, per person, per story,
which end they actually feel. That is the only instrument in the product that
could settle it and it settles it from data rather than from a sort order.

### What it refuses

No score against the engine's ends. No "you missed it". No combo, no accuracy
percentage, no rank. A person who presses nowhere has told the instrument
something true and the copy says so.

**No reward for a story that reads lighter.** This is the sharpest hazard in the
design. If replaying a story and getting a shallower route were rewarded in any
way, the product would have taught people to write their own accounts dishonestly
to move a number, which corrupts the only input the instrument has. Both routes
are drawn, neither is preferred, and no copy anywhere says one is better.

**Safety, and it is not optional.** Replaying a person's own charged account back
to them at pace, with the body lighting, is activating by design. Three rules:

1. Below the crisis floor the game is not offered. The instrument is offered.
2. A stop control is present on every frame and stopping keeps the marks.
3. Nothing auto advances. The next story is chosen, never queued.

### Build size

    engine/route.js        playback clock, mark comparison     ~60 lines
    ui/lineui.js           canvas, figure, presses, the draw   ~240 lines
    shell css              ~80 lines
    schema                 story.entries[].marks, boundary     ~25 lines
    tests/engine.js        8 assertions
    tests/functional.js    5 assertions
    tests/design.js        1 gate, 60fps on the playback

**About 405 new lines and one schema field.** Three days, and the extra day is
the frame rate. The playback is a single travelling point over a static figure,
so it is one moving object and it should hold sixty frames comfortably, but the
wheel is already on the page and the budget has to be measured rather than
assumed.

**Dependency.** None on the sort. It needs at least one committed story, which
means it is the second thing a new person can play and not the first. That is the
right order anyway.

# The ladder audit

The ladder shipped this week and it was built without me. I have booted it,
rendered it in three states and probed the engine. Twelve findings. The probes
are at the end of each so somebody can reproduce them before fixing anything,
which is the house rule.

**The good part, said once and meant.** `engine/ladder.js` is a clean host free
module, every function is pure against a profile and a passed moment, the
comments are honest about what they are doing, the icons are per mark with their
own families and seat colours, and the surface genuinely avoids the words. The
craft is not the problem. The design is.

---

### Finding 1. Two marks un-earn themselves. The hardest one.

`turned` and `seat` test on `l.clear`, which `ledgerRead` computes live from the
current axis values, and the file's own comment says clear "is the only one of
the four that can go down". A mark tested on a quantity that goes down is a mark
that goes away.

**Measured.** All nine axes at held 2 and opposite 7 earns `turned` and `seat`.
Move the same nine to held 7 and opposite 2 and both are gone from the earned
list, in the same session, without a reload.

```
before: ["nine","turned","seat"]
after:  ["nine"]
```

The module header says a mark is "a count of events that happened, and a count
of what happened is honest". Neither of those two is a count of an event. They
are live predicates on a state that moves with how a person happened to word a
story, which is the exact quantity `ui/record.js:11` was written to refuse to
draw conclusions from. So the product can withdraw a mark because somebody had a
hard week, and it does it silently.

**Fix.** A mark is written once, with a date, and never recomputed. That is
`meter.firsts`, which already exists. See finding 3.

---

### Finding 2. The streak is a loss frame with the absence named twice.

`RESEARCH-ladder.md` section 2, the crisis case, rule 3, in its own words: the
gap is not mentioned. Not as a welcome back, not as a gentle note, not as a
freeze that was helpfully applied.

**Measured.** A fourteen day run that ended eleven days ago renders, in this
order, on the compass:

    14 days , last run
    The run ended 11 days ago. Longest held: 14. Practise today and a new one starts.
    Today is not on the record yet.    [Build today's ritual]

That is the absence named in the second line, named again in the third, and a
primary coloured button placed under it. The largest number on the surface is a
thing the person no longer has. This is the abstinence violation effect built as
a card, and it is being shown to a population selected for carrying somatic and
psychological distress.

The research in this repo rejected the consecutive day streak on four separate
grounds, gave the crisis case as a worked example, and named this exact return as
the worst available move. It shipped anyway.

**Fix, and it is small.** Delete `streakRead`'s gap reporting from the surface.
Keep the days practised as a strip of the last thirty with a mark on the days
that carry one, no number, no consecutive count, no best ever, and no sentence
about the days that do not. `ui/record.js:66` already renders that exact shape
for snapshots. Port it. Two hours of work and it removes the only mechanic in the
product that can punish somebody for stopping.

---

### Finding 3. The wrong system shipped, and the right one is already in the file.

`meter.firsts` exists at `engine/schema.js:36`. It is written by `meterFirst` at
`:523`, deduplicated so a first happens once, validated at the boundary at `:232`,
exported, tested at `tests/engine.js:328` including that it survives the
boundary, and called from `ui/release.js:88` and `:92` for every new address and
every new seat.

**Nothing renders it.** Grep across `atuned_src/ui/` returns two call sites, both
writes.

So the build carries two progression systems. One is dated facts that cannot be
lost, written to the profile, validated, and invisible. The other is sixteen live
predicates, stored nowhere, recomputed on every render, and it is the one on the
screen. `DESIGN-progression.md` section 2.5 is unambiguous: there is no badge
system, because `meter.firsts` is one.

**Fix.** Render the firsts as a dated list, newest last, nothing absent shown.
Move the six marks that are genuinely first time facts into `meterFirst` calls at
the moment they become true. Delete the rest or convert them. Half a day.

---

### Finding 4. "Nine axes" is false, and it is printed as a claim about the person's own data.

The mark reads: *Every one of the nine has a value you entered. Nothing is a
default.* Its test is `l.clear + l.carry >= 9`, and `ledgerRead` increments both
counters for the same axis when it is heavy at both ends.

**Measured.** Five axes set to held 6 and opposite 6, four axes left at zero:

```
axesTouched: 5    clear: 5    carry: 5    nineEarned: true
```

Four of the nine untouched and the product states in writing that all nine carry
a value the person entered. That is not a rounding problem, it is the instrument
lying about its own inputs, in a product whose whole claim is that it is a
mirror you can inspect.

**Fix.** One line. Count distinct axes with either end above the threshold.

---

### Finding 5. The ledger prints axes and calls them addresses.

`ledgerRead` iterates `CHILD`, which is the nine axes. The surface prints:

    Held at the far pole    2 addresses

Nine is the ceiling, and the product tells people it has 112 addresses. A person
who has genuinely cleared forty addresses reads two. The unit is wrong, the
ceiling is invisible, and the number is the one a person is most likely to
screenshot.

**Fix.** Either count addresses, which is what the label says and what
`compute()` already knows, or rename the row to axes. Counting addresses is the
right answer because it is the quantity that matches the product's own
vocabulary. One hour.

---

### Finding 6. The streak counts the one act that is not the product.

`pracDays` reads `p.rituals` and nothing else. A release run writes history, the
meter, the unique keys and the firsts, and puts no day on the record at all.

**Measured**, on a profile with fourteen saved rituals and no release run:

```
rituals: 14   meter.lines: 0   meter.unique: 0   the record says: 14 days
```

So a person can spend a month running the release protocol, opening real ground,
and the record shows nothing, while a person who saves a ritual overlay every
morning and never opens an address reads as thirty days deep. The record is
counting the one surface that `DECISIONS.md` describes as logging a practice
nothing reads back.

**Fix.** Whatever the day mechanic becomes after finding 2, it counts ground
opened, not ritual saves. Ground opened is the act in the loop.

### Finding 7. A virgin profile opens on a zero in the largest type.

**Measured**, first render, nothing entered:

    The record
    0 days , last run
    Nothing on the record yet. Build one ritual and save it, and the first day is on.
    Today is not on the record yet.    [Build today's ritual]

Two defects in four lines. The stray space before the comma comes from
`cone.js:582` concatenating `' , last run'`, and it ships to every new person.
And the first thing this surface ever says to anybody is zero, set in the largest
type on the card, followed immediately by a statement that today is not on the
record.

The endowed progress finding in this repo's own research is that a ten stamp card
with two stamps already on it was completed by 34 percent against 19 percent for
an eight stamp card requiring identical purchases. The gift of a hundred patterns
is already the honest version of that mechanic and it is not on this surface at
all. Instead the surface opens at nought and says so twice.

**Why the gate did not catch it.** `tests/functional.js:806` injects nine
consecutive days before it renders. The zero state and the lapsed state are never
rendered by any gate.

**Fix.** No number at all until there is something to count. The empty state is
one sentence about what the record will hold. Twenty minutes, plus two gate
assertions that render the zero state and the lapsed state.

### Finding 8. The next mark points at the deepest rut.

`ladderRead` picks the unearned mark from the family the person has the most
earned marks in. So a person with five Practice marks and no Ground marks is
pointed at another Practice mark, and the family they have never touched is never
named.

In a product whose subject is 112 addresses and nine axes, the pointer should
invite the untouched thing. This one reinforces the dominant behaviour, which is
the opposite of what a progression pointer is for.

**Measured**, on the lapsed profile above, at day eight of practice with zero
ground opened: `next` is `month`, which is thirty consecutive days. A twenty two
day demand, named to somebody who has just been told their run ended.

**Fix.** The next mark is the nearest unearned mark in the family with the fewest
earned, with the reachable ones preferred over the distant ones. One function,
about twelve lines.

### Finding 9. Seven, thirty and ninety are all or nothing standards.

`week`, `month` and `season` require consecutive days. Lally, quoted in this
repo's own research: missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit
formation, automaticity dropped by less than half a point and recovered quickly.

A person who practises six days in seven, every week, for a year, never earns
"Seven days". The mark measures a calendar, not a practice, and the design answer
in the research is not a gentler streak, it is to hold no all or nothing standard
for a person to violate.

**Fix.** Days practised in a window, not days in a row. Twenty days in any thirty
is a real fact about a practice and it survives a flu.

### Finding 10. The marks have no dates, because they are not stored.

There is no `p.marks` anywhere. Every mark is a predicate evaluated at render
time, so no mark knows when it became true, and the one thing the approved design
says an achievement primitive must be is a fact with a date.

This is the root cause of finding 1 and it is why the whole thing feels like a
checklist rather than a record. A checklist is about what you have not done. A
dated list is about what you did.

**Fix.** Same as finding 3. Write on the event, read on render.

# What I refuse and what it costs

I will not put a manipulation pattern on a product that reads somebody's nervous
system. Named, so nobody has to guess where the line is:

- the variable ratio reward, in any dress, including a chest, a drop or a spin
- the loss framed streak, and every scaffold built to soften one
- the artificial scarcity timer
- the near miss
- the social pressure nudge and any view of another person's reading
- the dark pattern on cancel
- the resource drip that gates a session
- the notification that names an absence

Every one of them works. That is not in question and it has never been in
question. They work because they run on the same survival machinery this
instrument exists to release, and using them here would mean the product installs
the load it claims to read.

**The one rule under all of it: a person must be able to stop and be glad they
used it.** If a mechanic only works because leaving is punished, it is not
shipping.

### The number

**Patel et al. 2016, *Annals of Internal Medicine*. Thirteen weeks, four arms, a
seven thousand step daily goal. Proportion of participant days hitting the goal:
0.30 control, 0.35 gain framed, 0.36 lottery, 0.45 loss framed.**

**So refusing the loss frame gives up about 22 percent of adherent days against
an otherwise identical gain framed design.** That is the measured figure and it
is the honest anchor.

**Labelled as what it is.** That is a benchmark from elsewhere. It is a walking
trial with a cash incentive, not this product, not this population and not a
retention curve. It is the closest measured thing I have and I am not going to
dress it up as a prediction about our build.

**My own estimate on top of it, labelled as an estimate.** Against an otherwise
identical build carrying a loss framed streak, plan for day thirty retention
roughly a fifth to a third lower. That range is mine, from shipped mobile, and it
is not a measurement of anything here. Instrument it from the first thousand
people and replace my estimate with our number.

**What the refusal buys, in the same currency.** eQuoo, which is narrative and
skill based rather than streak and badge based, retained 21 percent more
participants than control in a large student randomised trial. The mechanics with
evidence behind them in mental health are made of story, skill and competence
feedback. The mechanics with harm reports behind them are made of counters that
can be lost. We are not giving up the category, we are giving up one lever in it.

**And the part that does not have a number.** A product that reads somatic and
psychological self report and attaches a loss framed counter to it is a
litigation surface. The research in this repo already cites a state attorney
general action alleging deliberate engineering for engagement against known risk.
The expected value of that is not a retention percentage, and it is not small.

# Grade

**How well the product currently rewards returning: D.**

The reasons, and each one is a thing that can be fixed rather than a feeling. The
only mechanic currently designed to bring somebody back is a consecutive day
streak that counts the one act outside the core loop and punishes the absence. The
firsts are written to the profile and rendered nowhere. The ritual log is written
and never read back, which `DECISIONS.md` has recorded as a gap for some time.
The record, which is the strongest evidenced mechanism available and is already
built, sits on one screen. The games tab holds one half game worth keeping and one
that should not have been built. And the fourth beat of the core loop, the one
that actually makes day thirty different from day one, is not on any surface.

**What moves it, and by how much.**

    finding 2 removed, window strip in           D  -> D+
    the firsts rendered                          D+ -> C-
    the sort shipped                             C- -> C+     two minute session exists
    the line shipped                             C+ -> B-     the fourth beat is visible
    the ritual read back as an if then plan      B- -> B      d = 0.65, cheapest item in the file

That is a B inside about three weeks, from a D, and none of it needs the pattern
keying ruling or the network seam.

**Ceiling: A minus.**

The material is genuinely extraordinary and I have not worked on a product with
this much game in it that was not a game. 112 addresses most of which have never
been opened, a sniffer that returns an ordered walk through the body with both
ends of the charge on it, nine poled axes, a compass with an oscillation range
over thirty, ninety and three hundred and sixty five days, a body that changes
when something is released, twelve decks, and a profile that compounds. Almost
none of it is being played.

**Why A minus and not A.** Two ceilings, and only one of them is ours.

The first is the pattern keying ruling. Until it lands, the markers cannot be
drawn honestly, the horizon cannot be shown beside a plan rate, and a progression
surface with an unreachable point at the top of it is the second time this product
has done that. That is the owner's call and it caps the ladder from above.

The second is mine and I am not asking for it to be lifted: one file, no network,
no manipulation patterns. That costs the top half grade against what a free to
play team would build with the same material, and it is the correct trade. A
product that reads a nervous system and hands the person back an instrument they
can put down is worth more than one that gets the extra 22 percent of days.
