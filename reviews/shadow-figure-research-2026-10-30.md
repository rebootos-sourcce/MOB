# Shadow figures, their opposites, and where the engine can hold them

Research and a naming proposal only. Nothing here is built, wired or ruled.
Prepared 30 to 31 October 2026 by the AI, ML and algorithms seat, on branch
`shadow-figure-research-30oct` off `258f44ef` (which carries the nerve to
celestial audit, `0d831e4a`).

The occasion. Lance, asked whether he wanted the one real super complex named
or more built: "Super complexes are the embodiments of Lucifer, Geryon,
Asmodeus, their polar opposites as well. And check GitHub and check both repos
you should find plenty of material. Search 3 times."

The house rule this has to honour, `DECISIONS.md`, 30 October: "instead of
referring to a person as Lucifer or Moloch, we want to describe the behavior in
a single word. Or two." So every name proposed below is a behaviour, and the
figure's name is kept only as a trace back to the source.

## The answer in five lines

1. The material exists and is large. One registry on an unmerged branch
   already holds 30 shadow records with a one or two word behavioural label
   each, made on the owner's own direction to "hide the source name and reveal
   only the characteristic".
2. Lance has named the super complex figures three times, and the list moved
   each time (below, section 1.4). Taken together: Lucifer, Geryon, Asmodeus,
   Baal, Moloch, Set and Druj. Plus Psychopathy, the one the engine already
   builds.
3. Six of those seven figures have a documented opposite. Baal does not.
4. Only Psychopathy has a clean home in the engine. The engine's super complex
   rule is "two hyper complexes together". Lucifer, in the owner's own model,
   is all nine descent layers at once, which no pair can hold. Geryon lands on
   the same pair as Psychopathy. The rest fit only by inference.
5. The blocking question is not about names. It is whether a super complex is
   a pair of hyper complexes (what the engine does) or a set of descent layers
   (what the owner described on 27 October). The two cannot both be the rule.

## Pass 3. Where the engine can hold them

### 3.1 What the engine actually builds

Read off `compute.js`, not off the brief. The brief said the engine "has 9
real hyper complexes" from `CODE_STRUCT`. That is not quite right, and the
difference decides everything below.

- `compute()` builds hyper complexes from **six families**
  (`HCX_LIB`: Grandiosity, Predatory, Collapse, Rigidity, Dysregulation,
  Dissociation) **and their six overshoot poles** (`FAM_POLE`: Self-erasure,
  Enabling, Mania, Indiscriminate, Numbness, Enmeshment), whose line is "the
  fix for [the family], done past the point where it helps". Twelve in all.
- A super complex is **any two of those twelve that sit next to each other by
  weight**, both at `SUP_W` or more. So the rung can hold 66 different pairs,
  and one of them has a name.
- That one is Psychopathy. `compute()` asks for Grandiosity and Predatory by
  name, wherever they rank, and puts the result in `r.psychopathy`, never in
  `r.sups`.
- `CODE_STRUCT`'s nine hyper complexes are canon.json's names with codes.
  Its own header says "nothing in compute() or trace.js reads it yet", and it
  carries no composition.
