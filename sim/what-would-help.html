Atüned, the UI and UX seat

What would have kept them

Nine figures, 1000 simulated arrivals a run, 19 runs of ninety days each. The grade was 38.91. This page asks the other question: what would have kept the people it lost.

Read this first, because it decides what the rest is worth. The thousand are simulated. Asking a simulated cohort what it wants returns this model’s own assumptions wearing the costume of an answer, and none of it is evidence about a human being. What is real on this page is the left hand column: every session length, every story told, every release run, every departure and every sticking point below was produced by driving the shipped engine for ninety days and reading the surface off source.html in Chromium. So the method is not a survey. For each figure, the run says where they stopped and what their session could afford, and the ask is derived from those two facts. Nothing goes on the list that does not trace to one. Taste is refused, including this seat’s own, and the refusals are printed beside the asks.

How an ask was derived

Three rules, applied to every figure.

  1. It has to trace to a departure, not to a complaint. “More simultaneous choices than a working memory holds” was met by 668 of a thousand and attributed 12 departures. “There is no way to hand it to anybody” was met by 199 and attributed 41. The second is the smaller experience and the larger loss, so it ranks above the first. A friction everybody meets and nobody leaves on is a nuisance, not a sticking point.
  2. It has to fit the session that figure actually has. Diane tells no stories at all in ninety days. That is not a preference and it is not reluctance: her session is worth 2.1 minutes across the whole quarter and the only door to her own charge is measured at 3.4 minutes. The improvement that implies is a narrower door, not a nicer one.
  3. It has to survive being modelled. Every ask on this page was turned into a change, applied to the same harness, and re-run to the same stopping rule. What it moved is printed beside it, including the ones that moved nothing.

What each figure would have got

Read down the ask column and the same three mechanisms account for nearly all of it.

  • A narrower door, not a better one. Diane, James and Marcus between them are 440 of 1000 and all three lose the same way: the session they have is shorter than the door they are offered. Nothing about the Story page’s quality is in that sentence.
  • Something to release, and a release that accumulates. Derek, Ana, Angela and Sofia are 510 of 1000. “Nothing is above the line, so there is nothing to release” cost 89 and “Every story makes the number worse” cost 9. Both are arithmetic. The engine already returns carrying for the first one and the action surfaces do not read it.
  • An instrument that reads what was written. “The box reads nothing out of what they wrote” cost 68 and “The reading names an address the person did not describe” cost 13. Measured through the shipped parseStory: the bank read 22 of 41 ordinary sentences before the patch and 40 after, and the roster’s own says lines went 2 of 14 to 9. That is 30 entries in one data file.

What was refused

These are the improvements a cohort would ask for if a cohort were asked, and none of them traces to a measured departure. The last one is this seat’s own and it is refused on the same grounds.

The proposalWhy it is refused
An onboarding tourNobody left because they did not understand the screen. “More simultaneous choices than a working memory holds” was met by 668 of a thousand and attributed 12 departures. The count is the problem, not the explanation of the count, and a tour adds a screen to a product already measured at 71 choices above the fold on the landing surface.
