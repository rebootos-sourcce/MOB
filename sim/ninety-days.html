Nine figures from the roster, 1000 simulated arrivals a run, 19 runs, driven through the shipped engine for ninety days each.

Read this once and then read the numbers plainly. Every person below is imagined. Nobody named exists and nothing in quotation marks was said by a human being. What is real is the product: every reading, every charge, every release, every streak and every mark was produced by calling the shipped engine, and every fact about the surface was read off source.html running in Chromium. The model in this report is one thing only, and it is when somebody opens the file and when they stop. That model is stated in full, it is argued from who each figure is, and it is judgement. The finding

It is not the retention curve. It is what happens to the person who does everything right.

One case, no model in it at all. A person answers the intake on day one, then for ninety consecutive days tells the instrument one true thing, runs the largest release the field offers, and keeps one practice. Driven through the shipped engine, with the release arithmetic checked line by line against the same run executed in Chromium.

Every line is flat. Ninety days, 1193 minutes of recorded practice, a 90 day streak, 13 marks earned and 444 patterns of ground opened, and the reading finishes 0.54 of a point from where it started. The band does not change. Four cases, four starting integrities, the same result.

And the first one takes a while to arrive. In the ceiling case, which tells it something true every single day, the first address crosses into the reading on day 6 with the intake answered and day 10 without it. Before that day nothing the person wrote is in the reading at all. An address has to reach four to enter it, and one story moves one by a fraction of that.

Three mechanisms, each measured, together produce the flat line.

  1. A story only ever adds charge, so a reading only ever falls from one. Measured across the cohort: the whole of every story a person told moves them -1.58 in Sofia's case and -2.62 in Ana's, and every release they ran moves them back by 1.57 and 2.58. The two cancel to inside a tenth of a point.
  2. The release takes twenty one percent of the weight and installs sixty two percent of what it took. So the far pole is never reached: after ninety days of daily releases the ceiling case holds 4 addresses at the opposite, out of a hundred and twelve.
  3. The reading is the intake. The same ninety days run at four different intake answers produce four flat lines at four different heights, and the height is the intake. The entire somatic half of the product, run every day for a quarter, is worth less than a point of the number it is supposed to move.

The allowance is a label

Measured in the shipped app, not modelled. 119 patterns of ground were opened until the allowance read zero, and then a release was run anyway. The panel printed 4 patterns of the 0 you have left and the run went ahead: 119 patterns before it and 123 after, the reading moving 41.33 to 42.25. relCoolDown never reads relLeft(). In the ceiling case the gift runs out on day 24 and the person goes on to open 444 patterns against a gift of a hundred.

That is one defect with two faces. There is nothing to sell, because there is no limit to sell past. And a person is told a number that is not true of them, on the one panel whose job is to say what a run costs.

The move

What goes, what moves, what folds. In the order that changes the number, not the order that is easiest.

  1. Make the release clear an address. This is the one. Twenty one percent of the weight and a sixty two percent install is a rounding error against a story that adds the same amount back. Either the removal compounds over a session, or the install reaches the far pole, or a cleared address stays cleared. Until one of the three is true, the product's core promise is a loop that returns to its own start and the marks are the only thing a person gets. Everything else on this list is smaller than this.
  2. Widen the box or change what it claims. 46% of ordinary writing reads nothing, and in the driven case the reading sat still through the first 3 commits. Two honest routes. Widen the lexicon until an ordinary paragraph lands, which is a data job and not an architecture one. Or say what it is: a vocabulary of 231 words, shown to the person, with the words it found lit and a line saying what it did not read. The second one is an afternoon and it converts the defect into the product showing its work, which is this instrument's whole defence.
  3. Stop printing an inferred address as a finding. 12 of 41 lines produce imprints that are entirely inferred, and the flag is already on every one of them. A person who wrote that they snapped at their cofounder is currently offered Denial Of Light, Envy, Jealousy. The panel has the flag. It needs to use it.
  4. Gate the allowance or take the number off the panel. One of the two, today. A stated limit with nothing behind it is a false statement on the one surface that quotes a price.
  5. Give the reading somewhere to go on a phone. Summary is off the strip at 390 and Summary is where the number lives. Two of six ICPs and one edge case have one device.
  6. Read the done flag in the streak. A saved plan is not a day practised. ledgerRead already separates planned from done; pracDays does not.
  7. One seed for an unmeasured law. Three callers, three numbers, and 6.25 points of coherence between two of them on an empty profile.
  8. Then the return. The largest single loss in this run is an absence that nothing interrupted. That is the notification seam, and it is already in scope. It is last on this list and not first, because a prompt to come back to a loop that returns you to your own starting point is a prompt to come back to nothing.

What to instrument, so the next run is measurement

There is no instrumentation in the product, so every retention figure above is derived. Six counters would replace most of this document with data.

  1. Story commits, and whether the parse returned an imprint. Two integers. It answers the largest finding here directly and it needs no identity: a count of commits and a count of commits that read nothing.
  2. Coherence at first reading and at every seventh day after it. The snapshot already exists and carries the stamp. Nothing reads it as a series except the graph.
  3. Releases run, and addresses that crossed to the far pole. The second is the number that says whether the loop works. It is 4 in the ceiling case and nothing on any surface reports it.
  4. Sessions, and the gap before each one. The interval is the whole question the owner asked and the product records no session boundary at all.
  5. Rituals saved against rituals marked done. Both fields exist. Neither is reported anywhere a person or a seat can see the ratio.
  6. The surface a session ended on. One string per session. It is the cheapest possible answer to where people stop.

Method, and how to run it again

The cohort is driven through engine.js: parseStory and applyStory for every story, compute for every reading, iqApply for the intake, meterPlan and meterRun for what a run costs, planAllowance for what is left, streakRead, ledgerRead and ladderRead for the record, and snapshot for the series.

The release itself lives in ui/release.js and is not in the engine contract, so it cannot be called from node. Its arithmetic is lifted into the harness character for character, and the harness refuses to run unless the lifted copy reproduces a release driven in Chromium on the same inputs to within a thousandth. That check passed on this run: 34.61 to 39.00 on Denial Of Light, Envy, Jealousy, 12 lines, 33 seconds, with every charge and every install agreeing.

Facts about the surface are read off source.html in a real Chromium at 1600 by 1000 and 390 by 844, and written to sim/measured.json. Both the harness and this page refuse to build without that file, so no number on this page is typed by hand.
