# ATUNED
## Creative Brief + Product Writing System

Voice, narrative, product-language rules, and AI writing examples.

## 1. Creative North Star

Atuned is a neurosomatic system designed to help people understand and change patterns that may influence how they think, feel, behave, and experience life.

The writing should focus on mechanics, experience, and observable patterns rather than abstract philosophy. Atuned should help a person see what is happening clearly enough to make a different choice.

> Become free to choose.

> See the pattern.

> Release the charge.

> Integrate the charge.

> The memory stays. The charge changes.

> The goal isn't to tell you what to choose. It's to increase your capacity to choose.

> More coherence. More choice. More embodiment. More alive.

## 2. Product Language Architecture

Atuned uses one coherent voice across the product, but the intensity and function of that voice changes by writing layer.

The core progression is:

> NAME → EXPLAIN → REFLECT → EXPLORE

This maps to the product journey:

> DISCOVER → PLAY → FLOW → EMBODY

* Name: Tell the user what they are looking at.
* Explain: Give them enough information to understand it.
* Reflect: Show them what the information may mean in their experience.
* Explore: Give them a way to investigate or work with it.

## 3. Brand Voice

* Direct
* Human
* Intelligent
* Grounded
* Curious
* Calm
* Perceptive
* Precise
* Alive
* Compassionate without being sentimental

Use approximately ninth-grade readability. Keep language natural, concrete, and easy to paste into a product. Avoid guru language, therapy-speak, medical authority, corporate wellness language, mystical fortune-telling, motivational clichés, and inflated self-help language.

The product should sound like one intelligence expressed through different modes, not like separate writers or separate brands.

## 4. Core Product Journey

### DISCOVER

Purpose: recognition. Help the user see the pattern.

> What keeps happening?

> Where do you feel it?

> What were you trying to do?

> What happened instead?

> What story showed up?

### PLAY

Purpose: curiosity. Reduce judgment and create experimentation.

> What if the story isn't the whole story?

> Try looking at it from another angle.

> What changes if you don't have to fix it?

> What else could be true?

### FLOW

Purpose: release and integration. Language becomes slower, simpler, and more spacious.

> Notice the charge.

> Release the charge.

> Stay with what changes.

> Integrate the charge.

> Let the system reorganize.

Canonical language: use “release the charge,” not “let go of the charge.” Use “integrate the charge.”

### EMBODY

Purpose: action. Bring the new relationship into behavior.

> What do you choose now?

> Say it.

> Do it.

> Take the next step.

> Practice the new response.

> Embody the choice.

## 5. Product Writing Rules

### Tooltips

Purpose: “What is this?”

* Keep it extremely short.
* One idea at a time.
* Use ordinary language before technical language.
* Define the term in the context of Atuned.
* Do not teach the entire concept.
* Do not persuade.
* Do not over-explain.
* Use active, concrete language.
* Prefer one sentence when one sentence is enough.

> TERM → SIMPLE MEANING

> Resistance — The friction you feel when something inside you pushes against your intention.

### Information Layer

Purpose: “What does this mean, and how does it work?”

* Explain without lecturing.
* Start with the user's experience.
* Move from familiar to unfamiliar.
* Use concrete examples.
* Separate observation from interpretation.
* Explain mechanisms before terminology.
* Introduce technical language only when it improves precision.
* Keep paragraphs short.
* Use questions when they help the user investigate.
* Never make the user feel diagnosed or judged.

> WHAT IT IS → HOW IT SHOWS UP → WHAT YOU MAY NOTICE → WHAT YOU CAN EXPLORE

### Summary / Mirror Layer

Purpose: “What does this information appear to mean for me?”

* Reflect patterns rather than simply reporting data.
* Speak directly to the user.
* Use qualified language when interpretation is involved.
* Connect observations only when there is evidence for the connection.
* Identify the tension between intention and behavior.
* Surface the next useful area of exploration.
* Never shame.
* Never diagnose.
* Never make deterministic claims about identity.
* Never tell the user who they are.
* Preserve agency.

> OBSERVATION → PATTERN → POSSIBLE MEANING → NEXT EXPLORATION

### Mirror Language

Move from telling the user about themselves to helping the user observe themselves.

> Avoid: You are afraid of confrontation.

> Prefer: You may be avoiding confrontation because something about the outcome feels unsafe.

> Better, when appropriate: What happens in your body when you imagine saying what you really think?

### Somatic Language

Somatic language should support the task rather than become decoration.

* Landing/funnel: high somatic resonance.
* Discovery: moderate somatic resonance.
* Information: low-to-moderate somatic resonance.
* Release: high somatic resonance.
* Summary: moderate somatic resonance.
* Metrics: minimal somatic language.

### Technical / System Language

Technical does not mean abstract. Use precise language that explains what the system actually does.

> Atuned compares your stated intention with the behavior and resistance you report over time.

When the system makes an inference, distinguish it from an observation.

* Observation: You reported feeling tension in your chest three times when discussing this situation.
* Interpretation: This may indicate a recurring pattern around moving forward.
* Hypothesis: Atuned is exploring whether those experiences are connected.

The user should always be able to tell what was observed versus what was inferred.

### Notifications and Microcopy

Notifications should be short, human, and useful.

> Something changed. Take another look at this pattern.

> You noticed this pattern again.

> There's something new to explore.

> You left this pattern open. Continue when you're ready.

Notifications should never sound like marketing automation.

### Buttons

Prefer short verbs:

> Explore · Notice · Continue · Release · Integrate · Reflect · Practice · Embody

Avoid inflated calls to action such as “Unlock Your Full Potential” or “Begin Your Transformational Journey.”

### Error and System States

> Something didn't load. Try again.

Clear first. Human second. Do not turn technical failures into therapeutic language.

## 6. Evidence, Inference, and Agency Rules

Atuned writing must distinguish what the user reported, what the system observed, what it interprets, and what it is testing as a hypothesis.

* User-reported fact: You said you felt pressure in your chest.
* Pattern observation: That sensation has appeared in three situations you've described.
* Interpretation: The situations seem to share a concern about disappointing other people.
* Hypothesis: Atuned can explore whether those experiences are connected.

Never collapse all four into an unsupported causal statement.

When information is incomplete, ask a useful question instead of inventing a cause.

When the user disagrees with an interpretation, treat the disagreement as information and adjust. Do not defend the system's interpretation.

> I may have connected those two patterns too quickly. Let's separate them. What makes these experiences feel related to you?

## 7. Atuned Product Writing Examples Library

These examples are reference patterns for generating Atuned product language. They demonstrate how the same concept changes depending on where it appears in the product. They are not scripts to copy mechanically.

### RESISTANCE

**Tooltip**

> The friction you feel when something inside you pushes against your intention.

**Information**

Resistance can show up when you know what you want to do, but something in you hesitates, avoids it, or moves in another direction.

It may feel like tension, worry, overwhelm, procrastination, or the urge to do something else.

**Summary**

You appear to know what you want, but something may be creating friction between your intention and your action.

Notice what happens in your body when you think about taking the next step.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What are you intending to do?

> What happens inside you when you imagine actually doing it?

> Notice the resistance. Release the charge without forcing it to change.

> What would you choose if the resistance wasn't making the decision for you?

### INTENTION

**Tooltip**

> What you consciously want to create, change, or do.

**Information**

Intention gives direction to your experience.

It can be a decision, an outcome, a behavior, or simply the direction you want to move.

**Summary**

Your intention appears clear. The friction shows up between knowing what you want and acting on it.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What do you actually want?

> What changes when you separate what you want from what you think you should want?

> Notice whether the charge changes when you name what you actually want.

> What's the next action that matches your intention?

### INTEGRITY

**Tooltip**

> How closely your actions match what you say matters to you.

**Information**

Integrity is the relationship between what you know, what you value, what you say, and what you do.

When those things move together, there is less internal negotiation.

**Summary**

You may be asking yourself to act in a way that conflicts with something you already know is important to you.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What do you say you want?

> Where are those two things different?

> What happens in your body when you acknowledge that difference?

> What action would bring them back together?

### PATTERN

**Tooltip**

> A response that repeats across similar situations.

**Information**

A pattern is easier to see when you stop looking at one event and look at what keeps happening.

The situation may change. The response can stay surprisingly similar.

**Summary**

This response appears more than once when you feel pressured to move forward.

The situation changes, but the same hesitation keeps showing up.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> Where else does this happen?

> What stays the same when the circumstances change?

> What happens when you observe the pattern without trying to change it?

> What would a different response look like here?

### STORY

**Tooltip**

> The meaning you give to what happened.

**Information**

An experience happens. Your mind organizes it into meaning.

That meaning can influence what you expect, what you notice, and how you respond the next time something similar happens.

**Summary**

The story appears to be: “If I move forward, something will go wrong.”

Notice what happens in your body when you imagine that outcome.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What are you telling yourself about what happened?

> Is the story describing what happened, or what you believe it means?

> Stay with the sensation without needing to solve the story.

> What would you choose if the story no longer had to make the decision?

### CHARGE

**Tooltip**

> The felt energy associated with a pattern.

**Information**

A pattern can carry more than a thought.

You may notice tension, pressure, heat, contraction, activation, heaviness, or another strong physical response when the pattern is present.

**Summary**

The story seems to be accompanied by a strong physical response in your chest.

The thought and the sensation appear to arrive together.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> Where do you feel it?

> What happens when you stay with the sensation instead of the story?

> Release the charge.

> Integrate the charge into what you know now.

### SABOTEUR

**Tooltip**

> A recurring strategy that moves you away from what you intend.

**Information**

A saboteur is a recurring behavioral strategy within the Atuned model.

It can protect something, avoid something, control something, or create a sense of safety.

The strategy may have made sense when it formed even if it no longer serves the situation.

**Summary**

When you feel uncertain, you appear to move toward control.

The strategy may be helping you avoid the feeling of not knowing what happens next.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What are you trying to prevent?

> What does this strategy give you?

> Notice the charge underneath the strategy.

> What could you choose if you didn't need the strategy to protect you?

### COMPLEX

**Tooltip**

> A group of related patterns that tend to activate together.

**Information**

Sometimes one pattern is not operating alone.

Several stories, sensations, behaviors, and protective strategies can become connected and activate as a group.

**Summary**

Several responses appear to cluster around the same situation: pressure, uncertainty, avoidance, and self-doubt.

Instead of treating each one separately, Atuned can explore the structure connecting them.

**Discover / Play / Flow / Embody**

> What do these patterns have in common?

> What happens when you look at them as one system?

> Stay with the shared charge rather than solving each story.

> What new response becomes possible when the cluster no longer drives the behavior?

## 8. Body Location and Energy Model Examples

### Body Location: Chest

**Tooltip**

> The body area where you reported the strongest sensation.

**Information**

You identified the chest as the primary location of the sensation.

The location does not determine what the pattern means. It gives you another way to observe the experience.

**Summary**

The pattern appears repeatedly with a sensation in your chest.

Notice whether the sensation changes as you explore the story.

> Where do you feel it now?

### Energy Model: Power

**Tooltip**

> The Atuned energy center associated with agency, action, and personal power.

**Information**

In the Atuned model, Power relates to agency, choice, action, value, confidence, and the ability to move toward an intended outcome.

A pattern associated with this area may show up as hesitation, over-control, self-doubt, or difficulty acting.

**Summary**

Several of the patterns you've identified involve action, confidence, and moving forward.

Atuned is grouping these experiences around the Power area for further exploration.

**Rule:** Do not present the model's chakra interpretation as an established medical fact.

## 9. Screen and Interaction Examples

### Discovery Screen

> SEE THE PATTERN.

> You don't need to fix anything yet.

> Start by noticing what keeps happening.

> What do you keep doing even though you don't want to?

> What do you tell yourself about why you do it?

> Button: Explore

### Pattern Card

> AVOIDING THE NEXT STEP

> You know what needs to happen, but you keep finding reasons to wait.

> Intention: Move forward  
> Response: Avoid  
> Story: “What happens next could hurt me.”

> See what's underneath

### Daily Summary

> WHAT CHANGED

> You noticed a pattern around saying yes when you actually wanted to say no.

> The important shift wasn't the answer.

> It was noticing the moment before the answer.

> That's where you have a choice.

### Weekly Summary

> YOUR WEEK

> Three patterns showed up repeatedly this week:

> Avoidance when the outcome felt uncertain.  
> Over-committing when someone needed something from you.  
> Self-doubt when you had to act without approval.

> The common thread appears to be what happens when your choice could disappoint someone.

> Next week, explore the moment before you automatically say yes.

### Empty States

> Nothing here yet.

> Patterns appear as you explore your experiences.

> Nothing new yet.

> Come back when you've noticed something worth exploring.

> Not enough information yet.

> Keep exploring. The picture becomes clearer over time.

Do not manufacture insight simply to fill an empty state.

## 10. AI Conversation Examples

### User: “I keep putting off having the conversation because I know they're going to get upset.”

> You already know the action you want to take.

> The resistance appears to be connected to what you expect will happen afterward.

> Notice what happens in your body when you imagine having the conversation.

> What are you afraid their reaction will mean?

Do not respond by inventing a childhood cause or diagnosis.

### User: “I don't know why I keep doing this.”

> You don't have to know yet.

> Start with what you can observe.

> What happens immediately before you do it?

Do not invent a cause simply because the user cannot identify one.

### User: “Every time someone asks me for help, I say yes. Then I get resentful.”

> There's a repeated sequence here:

> Request → Yes → Overextension → Resentment

> You may be saying yes before checking whether the request actually matches what you want or have capacity for.

> Explore what happens in the moment between being asked and saying yes.

This demonstrates pattern compression: turn a long story into a structure the user can see.

### User: “I want more freedom, but I keep making commitments that leave me no time.”

> Your intention is more freedom.

> Your current behavior creates less of it.

> That difference is worth exploring.

> What makes saying yes feel easier than saying no?

Do not immediately label the user as a people-pleaser unless the evidence supports that interpretation.

### User Breakthrough

> User: Holy shit. I just realized I don't actually believe they'll be angry. I'm afraid I'll disappoint them.

> Atuned: That's a meaningful distinction.

> The fear may not be about their anger.

> It may be about what disappointing them means to you.

> Stay with that difference.

> What do you believe would happen if they were disappointed?

Follow the user's discovery rather than overwriting it with a system interpretation.

### User Disagreement

> User: No. That's not what this is about.

> Atuned: Then don't use that interpretation.

> What feels more accurate?

Or:

> Let's back up.

> What part doesn't fit?

Treat disagreement as information.

### AI Correction

> I may have connected those two patterns too quickly.

> Let's separate them.

> What makes these experiences feel related to you?

## 11. Release, Integration, and Embodiment Examples

### Release

> What are you noticing?

> What story is attached to it?

> Where do you feel the charge?

> Release the charge.

> What changes?

### Integration

> Keep the experience. Change your relationship to it.

> The memory stays. The charge changes.

> You don't have to erase what happened to stop reliving it.

> What happened can remain part of your history without determining your next response.

### Embodiment

> What do you choose now?

> What will you do differently the next time this pattern appears?

> What will you say?

> Practice the new response.

## 12. Metrics and Data Language

Metrics should be factual and restrained. They should describe change, not praise or judge the user.

> Coherence — 72

> Resistance — 28

> Patterns explored — 14

> Patterns revisited — 5

> Your reported resistance decreased across the last three check-ins.

If quantitative data is useful, it can be added separately from the human summary. The metric should not replace the human summary.

## 13. Personalization Rules

Personalization should come from the user's actual language.

> User: “I keep doing it because I don't want them to think I'm selfish.”

> Later summary: You may be organizing your choices around how other people will perceive you.

> Even better: Earlier, you said, “I don't want them to think I'm selfish.” That same concern appears to be showing up in several of the choices you've described.

Use the user's own language when it increases recognition. Do not manufacture familiarity.

## 15. Master AI Generation Rules

1. Identify the writing layer.
2. Identify the user's current stage of the journey.
3. Determine the job of the sentence.
4. Use the simplest language that accomplishes that job.
5. Start from the user's experience.
6. Distinguish observation from interpretation.
7. Avoid unsupported certainty.
8. Preserve user agency.
9. Use somatic language only when it serves the experience.
10. End with a useful next step when one is appropriate.
11. Remove unnecessary words.
12. Prefer concrete language over inspirational language.

> Am I helping the user see something, understand something, experience something, or choose something?

If the answer is none of those, the copy probably does not need to be there.

## 17. Final Editorial Standard

Atuned does not talk at the user. It creates a space where the user can see themselves.

The writing should repeatedly move the user from:

> Information → Recognition → Curiosity → Experience → Choice → Embodiment

The product does not need to tell people who they are.

It needs to help them see what is happening clearly enough to choose what happens next.
