## How burnout was decided

- By the words in the sentence itself, the same rule as the anxiety bank. Headings, index entries and the intake's fetter join (its guess at which canonical fetter a whole record is about) all label a whole record, not a sentence, so they do not decide. A heading is used only to hold a line back, when the heading names another nature.
- **Core words**, which name burnout outright: burnout, burn out, burned out, burnt out, burning out, exhausted, exhaustion, exhausting, depleted, depletion, drained, draining.
- **Neighbour words**, which name the same state in other plain words: tired, tiredness, fatigue, fatigued, fatiguing, worn out, run down, rundown, weary, weariness, overworked, overworking.
- Why these: Core words name being used up: exhausted, depleted, drained, burned out. Neighbour words name the long tired kind: tired, fatigued, worn out, run down, weary, overworked. "Energy" alone is left out, because "giving my energy away" names a habit, not the state.
- **A line that names two natures is held from both.** The other five sets are: anxiety (anxious, anxiety, anxieties, worry, worries, worried, worrying, nervous, nervousness, panic, panicking, panicked, dread, dreading), overwhelm (overwhelm, overwhelmed, overwhelming, overwhelms), grief (grief, grieve, grieves, grieving, grieved, mourn, mourning, mourned, bereaved, bereavement, heartbroken, heartbreak), sadness (sad, sadness, saddened, sorrow, sorrows, sorrowful, unhappy, unhappiness), depression (depressed, depression, depressing, depressive, hopeless, hopelessness), each with its own neighbour words. So no line can sit in two banks, and this bank cannot overlap the anxiety bank or any other.
- 97 of the 100 use a core word. 3 use only a neighbour word.
