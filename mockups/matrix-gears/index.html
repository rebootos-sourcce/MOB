The matrix, redesigned, and the fetters and the sky as gears

Two jobs. Part A gives the Matrix the flow treatment the Field, the rail bars, the Flow band and the Shadow wave have been getting, in three directions. Part B answers "is there a way to visually show that" for the child fetters and the celestial layer, in three pictures that run. Every page is live: open it, press a person, hover, drag. All numbers come from the engine, dumped from source.html into data.js (13 reference people).

Part A. The Matrix

Today

The Matrix is the left rail section data-sec="matrix": 171 square buttons, 19 domains across and 9 child fetters down (ui/personas.js, mx and syncMx). A2. Polar open live

Frames at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 s. Rings enter inside out, 70 ms apart.

At a glance. Where on the wheel each fetter lives. A cell is the arc the Field already uses: domain c is exactly the sector of 2 pi over 19 starting at the top. The beads are the 108 real addresses at their real slot angles.

Treatment borrowed. The Field's concentric geometry, the Shadow wave as a radial ripple on every arc, a light sweeping once in 14 s, the root colour registers as the outer band.

Reads. W (slot, cf, sq), the cell table, DOMAIN, S.doms, AFFIN, CQ and DQ.

Reduced motion. Same rule: settled, one draw, nothing running.

Honest note. One address in the data has no child fetter (cf is null) and is not drawn. Addresses per fetter are uneven (Anger 27, Surprise 3 on Marcus), which is itself the reading.

Part B. The fetters and the sky as gears

What the engine actually has, and what that allows the pictures to say

The celestial layer is small. engine/astro.js and birth.js compute four points: the Sun, the Moon, the Ascendant (needs a time and a place) and the design Sun, as longitudes, plus the I Ching gate and line of the two Suns (64 gates of 5.625 degrees). There are no other planets in the engine, so there are no Venus or Mars gears to draw.

There is no angle between the sky and a fetter. A fetter has no longitude. The only real link in the product is a lookup: the Sun's element reads as a root (ELEM2ROOT: fire Engine, earth Architect, air Witness, water Weaver), and a root is 1.3 times more susceptible at its affine fetters (AFFIN). converge() compares the birth root with the root the person runs and prints agree or differ. So all three pictures draw that link as the claim, and draw aspects (real angles) only among the sky points. Where the person runs a different root from the one their Sun reads, the thread is dashed and the page says so.

Moon and Rising. The product maps only the Sun's element. These pictures apply the same table to the Moon, the Rising and the design Sun so the sky has four threads. That is a mockup extension and needs a ruling before it is built.

The causal question. A meshing gear says "this turns that". The product says supports, not causes. So none of the pictures lets rotation do the work: the rotation is a slow clock and a hand-turn, and the lit threads, which come from the data, are the whole claim. Each page says so in its small print. If the owner wants the gear metaphor kept, the line to hold is: the gears show a shared register, the threads show the lookup.

B1. Meshing gears open live

Frames at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 s. The gears start from rest with a 4 degree back swing, then spin up over 350 ms.

At a glance. The sky and the fetters sit on one register. The sky gear has 12 teeth (signs are the gaps), the root gear 4, the fetter gear 9. The 12 to 4 mesh is exact and tested: four signs turn the root gear once, and the zodiac repeats its element every fourth sign, so the same root tooth is always in a fire sign. (Tested over 60,000 phases; the root teeth run Engine, Weaver, Witness, Architect because the pinion turns against the sky.) The 4 to 9 mesh is only a picture; the thin lines from each root tooth to its affine fetters are the claim.

Teeth counts and ratios from the data. 12 signs, 4 roots (ELEM2ROOT), 9 child fetters. Inner rings: 64 gate ticks (gates of the Sun and design Sun lit gold) and 108 address ticks at their real slot angles, coloured by fetter, lit by SQ. Each fetter tooth carries a bar as long as its charge.

Interaction. Drag the sky: the others follow with inertia that decays over about 0.9 s.

Frame cost. 1.1 ms a frame desk, 1.2 phone. Canvas paths for 25 teeth, 108 ticks, 9 threads.

Reduced motion. No turning at all: gears sit at the person's Sun at the mesh. Drag still works.

B2. Nested rings open live

Frames at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 s. Rings open from the outside in, 90 ms apart, with one overshoot.

At a glance. Which sky points lean on which fetters. From outside in: the sky (12 signs, 64 gates, Sun, Moon, Rising, design Sun at their longitudes); the roots riding the fetters as one rigid piece, so a root arc always sits directly over its own fetters; the 19 domains; the 108 addresses; CQ in the centre.

Rates from counts. 2.4 degrees a second times 12 over the ring's count, alternating direction: sky 2.40, fetters minus 3.20, domains 1.52, addresses minus 0.27. A rule from the data, written on the page.

Couplings. A thread from each sky point to its root, solid when the person runs that root, dashed when not. The thread hugs the gap between rings so it never cuts across the fetters. Chosen domains tie to their root with a dotted line. Chords inside the sky ring are real aspects (none for Marcus).

The owner's "when they align". A soft ring lights on a root while a sky point sits over it. That is geometry, not data, so it is kept faint. The owner decides whether to keep it.

Interaction. Drag the outer ring; each inner ring follows on a spring (friction, as animation).

Frame cost. 1.0 ms desk, 0.7 phone, the lightest.

Reduced motion. No turning, no alignment glow; all rings at rest. Drag works.

B3. Layered tower open live

Frames at 0, 0.5, 1 and 2 s. Frame 0 shows the empty tower standing; the discs fill from the top down.

At a glance. What sits above what. Five discs, top to bottom: sky, roots, fetters (a pillar each as tall as its charge), domains (a pillar each as tall as the blueprint's reach), and the 108 addresses (a pillar each as tall as SQ). Light drops from each sky point to its root, from the root to its fetters, and from each charged fetter to the addresses that carry it (W[i].cf).

Honest read. Strata, not a mechanism. This is the picture with no causal flavour at all, and the one that best matches "supports, not causes".

Frame cost. 1.3 ms desk, 1.3 phone. Ellipses and lines in 2D canvas, no 3D engine. The fan to the addresses is about 100 hairlines.

Reduced motion. Settled, no rotation.

Cost, in plain words

All six are canvas 2D on the main thread, none are compositor only. Measured in real time in headless Chromium with software drawing: the page's own drawing code takes between 0.6 and 4.4 ms a frame (4 to 26 percent of a 16.7 ms frame), worst single frame 6 to 11 ms except one 48 ms outlier in Loom, and the browser held 60 frames a second throughout (17 ms between frames). Loom is the heaviest at about 4 ms, Nested rings the lightest at 1.0. A real GPU raster will be cheaper than this software one. If a surface needs to be cheaper still, the sweep and bead layers can be split to a second canvas and the static rings cached; not needed at these figures.
