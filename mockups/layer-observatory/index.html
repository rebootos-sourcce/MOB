The layer observatory

The nested rings and the ridgeline in one live instrument, with the Field's own grammar pulled in. Nine fetter wave bands run inside the sky, the roots, the domains and a ring of address tiles. The core pops by level: fetters, saboteurs, complexes or hyper complexes. A four column chain joins the levels with Bezier threads that carry the Field's pulses. A summary reads off whatever is on screen. Open it live, or at a phone width open a followed thread on Tomas. Every control works. URL switches: ?p=Tomas&view=sab|cx|hy&mode=thread|scrub&plan=free|one|two&still=1&lite=1.

3. Three ways to interact, behind one switch

He does not yet know what he wants to interact with, so all three are in the page. They share one selection, one summary and one clock. View by (fetters, saboteurs, complexes, hyper complexes) works in all three.

Lens open live

Complexes, then hyper complexes. The core lamps, the counts, the headline and the emphasis of the columns all re-read through the level. The other columns fall to 66 percent.

What it does. Pick a level and the whole picture re-reads through it: which lamps pop in the core, which column is lit, what the summary leads with. A node pressed switches to its level.

Good for. Asking one question of the whole person: what are my saboteurs, what are my complexes.

Follow a thread open live

Tomas, the heavy example: a saboteur followed, then a fetter followed. Everything not joined dims to 13 percent. The addresses under the thread stay lit on the tile ring and the bands.

What it does. Press any node, in the chain or in the core, or press one of the three chains: everything not joined to it dims, its threads run, the tiles and sparks of its own addresses stay lit, and the summary reads the chain in words.

Good for. Tracing one saboteur down to the addresses and up to its hyper complex.

Scrub open live

Tomas at entries 4, 7 and 10 of 12. Layers light as the entries come in and the summary names what was lit and what left.

What it does. A scrubber and a play button replay how the layers lit over twelve entries. Rows hold their place; layers that are not lit yet are ghosts.

This history is a mock. The charge is scaled up by a fixed schedule and the engine's own compute() runs at every step, so every chain is real arithmetic over made up input. No entry text exists. The page says so on the scrubber and in the summary.

8. Reduced motion

The end state, drawn once, with no loop: measured, zero frames drawn while idle. There are no beats, no pulses, no beads, no spin of the sky, no easing and no entrance. What carries the same reading standing still: a lamp or a row that is carrying shows steady at a brightness from its heaviest address (0.18 plus half its gamma), the tiles are lit by SQ, the waves sit at one phase, and the links draw at their weights. Scrub still works, by hand, and steps instantly; Play is removed rather than slowed. Somebody who asked for stillness gets a finished picture, not a slower one.

Frame cost, in plain words

Everything is 2D canvas on the main thread, so none of it is compositor only. The page redraws the whole stage every frame: about 700 line points for the nine bands, one tile per body address, up to 20 lamps with spokes, 80 rows and up to 60 threads with a dashed pulse pass on each. The cheapest version is built in: ?lite=1. It drops the wash and the halos, draws the bands without the glow pass at a 5 px step, runs at 30 frames a second and at one pixel per pixel. On the desktop run the median script time goes from 4.0 to 3.0 ms, a cut of about 25 percent, and on Tomas, the heaviest, from 4.5 to 3.3 ms, a cut of about 27 percent. The 35 to 60 ms gap between frames is the headless browser rastering in software under load, not script. It keeps every datum, the beats, the links and the summary, and loses the glow. Reduced motion is the cheapest of all: it draws once and then nothing, measured at zero frames while idle. Two cheaper moves are not taken yet and are open: caching the static rings (roots, domains, guides) as one bitmap after the entrance, and drawing the chain on its own canvas only when a beat changes a link.

10. The pop rule, and the summary rules

The pop rule, as written on the page. Every address that holds charge beats on its own clock: 7.2 s minus 0.62 s per point of SQ, never under 1.4 s. It flares for 12 percent of the beat, with a fast rise and a slow fall. Its phase is its slot times 0.618 of a turn, so beats never line up. Brightness is its SQ through the ridgeline's gamma. A node shows the beats of the addresses it is made of: a fetter its own addresses, a saboteur its parts, a complex and a hyper complex the parts beneath. Same person and same time always draw the same picture. The consequence: a fetter with many addresses is steady, a fetter with few flickers, a fetter with none is dark. Steady against flickering is itself the reading.

The summary. Four counts that are also the lens; one sentence through the picked level; then three lines, what is active, what is heaviest at this level, what connects to what; then the three strongest chains. Every sentence is built from the data on screen, and the page does not claim a cause: threads stand on, join and sit in. The strength of a chain is the mean weight along it, out of 10, and a chain stops where the next level is locked or does not exist yet. The clinical names that sit beside the six families in the engine are never shown, only the codex's own plain lines. The weight of a saboteur is the mean charge of the addresses it is built from.
