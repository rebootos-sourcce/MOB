Login: three directions

All three: username and passphrase, no email field on the sign in, sign up lets the person choose the username, the button reads Guest, Developer options is small in the lower right, and the ground is the Field’s own grammar in 2D. Every motion is a state, and every motion stops under reduced motion with the final state drawn. My pick is A, because the owner already ruled the login belongs on the boot screen and A is that screen. That is a recommendation, not a decision.

A. The ring Open live and type into it

The boot’s own ring, with the form inside it. This is the owner’s round MH ruling kept: the login sits on the Atuned animation screen and the first paint is the animation itself.

At a glance
A dial with one name on it. Two fields, one big button, a small Guest. The ring is waiting for you.
Costs the person
About 10 seconds (Judgement): one name, one passphrase, one press. No email anywhere on the sign in.
Costs to build
Cheapest to wire into what exists: the 112 ticks and the arcs are the Field’s own, and the boot ring becomes this ground, so the 5.4 second boot overture stops being a separate screen. New work is two SVG paths for the connect line.
What each motion means
Username typed: a node appears at the top (signal). Passphrase typed: a path forms down both sides (connect). Log in: the ring closes and every tick reads full (confirm). Mismatch: the path stops and splits into two dashed branches with one alarm marker (contradiction). Alarm red is used on that marker and that sentence only.

B. The bands Open live and type into it

Seven waves, one per seat, with the form on a plate. Typing moves the two waves that mean something here.

At a glance
A calm instrument. A large sentence, seven coloured lines, a plate. It reads as a place before it reads as a form.
Costs the person
About 10 seconds (Judgement). Same fields as A. The waves give the most feedback of the three, which is a cost to attention: they move while you type.
Costs to build
Waves are seven redrawn paths, about 60 lines of script. Cheap, but it runs a frame loop while the page is open, so it needs the reduced motion path (drawn, built in) and a pause when the tab is hidden (not drawn).
What each motion means
Username lifts the Throat wave (the name is said aloud). Passphrase lifts the Root wave (ground). Log in flattens all seven into parallel lines (settle). Mismatch puts the Root wave out of phase and ends it on an alarm marker. The seat pairing is PROPOSED.

C. The address grid Open live and type into it

112 nodes, 16 to a seat. The form sits on one row above them. Typed characters light nodes in two rows.

At a glance
A sentence, one row to fill in, and a field of small rings. The most like a lab instrument and the least like a login page.
Costs the person
About 10 seconds (Judgement). Two fields and a button share one row at 1600, which is the shortest path of the three. At 390 it stacks.
Costs to build
Lowest of the three: 112 small circles and two lit rows. It loses the ring, so it shares nothing with the boot and the 5.4 second overture would stay a separate screen.
What each motion means
Each character in the username lights a node in the Throat row. Each in the passphrase lights a node in the Root row, so the nodes work as the password dots do. Log in lights every node. Mismatch puts one alarm ring on the last Root node. Seat pairing PROPOSED.

Checks, and what they found

  • Voice gate (check.py --line) on every sentence of rendered text across all 43 pages, 103 lines of three words or more: 101 clean on the first run. The two flagged were mine and are rewritten: “How do you actually operate?” failed the plain words list on “operate”. The same gate fails two of the TDD’s own lines: “Welcome to a somatic experience.” fails as a preamble, and “Most people operate somewhere in the middle.” fails on “operate”. Run on the source files, check.py reports two figure label failures in flow.js; both are the signal chips (a phrase and a colour in one array) and are false positives of the gate’s row shape, not copy.
  • Contrast, against each element’s own ground, Dark. Text: ink 16.6, mid 9.0, dim 6.1 (5.8 on the Login B plate), accent 9.0, button label on accent 8.6, alarm text 6.9. Seat rings against the ground 4.8 (Root) to 6.6 (Third eye). Two defects found and fixed: control borders were white at 16 percent, 1.56 to 1, and are now 38 percent, 3.54 to 1; the quiet starting points were at half opacity and their labels measured 3.0, and they are now at 75 percent, 5.4. The unlit field ticks are decoration and are left below 3 on purpose. Today’s login input border is about 1.5 to 1.
  • Tap targets, 403 controls across all 43 pages at both widths, measured from the rendered boxes: 0 under 44 by 44 after fixing the sign in switch (40) and the Type and Speak switch (43).
  • No em dashes in any source file, and sentence case throughout.
  • Round PB frames (the laws list, the six worked questions and the starting points note) went through the voice gate line by line, all clean. They were not put through the contrast and tap target sweeps above, which were run on the 43 pages of round OT. Run those before relying on them.
  • Not done: Snow, Punch and Glass lightings; a keyboard walk; a screen reader pass; the real engine behind any frame. These are drawings.

Open, and whose call

  • Account order. These mockups put the account door first, as the owner asked for the login, and keep Guest as a first class entry. The TDD puts the account before the first release and the earlier storyboards put it after. The review recommends after (question 1). Frame O14 shows the after version for a Guest.
  • Username needs a server change. The server keys an account on the email today (ui/login.js notes it). Username sign in with no email needs a username field there, and a way back in when a passphrase is lost, which an email would have done. Nothing here draws recovery.
  • The mapping from a starting point to a seat is PROPOSED. There is no such table. Pain lights Root here, on the same footing as the others.
  • Twelve starting points. The main version is twelve. Whether it is too many is read off three measures, and none of them can be counted inside the product while the privacy policy says nothing about use is reported.
  • Eleven laws wait. Non-harm, Courage, Responsibility, Detachment, Generosity, Aesthetic beauty, Justice, Presence, Equanimity, Awareness and Nature are not in his ten. When they are asked, and whether they ever are, is not ruled.
  • Release sentence. The TDD sentence is on the screen as a placeholder; the ruled one is “letting go” with six verbs.
