# Summary page: the pitch to the ICPs and the focus group

**Everything in this file is simulated.** No person was shown anything. The
reactions are written from each reference profile's stated risk and need in
`ui/personas.js`, `ATUNED-art-ux-icp-review.md` section 17 (the focus group) and
`BUYERS.md` (the buyer grid), and from what the page measured for them. They are a
way to look for failures with a named person in the chair. They are not evidence of
how a real person would answer and must not be quoted as research.

Where each of the six sits on the grid, read off the engine, not typed:

| Profile | Age, role | CQ | Band | Grid level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sofia | 41, somatic practitioner | 73 | Compounding | 8, Aligned |
| Diane | 46, founder, second company | 59 | Even | 6, Receptive |
| Marcus | 44, creative director | 62 | Gaining | 7, Tuned |
| Angela | 36, seeker, six kinds of healing | 64 | Gaining | 7, Tuned |
| Derek | 39, high performer | 49 | Oscillating | 5, Searching |
| James | 57, C suite | 44 | Oscillating | 5, Searching |

`BUYERS.md` says levels 4 and 5 are the largest population and the hardest sell,
"wants magic, not mechanics." Two of the six reference people are at 5, so the page
was walked as Derek and as James and not only as Sofia.

Pictures used. Sofia is every `pass*` image. The tissue test rendered James and
Angela at both widths, `icp-james-loaded-1600-full.png` and
`icp-angela-loaded-1600.png`, and Derek carrying the owner's own name,
`pass3-owner-1600-full.png`. Diane and Marcus were read from the Sofia pictures and
their numbers, and were not rendered.

## What was pitched

Pass 2, then pass 3. Pass 3 is the page after the owner's round OJ message: the
story left, the first drivers right, the four questions below, the way out after.

## The reactions

### Sofia, 41, level 8. Simulated.

- **Stays.** "The line at the top does what I would say to a client: where it goes."
  The note that a light overlap is one many people share earns her. She would say
  it to a client.
- **Objection.** The Named stage. "To pierce" under a first name is a claim, and the
  only source is a hover that says the founder said it. She would translate it to a
  client only with a reference.
- **Closes the tab if** a name meaning arrives with no source she can read.
- **Changed.** "No meaning on file" is said plainly and nothing is guessed.
  The source is in the tooltip and in `data-src`, not printed (open question 4).

### Diane, 46, level 6. Simulated.

- **Stays.** The reading, because it names a cost: "the cost is elsewhere."
- **Objection.** Where is what I do about it. At 1600 the output row is at 1899 pixels.
  The name panel is "interesting, not Tuesday."
- **Closes the tab if** she reaches the bottom and there is nothing she can do
  tonight.
- **Changed.** On a phone the output row now follows the story, the drivers and the
  marks (4386 down to 2314). Desktop is not changed and is raised as open question 3.

### Marcus, 44, level 7. Simulated.

- **Stays.** The numbered line with its rings, the strip of birth marks, the
  zodiac glyphs. "That looks drawn."
- **Objection.** Three things. Pass 2 had holes inside cards, 150 to 200 pixels. The
  three folds at the foot "look like leftovers." And the right rail beside the page
  prints the nine again as a table, so there are two summaries on one screen.
- **Closes the tab if** he sees the same data twice with two different layouts.
- **Changed.** Holes reduced, row level 0.77 to 0.84. The rail duplicate is the
  shell's and is open question 5.

### Angela, 36, level 7. Simulated.

- **Stays.** "No root meaning on file for Angela, Mercedes or Ruiz." It does not
  pretend to know her. The light overlap note reads as honest.
- **Objection.** Two words. "What it costs" reads as an accusation and "What drives
  it" reads as a claim that a name decides her. She is afraid of anything too sure
  about her, and the page is sure in its headings and careful in its sentences.
- **Closes the tab if** her name were given a meaning that sounded like a verdict.
- **Changed.** Nothing, because both headings are his. Raised as open question 2,
  with the options drawn.

### Derek, 39, level 5. Simulated.

- **Stays.** The 3 by 3 nine and the rings. It reads as a dashboard.
- **Objection.** "Show me the next move first." He is a level 5, and the grid says
  he wants magic and not mechanics, and he is also the one who goes straight to the
  number. The way out being third in the page loses him.
- **Closes the tab if** the first screen is only sentences about origins.
- **Changed.** The story and the first drivers fill the first screen, which is the
  magic half, and the rings are one scroll down. On a phone the action is lifted.

### James, 57, level 5. Simulated.

- **Stays.** "A light overlap is one many people share. A strong one is rare."
  That is the first sentence on the page that admits a limit.
- **Objection.** The four small circles in pass 2 are unreadable and a phone cannot
  hover. And the Named stage, because he will look the names up, and
  `TASKS.md` round DM already did: the references give other roots for all three
  of the owner's examples.
- **Closes the tab if** he finds a stated meaning that a reference contradicts and
  the page has not said whose it is.
- **Changed.** The circles are words. The sentence that explains how a meeting was
  made is on the ring of each row. Open question 4 is for him.

## What the group agrees on

Five of six stay on the story and the person's own sentences. Two of six (Sofia,
James) condition the name meaning on a visible source. Two (Diane, Derek) want the
way out higher. One (Marcus) wants the duplicate in the right rail gone.

## The tissue test, by band

The same walk was run with a level 5, a level 7 and a level 8 and checked for a flow
that works for one and fails for another: James (5), Angela (7), Sofia (8), and Derek
(5) as the owner's name. The page renders for all of them with no overflow and no
control under 44 by 44 at either width. What differs is what they stop at, above.
The layout does not fork by band. One thing does, and it is in the data and not the
layout: none of the four has a strong overlap, James has one clear and the rest only
light ones, and the page says so once under the stage and not on every row.

## Re-pitch of the final page

All six, with pass 3 in hand and simulated again, would stay for the first screen.
The two open complaints are the same two: the way out is lower than Diane and Derek
want it, and the Named stage is only as trusted as its source.
