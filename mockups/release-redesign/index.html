How the writing keeps time with the voice

One function: charsAt(line, t) returns how many characters are written at time t. Only where t comes from changes.

  • His clip, with its timing track. t = audio.currentTime, read every animation frame. Nothing is counted by a timer, so it cannot drift, Pause freezes it, and a stall in playback stalls the text.
  • His clip, no timing track. The same, spread across audio.duration by the weight of each word, with the pauses the recorder found if it has any.
  • The app voice, as the fall back. speechSynthesis boundary events give charIndex at each word on local voices in Chrome desktop, Safari and Firefox. Network voices and most Android voices send none. So: snap to charIndex when events arrive, and between them advance at the measured rate (RUN.spokeMs / RUN.spokeW, already kept); if no boundary arrives within 700 ms of onstart, run on the schedule alone.
  • Voice off. The same schedule at the reading rate, 0.40 seconds a word (REL_WORD_S). The opening is not a different screen when it is silent.

Every character is in the document from the first frame and only its opacity moves, so nothing reflows while it writes, textContent is always the whole sentence for a reader or a test, and reduced motion simply shows it all. Cost: the opening runs one animation frame loop for about twenty seconds, and changes at most a few classes per frame.

Making the timing track, and the recorder

The easy way, built: record.html is one self-contained page for him. It shows the three lines, records each with a big Record button, plays each back with the words writing out so he can see whether it is right, lets him edit the words, and saves two files: atuned-opening.wav and atuned-opening-timing.json. Steps written for a ten year old are on the page: the Allow microphone box, where it appears, and which two files to send back.

How it finds the words: it records raw samples (not MediaRecorder, which hides the audio inside a codec), measures loudness every 10 milliseconds, finds the pauses between sentences, and spreads the words between them by length. Tested here against a synthetic take with five sentences: every sentence start and end found within 30 milliseconds. It says so on the page when the pauses it found do not match the sentences, and asks for a slower take.

The exact way, if ever wanted: a forced aligner run once over the clip and the script (whisper with word timestamps, or aeneas) writes the same file shape, word by word, to the millisecond. The player does not care which made it.

The run: three directions, every one with no scroll

All three share the top, the pinned sentence, the prompt, the bar and the grammar above. They differ in where the four counters live.

A. The ring recommended

The address is the centrepiece: its ring, its own glyph, its percent falling live as each line is said. The left side is the left flank of the ring and the right side is its right flank, so the picture is the body. Release and install sit under it as a pair. The same address at the head of a block, on each side of each half, and as the second address.

B. The edges

Left and right are the screen's own edges: a smooth tube down the left that breathes, a ten segment ladder down the right that steps. The four counters sit in one row at the foot. Smallest footprint, calmest, and the most peripheral: the sentence has the whole middle.

C. The stage

The address hero on top, the prompt in the middle, four labelled counter cards in two rows: sides on top, halves beneath. The most legible and the heaviest: it spends the most height and is the most like a dashboard.

States that must survive: heavy, paused, the sound sheet, a later run

Marking a line heavy is a tap on the sentence itself (the whole sentence is the target; a red ring at its end is the affordance, and fills with a double ring when it is on). Pause dims the stage and turns into Resume. The Voice control is a split: the left half switches the voice, the right half opens the sound sheet that holds Seat tone, Vibration and the voice's name and where it runs. A later run uses the engine's own six channel sentence.

Five people, from different rungs: the same screen

Gordon is the heaviest and Sofia the lightest in the cast; Angela and Derek sit between. The ring's percent reads 12 to 95 across them and the layout does not move.

Done and the cooldown, in the same language

The cooldown is the opening again: his clip, the four lines writing out as they are said, and the two minutes as a clay ring that drains, with its time inside it. Under it the four counters at their final values, with a tick on each finished side. Done is the one filled button. When the two minutes run out, or on request, the summary: one sentence, the two counters, up to three heavy lines, the addresses as rings to mark heavy, and Details, which swaps in the expression note and the legend. A run ended early with End says what it actually said.

Honest notes

What a person reads at a glance

Four seconds in: a green heart ring at 52 percent that is shrinking (this is what I am working on, and it is going down), one sentence in the middle (this is what I say), and four small counters, two of which are moving. What they do next is obvious: say it. Their fear, that they are doing it wrong or that it will never end, is answered by the two numbers that fall.

The count: seven things to look at (address, pinned sentence, prompt, four counters) and five things to press (the sentence, voice, sound, pause, end), against about twenty two visible and 408 in the document today. The words on the stage besides the prompt: four counter labels and the address name. Words before the first number: the address name and its seat, three. Taps to the thing they came for: none.

Tissue test, walked with four people. Derek reads the Release number first and trusts it. Gordon reads the sentence and the ring, and the subject on the sentence is what moves him. Angela (level 4 or 5, ring at 15 percent) will not decode two glyphs on first sight, so the words Left and Right are always printed under them; she does not need the symbols to use it, and the first run is where she learns them. Sofia will want the pole words, and they are in the legend under Details and in the aria labels, not on the run, as ruled. Where they would stop: the install side reads "Joy." a hundred times in a row, which is a copy gap and not a layout one (question 4).

First-screen height at 360 by 640, measured

Every direction fits with no scroll. The tallest statement in the cast, with its subject, is five lines at 360 and sits in a zone that holds six. Worst case checked by check.js for four people, three directions, three viewports, every block.

What it costs per frame

Measured in headless Chromium with the software renderer, ten seconds each, so read the shape and not the figure. The mock recomputes the run state on every frame, which the build does not: it updates once a statement, and the rows marked "one update a statement" are the build's cost. In the build the run has no frame loop at all: the left drain, the ring arc and the segments are CSS transitions set once per statement, the left breath and the right pulse are CSS keyframes on opacity, and the numbers change four times a second at most. The one frame loop is the opening, about twenty seconds.

Length, which the screen cannot fix

At the default of 100 patterns a channel and four seconds between statements, three addresses is 82 minutes (relSecFrom: 4949 seconds, Gordon). The screen is built to be left alone for that long: nothing flashes, nothing animates faster than a breath, the only thing that moves each four seconds is one sentence, and every counter is a glance. But a person who must stay for 82 minutes has a different problem, which is the dose, and it is his call (question 6).

Reduced motion shows every character at once, removes the breath, the pulse and the drift, and keeps the numbers and the fills.
