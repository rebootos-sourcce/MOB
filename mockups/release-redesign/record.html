Record the opening

You read three short lines aloud. This page records them, shows you the words writing themselves out in time with your voice, and saves two files to send back. Nothing leaves this page: it has no network, no account and no upload.

Before you start

  1. Use Chrome or Edge, on a computer. Sit somewhere quiet, a few inches from the microphone, with the door closed. Headphones are fine and keep the app's own sound out of the take.
  2. Press the big red Record button on the first line below. A small box appears at the top left of the browser window, just under the address bar. It asks to use your microphone. Click Allow. (It may say Allow this time or Allow while visiting the site. Any of the three is right.) You only do this once.
  3. Read the line aloud at your own pace, with a short pause between sentences. When you finish, press Stop. Press Play to hear it and watch the words write out. Not happy? Press Record again. You can do it as many times as you like.
  4. Do the same for the next two lines. You can change any wording first: press Edit the words, change them, and the timing follows.
  5. Press Download both files at the bottom. Your browser saves two files into your Downloads folder (Chrome shows a small arrow at the top right of the window: click it, then click Show in folder). Send both files back: atuned-opening.wav and atuned-opening-timing.json.

Save

Record all three lines to unlock the download.

If something does not work

No box asked for the microphone. Click the small padlock or settings icon at the left end of the address bar, find Microphone, and set it to Allow. Then reload this page.

The page says it cannot find a microphone. Plug in a headset or check the computer's sound settings, then reload.

The words write out too early or too late. Record the line again a little slower, with a clear short pause at each full stop. The page finds the pauses and spreads the words between them.

Safari. Safari does not allow the microphone on a page opened from a file. Use Chrome or Edge, or ask for this page as a web link.

For the team: the recording is captured as raw audio (not with MediaRecorder), so the page can read the loudness of every 10 milliseconds, find the pauses, and place each word. The timing file lists, for each line, the start and end of every word in seconds from the start of the saved clip. The build step turns the clip into a small compressed file and embeds it.
